# Langerhans Cell Histiocytosis

What is a langerhans cell
- Specialised dendritic cell found predominantly in the skin
- Derived from bone marrow monocytes
- Present antigen to naive T cells. 

Langerhans cell histiocytosis is a neoplastic proliferation 
- Characteristic Birbeck (tennis racket) granules seen on electron microscopy
- Cells are CD1a+ and S100+ by immunohistochemistry

![[Pasted image 20240912162522.png]]

Features  

- bone pain, typically in the skull or proximal femur
- cutaneous nodules
- pituitary involvement: leads to [[Diabetes Insipidus]] due to pituitary stalk involvement
- pulmonary involvement: More common in adults, presenting with dyspnoea, cough, and chest pain
- recurrent otitis media/mastoiditis
- tennis racket-shaped Birbeck granules on electromicroscopy

If a person's name is in the title it is malignant. Malignant have skin involvement. If 2 names then 2 years old, if 3 names then 3 years old. 

Subtypes
- Letterer Siwe disease
	- Malignant
	- Classic presentation is skin rash and cystic skeletal defects in an infant 
	- Multiple organs may be involved 
	- Rapidly fatal
- Eosinophilic granuloma
	- Benign
	- Classic presentation is pathologic fracture in an adolescent; no skin involvement
	- Biopsy shows Langerhans cells with mixed inflammatory cells including eosinophils
- Hand Schuller Christian disease
	- Malignant
	- Classic presentation is scalp rash, lytic skull defects, [[Diabetes Insipidus]] and exopthalmos in a child greater than the age of 3
