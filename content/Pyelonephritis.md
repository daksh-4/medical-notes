# Pyelonephritis

A. Infection of the kidney

1. Usually due to ascending infection; increased risk with vesicoureteral reflux

2. Presents with fever, flank pain, WBC casts, and leukocytosis in addition to symptoms of cystitis
    
3. Most common pathogens are
    
    1. E coli (90%)
        
    2. Klebsiella species
        
    3. Enterococcus faecalis

### Chronic Pyelonephritis

1. Interstitial fibrosis and atrophy of tubules due to multiple bouts of acute pyelonephritis
    
2. Due to vesicoureteral reflux (children) or obstruction (e.g., BPH or cervical carcinoma)
    
3. Leads to cortical scarring with blunted calyces; scarring at upper and lower poles is characteristic of vesicoureteral reflux.
    
4. Atrophic tubules containing eosinophilic proteinaceous material resemble thyroid follicles ('thyroidization' of the kidney, Fig. 12.17); waxy casts may be seen in urine.
