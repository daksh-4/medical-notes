# Amyloidosis

- Amyloid is a misfolded protein that deposits in the extracellular space. Multiple proteins can deposit as amyloid
- Usually have a B pleated sheet configuration
- Congo red staining and apple-green birefringence under polarised light

Systemic Amyloidosis

- Primary Amyloidosis
    - When there is systemic deposition of AL amyloid derived from Ig light chain, associated with plasma cell dyscrasias (abnormalities of the plasma cell, leading to overproduction of the light chain).

- Secondary Amyloidosis
    - Systemic deposition of AA amyloid derived from SAA. SAA is an acute phase reactant that is increased in chronic inflammatory states, malignancy and Familial Mediterranean fever
        - Familial Mediterranean Fever - dysfunction of neutrophils presents with episodes of fever and acute serial inflammation, high SAA during attacks

- Clinical findings of amyloidosis
    - [[Nephrotic Syndrome]] - kidney is the most common organ involved
	    - Kidney is the most commonly involved organ in systemic amyloidosis. Amyloid deposits in the mesangium, resulting in nephrotic syndrome. Characterised by apple-green birefringence under polarised light after staining with Congo red. 
    - [[Restrictive Cardiomyopathy]] or arrhythmia
    - Tongue enlargement, malabsorption and hepatosplenomegaly
    - Diagnose using tissue biopsy in abdominal fat pad and rectum
    - Damaged organs must be transplanted as amyloid can’t be removed
    - Perioorbital bruising - Raccoon sign

Localised Amyloidosis

- Senile cardiac amyloidosis
    - Non-mutated serum transthyretin deposits in the heart
    - Usually asymptomatic and present in over 25 % of people over 80.
- Familial amyloid cardiomyopathy
    - Mutated serum transthyretin deposits in the heart leading to [[Restrictive Cardiomyopathy]]
    - 5% of African Americans carry the mutated gene.
- Type 2 diabetes
    - Amylin deposits in the islets of pancreas which is derived from the excess insulin that the pancreas produces in the early stages of the disease
- Alzheimer’s Disease
    - AB amyloid deposits in the brain derived from beta-amyloid precursor protein on chromosome 21 associated with Down’s Syndrome
- Dialysis associated amyloidosis
    - Beta2-microglobulin deposits in joints
    - Beta2-microglobulin provides structural support for MHC class 1. Patients on dialysis, the beta2-microglobulin is not filtered well and builds up and deposits.
- Medullary carcinoma of the thyroid
    - Calcitonin deposits within the tumour
    - The tumour is derived from C cells which are neuroendocrine cells in the thyroid that make calcitonin, leading to localised amyloidosis
    - Tumour cells in an amyloid background on fine needle aspiration biopsy

Diagnosis
- Bone marrow trephine and fat aspirate - diagnostic in 90%
- Renal biopsy - diagnostic in 95%
- Congo red stain - amyloid is pink
	- Under polarised light - there is apple-green bifringence.
