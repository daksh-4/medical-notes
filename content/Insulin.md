# Insulin

![[Pasted image 20250321104445.png]]

Insulin forms hexamers in the body of six insulin molecules that are linked together

Hexamer insulins have a slower onset of action vs monomer insulins

Rapid acting
- Lispro aspart and glulisine
- Modified human insulin with different amino acids resulting in reduced hexamer formation
- Onset 15 mins
- Peak 1 hour
- Duration 2-4
- Often used pre-meal

Regular insulin
- Synthetic analog of human insulin
- Recombinant DNA techniques
- Onset 30 minutes
- Peak 2-3 hours
- Duration 6 hours
- Commonly used in hospitalised patients
	- Sliding scale insulin
- Only type of insulin that is given IV
	- Used in DKA and HHS and hyperkalaemia

NPH insulin
- Regular insulin combined with neutral protamine
- Slows absorption 
	- Peak around 4-8 hours
	- Duration 16 hours

Glargine
- Insulin with modified amino acid structure
- -Soluble in acidic solution for dosing
- ﻿﻿Precipitates at body pH after SQ injection
- ﻿﻿Insulin molecules slowly dissolve from crystals
- ﻿﻿Low, continuous level of insulin
	- ﻿﻿Onset: 1-1.5 hours
	- ﻿﻿Duration: 11-24 hours
- ﻿﻿Often given once daily

Detemir
- ﻿﻿Insulin with fatty acid side chain added
- ﻿﻿Slow rate of absorption
	- ﻿﻿Aggregation in subcutaneous tissue
	- ﻿﻿Also binds reversibly to albumin
- ﻿﻿Onset: 1-2 hours
- ﻿﻿Duration: > 12 hours
- ﻿﻿Usually given once or twice daily
- ﻿﻿May cause less weight gain
