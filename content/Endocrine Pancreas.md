# Endocrine Pancreas

Beta cells - insulin

Alpha cells - glucagon

Delta cells - somatostatin

Alpha/delta cells - outer islet

Insulin
- Synthesised as preproinsulin made by ribosomes of rough endoplasmic reticulum
- Preproinsulin is cleaved to proinsulin and transported to the golgi apparatus
- Packaged into secretory granules - proinsulin cleaved to insulin and C peptide in granules

- Glucose is brought into beta cells via the GLUT 2 transporter
- Glucose → G-6P via glucokinase
- ﻿﻿ATP produced → Closure of K+ channels
- ﻿﻿Depolarization
- ﻿﻿Voltage-gated calcium channels open
- ﻿﻿Calcium → insulin release from vesicles

Amino acids also trigger the release of insulin

Production of insulin is inhibited by epinephrine
- Beta 2 receptors increase insulin
- Alpha 2 receptors decrease insulin release
- Alpha effect is dominant effect in pancreas
- Fight or flight response results in a raised plasma glucose response

Glucokinase
- Found in beta cells
- Catalyses the first step of glycolysis which converts glucose into glucose 6 phosphate 
- Found in the liver and pancreas
- Induced by insulin
- Insulin promotes transcription 
- High Km - rate varies with glucose
- High Vm can convert lots of glucose if there is a steady supply

GLUT 2 transporter
- Bidirectional
- Found in liver kidney and beta cells
- Also in intestines and other tissues

Insulin receptor
- Alpha is extracellular
- Beta is transmembrane 
- Connected with disulfide bridges

PIK3 pathway
- Intracellular lipid kinases
- Forms PIP3 from PIP2
- Catalyses many intracellular processes
	- Glycogen formation
	- Fatty acid synthesis
	- GLUT 4 transporter

GLUT 4 transporter
- Major mechanism for increased glucose uptake in muscle and fat

RAS/MAP Kinase pathway
- Insulin activate G protein called RAS
- RAS can activate Raf, MEK, MAP
- Modify cell growth and gene expression

Insulin Independent Organs

- ﻿﻿Brain and RBCs
	- ﻿﻿Use GLUT-1 for glucose uptake
	- ﻿﻿Not dependent on insulin
	- ﻿﻿Takes up glucose when available
	- ﻿﻿RBCs: No mitochondria (depend on glycolysis)
	- ﻿﻿Brain: No fatty acid metabolism (glucose/ketones)
- ﻿﻿Liver, kidney, intestines
	- ﻿﻿Also insulin independent (GLUT-2)
- ﻿﻿Other organs: nerves, lens

Insulin Effects

- ﻿﻿Glucose uptake (skeletal muscle, adipose tissue)
- ﻿﻿Glycogen synthesis
	- ﻿﻿Activates glycogen synthase
	- ﻿﻿Inhibits glycogen phosphorylase
- ﻿﻿Inhibits gluconeogenesis
	- ﻿﻿increased Fructose-2,6-bisphosphate levels
	- ﻿﻿Inhibit Fructose 1,6 bisphosphatase 1
- Fatty acid synthesis
	- Activates acetyl CoA carboxylase
	- Inhibits hormone sensitive lipase
- Protein synthesis
	- Stimulates entry of amino acids into cells
	- Important for muscle growth
- Key side effect of insulin therapy - weight gain
- Sodium retention 
- Lowers potassium 
	- Enhanced activity of Na K ATPase pump in skeletal muscle 
- Inhibits glucagon release

Hormone sensitive lipase
- Removes fatty acids from triacylglycerol in adipocytes
- Inhibited by insulin
- Activated by glucagon and epinephrine

Glucagon
- Protein hormone
- Single polypeptide chain
- Main stimulus release is low plasma glucose
- Increases liver (not muscle) glycogen breakdown
	- Raises blood glucose level
- Increases gluconeogenesis
- Increases amino acid uptake in the liver (not muscles like insulin)
	- More carbon skeletons for glucose via gluconeogenesis
- Activates hormone sensitive lipase

Glucagon receptor
- Gprotein camp PKA pathway
- Found mostly in the liver
- Not found in skeletal muscle

Beta blocker overdose
- Activates adenylyl cyclase
- Different site from the beta adrenergic agents
- Raises cAMP increasing myocyte calcium
- Same mechanism as beta stimulation via Gs proteins

Insulinoma
- Rare pancreatic islet cell tumour
- Occurs in 50 year olds
- Key features fasting hypoglycaemia 
	- Insulin levels remain elevated when fasting
- Neuroglycopenic symptoms
	- Confusions and odd behaviour
- Sympathetic activation from low glucose
	- Palpitations, sweating, tremor

Diagnosis
- Fasting level 
- Elevated C peptide and proinsulin
- Consider exogenous insulin use

Glucagonoma
- Excess glucagon secretion
- Leads to glucose intolerance - presents like diabetes
	- Elevated fasting glucose levels
	- Rare to develop DKA because insulin function is still intact
- Weight loss
	- Due to liver gluconeogenesis
- Necrolytic migratory erythema
	- Red blistering rash that is itchy and painful that fluctuates and occurs in genitals, buttocks and groin

Diagnosis
- Increased levels of plasma glucagon

Treatment
- Somatostatin analogs
- Inhibits glucagon secretion and improves symptoms but does not treat the tumour
