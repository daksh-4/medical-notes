# Diabetes Insipidus

### Cranial DI

1. ADH deficiency
    
2. Due to hypothalamic or posterior pituitary pathology (e.g., tumor, trauma, infection or inflammation)
    
3. Clinical features are based on loss of free water.
    
    1. Polyuria and polydipsia with risk of life-threatening dehydration
        
    2. Hypernatremia and high serum osmolality - swelling of the nerves and cranial swelling which can cause neurological symptoms 
        
    3. Low urine osmolality and specific gravity
        
4. Water deprivation test fails to increase urine osmolality (useful for diagnosis).
    
5. Treatment is desmopressin (ADH analog).

### Nephrogenic DI

1. Impaired renal response to ADH
    
2. Due to inherited mutations or drugs (e.g., [[Lithium]] and demeclocycline)

C. Clinical features are similar to central diabetes insipidus, but there is no response to desmopressin .
