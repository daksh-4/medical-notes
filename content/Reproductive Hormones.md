# Reproductive Hormones

Steroid hormones

Poorly soluble in plasma
- Carried by sex hormone binding globulins and albumin

Bind to intracellular receptors

SHBG
- Glycoproteins made in the liever
- Binds androgens much more than oestrogens

Oestrogen amplification
- Free hormones are the ones that cause clinical effects not bound ones
- Increased SHBG leads to decreased free androgens and oestrogens
	- Because androgens bind more strongly, ratio of free oestrogens to androgens increases
- This leads to amplification of oestrogenic effects

[[Cirrhosis]]
- Oestrogen can induce SHBG production
- Oestrogen effects
	- Gynecomastia, spider naevi, palmar erythema, testicular atrophy, impotence
- Altered metabolism/excretion leads to inreases oestrogen levels
- Increased SHBG leads to oestrogen effects

### Male Reproductive Hormones

LH stimulates Leydig cells to make testosterone. FSH stimulates Sertoli cells which controls spermatogenesis

![[Pasted image 20250401151255.png|500]]

DHT
- Test converted to DHT in peripheral tissues via 5 alpha reductase
- Many testosterone effects mediated by DHT
- DHT has higher potency than test
	- Binds androgen receptor more strongly 
	- Is more stable
- Finasteride
	- Inhibits 5 alpha reductase 
	- Used for treatment of prostatic hyperplasia and hair loss 

Estradiol
- Testosterone also converted to estradiol 
- Occurs in adipose tissue and Leydig cells via aromatase enzyme
- Some testosterone effects are mediated by estradiol

Testosterone effect in the fetus
- Development of testes requires functioning Y chromosome
	- SRY gene produces testis determining factor
	- All males XY born with testes
- Internal/external genitalia requires hormones
- Internal genitalia
	- Derived from mesonephric ducts
	- Seminal vesicles, epididymis, vas deferens
	- Requires testosterone
- External genitalia
	- Derived from urogenital sinus
	- Penis, scrotum, prostate, bladder
	- Requires DHT

5 alpha reductase deficiency
- Autosomal recessive disorder of sexual development
- 46 XY male able to make test but not DHT 
- Normal internal genitalia
- External genitalia predominantly female
- Typical case
	- Male with ambiguous genitalia
	- Female child with masculinisation at puberty
	- Blind vagina
	- Absence of uterus
	- Bilateral undescended testes
	- Normal testosterone levels

Test effects in children

Growth spurt in puberty via estrogens
- Testosterone converted to estrogens
- Increased linear growth 
- Closure of epiphyseal plates

Acne
- Associated with increased sebum
- Androgen receptors on sebaceous glands
- Androgens stimulate growth/secretions
- Also common in other forms of androgen excess
	- [[Polycystic Ovarian Syndrome]]
	- [[Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia]]

Effects in Adults

Prostate growth

May effect lipids
- Exogenous test -> down HDL/ up LDL

Male pattern balding 

Male hypogonadism
- May occur with aging
	- Decreased serum test 
	- Increased SHBG
	- Decreased free test
- May be associated with 
	- Decreased sexual function
	- Decreased bone mass
	- Decreased anaemia (exogenous test can cause elevated haematocrit)

Testosterone therapy

Adverse effects
- Raised haematocrit
- Acne
- Balding
- Worsening of BPH

Spermatogenesis
- Suppressed by exogenous testosterone
- Test suppresses LH secretion
- Decreased test from Leydig cells
- ==Exogenous hormone has weak activity in the testes==
	- Decreased spermatogenesis

GnRH
- Peptide produced by the hypothalamus
- Released in pulses 
	- Frequency and amplitude of pulses varies
	- Changes effect release of LH and FSH of pituitary
- Induces FSH/LH production via a Gq protein system with IP3 secondary messenger

Leuprolide
- GnRH agonist
- Derived from GnRH
- D amino acid substitution for native L amino acid
- Resistant to degradation
	- Increased half life so it occupies the Gq receptors for prolonged period of time
- Initial binding can stimulate LH/FSH release
- Chronic treatment down regulates the GnRH receptor leading to pituitary desensitisation
- Supressing ovarian follicular growth and ovulation 
- Results in low levels of oestradiol and progesterone, which is similar to menopause
- Pulsatile dose (rarely done)
	- Stimulates LH/FSH 
	- Administered by infusion pump
	- Used to create LH surge for ovulation
- Continuous
	- Suppression of LH/FSH
	- Endometriosis
	- Uterine fibroids
	- Prostate cancer 
	- Precocious puberty

Pituitary Reproductive Hormones
- LH, FSH, TSH and HCG are heterodimers
	- Two chains alpha and beta 
	- They share a same alpha chain with a different beta chain 
- All have a cAMP second messenger system

Anabolic steroids

Adverse effects
- Lowers HDL and raises LDL
- Erythrocytosis
- SMall testes
- Azoospermia
- Gynecomastia
	- Some test is converted to oestrogen
	- ALtered production of sex hormone binding globulin
	- Between doses of anabolic steroids, the testes make oestrogen

Spironolactone
- Blocks androgen receptor
- Decreased androgen production from andronestenedione
- Rise in oestrogen effects and fall in testosterone effect
- In women can be used as a treatment for acne, hirsutism and alopecia in women
	- Causes amenorrhea because it stimulates progesterone receptors

Sertoli cells
- Support and nourish developing spermatozoa 
- Regulate spermatogenesis
- Stimulated by FSH
- Supported by Leydig cell testosterone (paracrine)
- Need FSH and LH for spermatogenesis
- Form a blood-testis barrier through tight junctions

- Secrete inhibin B to inhibit FSH 
- Secrete androgen binding protein
	- Raises local test levels
	- Intra-testicular test concentration is 100x the peripheral concentration
- Produce anti-mullerian hormone
	- Results in degeneration of the mullerian ducts

Anti-mullerian hormone
- In utero there are two systems
	- Indifferent gonad which could develop into ovaries or tests
	- Paramesonephric (Mullerian) duct - female structure
	- Mesonephric (Wolffian) duct - male structure
- Y chromosome -> testes -> Sertoli cells
- Secretion of anti-mullerian hormone
	- Mullerian inhibitory hormone/substance
- Degeneration of mullerian system
- Leaves gonad and mesonephric ducts
