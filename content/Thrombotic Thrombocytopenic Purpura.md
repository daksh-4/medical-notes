# Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura

TTP 

Patients get microthrombi due to a decrease in the protein ADAMSTS13. 
- Normally when you make vWf, it is made as a multimer which are degraded over time and chopped into monomers. 
	- This is done by ADAMSTS13. 
	- Large multimers of vWf can result in platelet adhesion 
- This results in the formation of microthrombi which shear RBCs and cause microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia 

- Decreased ADAMSTS13 is usually due to acquired autoantibody
- Classically seen in adult females. 

Clinical features
- Skin and mucosal bleeding
- Microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia 
- Fever
- Renal insuffiency (predominant in HUS due to the uraemia)
- CNS abnormalities (predominant in TTP)

Lab findings
- Thrombocytopenia with increased bleeding time
- Normal PT/PTT (this is because coagulation cascade is not being activated it's only adhesion)
- Anaemia with schistocytes
- Increased megakaryocytes on bone marrow biopsy. 

Treatment
- Plasmapharesis (particularly in TTP because you can remove the autoantibody against ADAMSTS13) and corticosteroids (reduces production of autoantibodies)
