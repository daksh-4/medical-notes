# Cortisol

- Cortisol poorly soluble in plasma, most is bound to cortisol binding globulin. Increases when levels of oestrogen go up (just like thyroid binding globulin)
- Cortisol maintains blood pressure 
	- Increases vascular sensitivity alpha 1 to adrenaline/norad
	- Decreases NO mediated vasodilation
- Suppresses the immune system
	- Sequester lymphocytes in the spleen/nodes
	- Blocks neutrophil migration (leading to a benign neutrophilia)
	- Blocks histamine release by mast cells
	- Decreased eosinophils
	- Inactivate NF-KB
		- Key inflammatory transciption factor that mediates the response to TNF alpha
		- Controls synthesis of COX-2, PLA2 and lipoxygenase
- More glucose produced in the liver
	- Increased synthesis of glucose 6 phosphatase
	- Increased gluconeogenesis
	- Less glucose taken up peripherally
	- More glycogen synthase in the liver to improve glycogen storage
- Lipolysis in adipocytes
	- Increased FFA, cholesterol and triglycerides
	- Can also stimulate adipocyte growth leading to fat deposition
- Enhanced effects of glucagon, epinephrine
	- Leads to insulin resistance in long term steroid use
- Muscle atrophy
- Skin effects
	- Decreased collagen and inhibition of fibroblasts - thin skin, easy bruising
- Bones - inhibits osteoblasts so osteopenia and osteoporosis
