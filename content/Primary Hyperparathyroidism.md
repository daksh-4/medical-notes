# Primary Hyperparathyroidism

1. Excess PTH due to a disorder of the parathyroid gland itself
    
2. Most common cause is parathyroid adenoma (>80% of cases); sporadic parathyroid hyperplasia and parathyroid carcinoma are less common causes.
    
3. Parathyroid adenoma is a benign neoplasm, usually involving one gland.

l. Most often results in asymptomatic hypercalcemia; however, may present with consequences of increased PTH and hypercalcemia such as

1. Nephrolithiasis (calcium oxalate stones)
    
2. Nephrocalcinosis-metastatic calcification of renal tubules (Fig. 15.9), potentially leading to renal insufficiency and polyuria
    
3. CNS disturbances (e.g., depression and seizures)
    
4. Constipation, [[Peptic Ulcer Disease]], and [[Acute Pancreatitis]]
    
5. Osteitis fibrosa cystica-resorption of bone leading to fibrosis and cystic spaces (Fig. 15.10)

6. Phosphaturia

2. Laboratory findings include up serum PTH, up serum calcium, down serum phosphate, up urinary cAMP, and up serum alkaline phosphatase.
- up Urinary cyclic AMP - PTH causes G protein coupled receptor pathway to convert ATP to cAMP. 
- up alk phos - osteoblast is turned on and lays on bone and also creates an alkaline environment. Osteoclasts are also activated but ALP is a sign of osteoblastic activity

3. Treatment involves surgical removal of the affected gland.
- Post op hypocalcaemia
	- Remaining parathyroid gets suppressed
	- Numbness or tingling in fingertips, toes, hands
	- Twitching or cramping of muscles
