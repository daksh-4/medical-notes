Rule of 4s

![[Pasted image 20241106194519.png|500]]

4 nerves above pons - 1,2 (these are not in the brain stem) 3,4
- 3 - eye turned out and down
- 4 - eye unable to look down when looking towards nose

4 in the pons
- 5 - ipsilateral facial sensory loss
- 6 - ipsilateral eye abduction weakness
- 7 - ipsilateral facial weakness/droop
- 8 - ipsilateral deafness

4 in the medulla
- 9 - ipsilateral pharyngeal sensory loss
- 10 - ipsilateral palatal weakness
- 11 - ipsilateral shoulder weakness
- 12 - ipsilateral weakness of tongue

Midline
- Motor pathway (corticospinal) - contralateral weakness
- Medial lemniscus - contralateral proprioception/vibration
- MLF - Ipsilateral internuclear ophthalmoplegia
- Motor nucleus and nerve - ipsilateral CN motor loss 3,4,6,12

Side
- Spinocerebellar pathway - ipsilateral ataxia
- Spinothalamic - contra pain/temp sensory loss
- Sensory nucleus of CN5 - ipsilateral pain/temp loss in face
- Sympathetic pathway - ipsi Horner's

Rule of 4s Caveats

Trigeminal Nerve (V)
- ﻿﻿Lesion: loss of ipsilateral pain/temp face
- ﻿﻿Rule of 4 Pons Nuclei and side (lateral tract)
- ﻿﻿Don't use to localize to Pons because it is a big cranial nerve
- ﻿﻿Use for lateral tract localization
Vestibulocochlear (VIII)
- ﻿﻿Don't use vestibular signs to localize to pons because the vestibulocochlear nuclei are large
- ﻿﻿Vestibular signs can be medulla/pons
- ﻿﻿Lesion: hearing loss
