# DNA Polymerases

All DNA polymerases synthesize new DNA only in the 5'→3' direction, and all of them require a free 3'-OH to extend from — none can start a strand de novo. This is why priming (see [[DNA Replication]]) is always needed before DNA polymerase can act, unlike RNA polymerase (see [[Different types of RNA Polymerase]]), which can initiate without a primer.

## Prokaryotic DNA polymerases

| Enzyme | Role |
|---|---|
| DNA Pol III | The main replicative enzyme — does the bulk of actual chromosomal DNA synthesis on both leading and lagging strands. High processivity (stays on the template for long stretches) via the sliding clamp. Has 3'→5' exonuclease proofreading activity. |
| DNA Pol I | Removes the RNA primers laid down by primase (5'→3' exonuclease activity) and fills the resulting gaps with DNA. Also has 3'→5' proofreading activity. Low processivity — suited to short primer-sized gaps rather than bulk replication. |
| DNA Pol II | Mainly involved in DNA repair (e.g. bypassing/repairing damaged DNA), not primary replication. |

Primase itself is not a DNA polymerase — it's an RNA polymerase-like enzyme that lays down short RNA primers, giving DNA polymerase the free 3'-OH it needs to start extending.

## Eukaryotic DNA polymerases

| Enzyme | Role |
|---|---|
| DNA Pol α | Has primase activity built in — initiates each new strand/Okazaki fragment by laying an RNA primer and extending it a short way with DNA before handing off. |
| DNA Pol δ | Takes over from Pol α on the lagging strand; high processivity via the sliding clamp PCNA. Also has proofreading activity. |
| DNA Pol ε | The main leading-strand polymerase; also high processivity via PCNA, with proofreading activity. |
| DNA Pol γ | Replicates mitochondrial DNA specifically (the only one that acts outside the nucleus). |
