# Renal Tubular Acidosis

All three types of renal tubular acidosis (RTA) are associated with hyperchloraemic metabolic acidosis (normal anion gap). Often have low bicarbonate or abnormal K+

Bicarb is reabsorbed in the PCT

H+ is secreted into the collecting duct. This is caused by aldosterone

Urine Anion Gap

- ﻿﻿Used for diagnosis of metabolic acidosis
- ﻿﻿Evaluation of renal acid excretion

- ﻿﻿In acidosis, excess NH, excreted (removes H+)
- ﻿﻿NH, not measured directly
- ﻿﻿Surrogate: urinary anion gap
- ﻿﻿NH, leaves with Cl  
    Negative UAG when acid (H+) being excreted
- ﻿﻿UAG should be negative in acidosis  

    UAG = Na + K - Cl 

- ﻿﻿In GI metabolic acidosis (diarrhea):
	- ﻿﻿UAG becomes negative
	- ﻿﻿Excretion of NH, with Cl increases
	- ﻿﻿Urine Cl concentration goes up
- ﻿﻿Also negative in proximal (type II) RTA
	- ﻿﻿Intact distal H+ secretion

- ﻿﻿In distal RTA and type IV RTA UAG is positive
- ﻿﻿Kidneys can't excrete H+
- ﻿﻿NH4 and Cl don't increase
- ﻿﻿UAG (Na + K - Cl) does not become negative

Ammonium Chloride Challenge

- ﻿﻿Used for diagnosis of metabolic acidosis
- ﻿﻿"Challenge" patient with NH Cl

- ﻿﻿Gives an acid load
- ﻿﻿Should lower urine pH
- ﻿﻿In distal RTA, urine pH remains >5.3
  
Type 1 RTA (distal)  

- inability to generate acid urine (secrete H+) in distal tubule
	- urine pH is high despite the metabolic acidosis
- Very low bicarb
- causes hypokalaemia
- complications include nephrocalcinosis and renal stones
	- Alkaline urine precipitates stones
	- Acidosis causes increased calcium release from bones
	- Acidosis suppresses calcium resorption in the kidneys 
	- Low serum calcium can also lead to rickets and growth failure in children
- causes include idiopathic, rheumatoid arthritis, SLE, Sjogren's, amphotericin B toxicity, analgesic nephropathy

Type 2 RTA (proximal)  

- decreased HCO3- reabsorption in proximal tubule
- causes hypokalaemia
- often asymptomatic
- decreased urine pH
	- Distal tubule excretes H+ as acidosis becomes established
	- Negative urine anion gap
- complications include osteomalacia
- causes include idiopathic, as part of Fanconi syndrome, Wilson's disease, cystinosis, outdated tetracyclines, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (acetazolamide, topiramate)
	- Fanconi syndrome is a general failure of the proximal tubule 
		- urine loss of phosphate, glucose, amino acids, urate, protein
	- [[Multiple Myeloma]]
		- Some of the light chains are toxic to the proximal tubule
	- Acetazolamide
		- Carbonic anhydrase inhibitor 

Type 3 RTA (mixed)  

- extremely rare
- caused by carbonic anhydrase II deficiency
- results in hypokalaemia

Type 4 RTA (hyperkalaemic)  

- reduction in aldosterone leads in turn to a reduction in proximal tubular ammonium excretion
- causes hyperkalaemia
- causes include hypoaldosteronism, diabetes
