# MEN Syndromes

Autosomal dominant - germline mutations

MEN 1, 2A and 2B

### MEN1

- [[Pituitary Adenoma]]
- Parathyroid adenoma
- Pancreatic tumours

Germline mutation of MEN1 gene (11q13)
- Codes for the protein menin which is a tumour suppressor
- 2 hit hypothesis, 2nd hit occurs in the endocrine glands

Parathyroid adenoma
- First presentation
- Presents as hyperparathyroidism
- May cause recurrent kidney stones

[[Pituitary Adenoma]]
- 70% of patients
- Most commonly a prolactinoma, 2nd most is Gh secreting adenoma
- NOT SEEN IN OTHER MEN SYNDROMES

Pancreatic-duodenal neuroendocrine tumours
- Most commonly a gastrinoma
- Zollinger-Ellison 
- Rarely insulinomas, glucagnomas, VIPomas

### MEN 2A and 2B

Germline mutation in RET proto oncogene
- Codes for a receptor tyrosine kinase
- Gain of function 

Both involve medulllary tumours
- Medullary thyroid carcinoma 
	- Parafollicular cells (C cells)
	- Calcitonin lowers serum calcium
	- Cancer develops in 30s as opposed to 60s in normal
	- 100% risk of developing one
- Pheochromocytoma (tumour of the adrenal medulla)
	- Usually occurs after thyroid

MEN2A
- Medullary tumours plus parathyroid hyperplasia
- No physical findings

MEN2B
- Medullary plus M's
- Mucosal neuromas
- Marfanoid appearance 
	- Tall with long wing span
	- Skeletal deformities of the spine
	- No lens or aortic involvement 
- No parathyroid involvement
