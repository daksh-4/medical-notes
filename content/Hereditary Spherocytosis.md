# Hereditary Spherocytosis

RBCs have a cytoskeleton that is tethered to the membrane to help maintain the structure. The molecules that tether the cytoskeleton to the membrane are messed up, forming blebs of membrane. This results in loss of membrane (by the spleen). This results in spherical RBCs. 

Inherited defect of RBC cytoskeleton membrane tethering proteins. 
Most commonly involves spectrin, ankyrin or band 3.1 

There is loss of central pallor due to loss of the biconcave shape. There is range in the size of the cells so the oldest cells have lost the most membrane. High RBW (red blood cell distribution)

![[Pasted image 20240910152552.png|500]]

Spherocytes are less able to maneuver through splenic sinusoids so the spleen will consume them. Otherwise there is no problem with spherocytes

Spherocytes without loss of central pallor can be observed in **liver disease**, particularly advanced liver failure or cirrhosis. In this condition, there may be mild spherocytosis due to altered red blood cell membrane stability caused by metabolic disturbances. Unlike hereditary spherocytosis or autoimmune hemolytic anemia, the central pallor of red blood cells is retained. 

Clinical and lab features 
- Spherocytes with loss of central pallor 
- + RDW and + MCHC (mean corpuscular haemoglobin concentration also seen in autoimmune anaemia)
- Splenomegaly (hypertrophy of macrophages), jaundice with unconjugated bilirubin and increased risk for bilirubin [[Gallstones]]. 
- Increased risk for aplastic crisis with parvovirus B19 infection of erythroid precursors

Diagnosis
- Osomotic fragility test
- Increased fragility in hypotonic solution. This is because the cell has no extra membrane and can burst easily. 
- EMA binding test

Treatment
- Splenectomy
- Anaemia resolves 
- Spherocytes persist (but this is ok) as there is still reticuloendothelial activity in the liver and lymph nodes. 
- Howell-Jolly bodies emerge on blood smear post splenectomy. These are red blood cells with fragments of nuclear material in it (it is normally the job of the spleen to get rid of these) See below for image
