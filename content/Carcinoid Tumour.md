# Carcinoid Tumour

Malignant proliferation of neuroendocrine cells; low-grade malignancy

Tumour cells contain neurosecretory granules that are positive for chromogranin. Can arise anywhere along the gut; small bowel is the most common site. Every site has a specific cancer but there is none for small bowel specifically so you give the small bowel the carcinoid tumour. 

Grows as a submucosal polyp-like nodule (Fig. 10.20) C. Often secretes serotonin
![[Pasted image 20240930220009.png|500]]

Serotonin is released into the portal circulation and metabolised by liver monoamine oxidase (MAO) into 5-HIAA.

5-HIAA is excreted in the urine.

Metastasis of carcinoid tumour to the liver or other extraintestinal places allows serotonin to bypass liver metabolism. This is the only case where you get the serotonin like syndromes flushing etc.  

Serotonin is released into the hepatic vein and leaks into systemic circulation via hepato-systemic shunts, resulting in carcinoid syndrome and carcinoid heart disease.

Carcinoid syndrome is characterised by bronchospasm, diarrhoea, and flushing of skin; symptoms can be triggered by alcohol or emotional stress, which stimulate serotonin release from the tumour.

Carcinoid heart disease is characterised by right-sided valvular fibrosis (increased collagen) leading to tricuspid regurgitation and pulmonary valve stenosis; left-sided valvular lesions are not seen due to presence of monoamine oxidase (metabolises serotonin) in the lung which converts the serotonin into 5 HIAA before it reaches the left side of the heart.
