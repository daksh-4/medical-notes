# Autoimmune Disorders

- In the bone marrow immature B cells are tested for binding to self antigens. If binding to self antigen is present, self editing can occur: RAG genes are re-expressed to edit the light chain of the immunoglobulin so it no longer binds self antigen. Or it undergoes apoptosis.
    
- If antigen binding is present without a costimulatory signal, then the lymphocyte will undergo anergy and shutdown via CD95 (FAS) and FAS ligand.
    
- Regulatory T cells also regulate autoimmunity
    
    - A regulatory T cell express a molecule called CTLA4 which can compete and bind B7, preventing binding with CD28 and stopping the second signal leading to anergy.
    - Treg can also secrete cytokines like IL10 (limiting the production of costimulatory molecules) and TGF beta (inhibits activation of macrophages).
    - Regulatory T cells are CD4+, CD25+ (IL2 receptors), FOXP3+. Regulatory T cells are dependent on IL2 for growth and survival. FOXP3+ is a transcription factor that develops and maintains the Treg.
        - CD25 polymorphisms are related to autoimmunity such as MS and type 1 DM.
        - FOXP3 mutations lead to IPEX syndrome (immune polyendocrinopathy enteropathy X linked). Autoimmune dysfunction leading to destruction of endocrine glands and diarrhoea from autoimmune enteropathy. It is a X linked syndrome. Normally also have eczema of the skin.
- Autoimmune disorders classically affect women of childbearing age. One theory is that oestrogen reduces the apoptosis of self reactive B cells in the bone marrow or periphery.
    
- Environmental trigger in genetically-susceptible individuals
    
    - Evidence of genetic component - increased incidence in twins, HLA subtype associations (which code for the MHC) and PTPN22 polymorphisms (it is a tyrosine phosphatase and mutations result in a decrease in signalling that indicates self antigen binding).
    - Examples of environmental triggers - infections (revs up the immune system, triggering self reactive cells as well as normal lymphocytes), antigen on microbe mimics self antigen (molecular mimicry)
