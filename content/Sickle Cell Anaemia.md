# Sickle Cell Anaemia

Autosomal recessive mutation in the beta chain of haemoglobin

Normal glutamic acid (hydrophilic) is replaced with valine (hydrophobic)

This gene is carried by 10% of individuals of African descent likely due to protective effects against falciparum malaria

You need 2 abnormal beta genes for sickle cell to happen. Results in >90% of sickled haemoglobin in RBCs. 

HbS POLYMERISES (this is reversible) when deoxygenated - resulting in aggregation of needle-like structures resulting in sickle cells. 

Increased risk of sickling with
- Hypoxaemia 
- Dehydration
- Acidosis

HbF protects against sickling
- High HbF at birth is protective for the first few months of life. 
- Treatment with hydroxyurea increases levels of HbF

Cells continuously sickle and de-sickle while passing through the microcirculation. This results in complications related to RBC membrane damage. 

Because there's membrane damage there is extravascular haemolysis. This is because RBCs can't move quickly through the spleen so they get broken down. There is also intravascular haemolysis (minimal amount) due to damage to the cell membrane. This is enough to decrease the haptoglobin and show target cells on the blood smear. This is because the cells dehydrate as the membrane is damaged, reducing the cytoplasm volume in the cell. 

This results in massive erythroid hyperplasia
- Haematopoiesis in the skull and facial bones
- Hepatosplenomegaly
- Risk of aplastic crisis with parvovirus B19

### Vaso-occlusive crises

You can also get irreversible sickling leading to complications of vaso-occlusion. This can result in infarction of tissue. 

Dactylitis
- Swollen hands and feet due to vaso-occlusive infarcts of bones
- Common presenting sign in infants

Autosplenectomy leads to shrunken, fibrotic spleen
- Increased risk of infection with encapsulated organisms as there is no antibody production in the spleen (H flu, strep pneumo)
	- This is the most common cause of death in children\
- Increased risk of salmonella paratyphi osteomyelitis
- Howell-Jolly bodies on blood smear. 

Acute chest syndrome
- Vaso occlusion in pulmonary microcirculation
- Presents with chest pain, shortness of breath and lung infiltrates; often precipitated by pneumonia
- Most common cause of death in adults. 

Pain crisis 

Renal Papillary necrosis
- Gross haematuria and proteinuria

### Sickle cell trait

There will be 45% HbS and 55% HbA. You need more than 55% HbS to sickle so most of the time these patients are asymptomatic.

However in the renal medulla there is extreme hypoxia and hypertonicity which cause sickling. 
	This leads to microinfarctions in the kidney, leading to microscopic haematuria and eventually decreased ability to concentrate urine. 

Lab findings
- Sickle cells and target cells are NOT SEEN. 
- Metabisulfite screen - causes cells with any amount of HbS to sickle; positive in both disease and trait
- Hb electrophoresis confirms the presence and amount of HbS
