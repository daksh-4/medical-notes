# Thrombosis

Pathologic formation of an intravascular blood clot

Characterised by lines of Zahn (alternating layers of RBCs and fibrin) and attachment to the vessel wall. Both features distinguish thrombus from postmortem clot. 

Three major risk factors:
Disruption in blood flow - immobilisation, cardiac wall dysfunction, aneurysm
Endothelial cell damage - Disrupts protective function of endothelial cells  
Hypercoagulable state

How does the endothelium stop thrombosis?
Forms a barrier against the sub endothelial collagen which is what activates the coagulation cascade. 
Makes PGI2 which does the opposite of thromboxane A2 and stops platelet plug formation
NO release
Heparin light molecules activates antithrombin 3 which gets rid of thrombin and other factors. 
Tissue plasminogen activator which converts plasminogen to plasmin which breaks down fibrinogen and fibrin. 
Thrombomodulin modulates thrombin so that it activates protein C which inactivates factors 5 and 8 

Causes of endothelial damage
Atherosclerosis
[[Vasculitis]]
High levels of homocysteine - Vitamin B12 or folate deficiency (can no longer homocysteine to methionine)
- Cystathionine beta synthase deficiency results in high homocysteine levels with homocystinuria. Characterised by vessel thrombosis, mental retardation, lens dislocation and long slender fingers. 

Hypercoagulable state
- Due to excessive procoagulant or defective anticoagulant proteins
- May be inherited or acquired. 
- Present with recurrent DVTs (or hepatic and renal )

Protein C or S deficiency 
- Decreases negative feedback on the coagulation cascade.
- Proteins C and S normally inactivate factors V and VIII. 
- Increased risk for warfarin skin necrosis. 
	- Warfarin blocks vitamin K which negatively affects factors 2,7,9,10, protein C and S. 
	- If you give warfarin protein C and S will degrade even more. 
	- There is increased risk of 2,7,9,10
	- This is why you give heparin alongside to stop the factors. 
	- But if there is a pre-existing deficiency of protein C and S there is very high risk of getting thrombosis in the skin which leads to warfarin skin necrosis

[[Factor V Leiden]]
- Mutated form of factor V that lacks cleavage site for deactivation by proteins C and S
- Most common inherited cause of hypercoagulable state

Prothrombin 20210A
- Inherited point mutation in prothrombin
- results in increased gene expression 
- Promotes thrombus formation 

[[Antithrombin III Deficiency]]
- Decreases the protective effect of heparin like molecules produced by endothelium increasing the risk for thrombus
- PTT does not rise with standard heparin dosing. 
	- High doses of heparin activate limited ATIII but this is not enough so you give warfarin alongside to maintain anticoagulation

Oral contraceptives
- Oestrogen induces increased production of coagulation factors. 
- Which increases the risk for thrombosis.
