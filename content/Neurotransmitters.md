# Neurotransmitters

Noradrenaline, acetylcholine, dopamine, serotonin, GABA, glutamate

Norepinephrine
- ﻿﻿Stress/panic hormone
- ﻿﻿Increased levels in anxiety
- ﻿﻿Decreased levels in depression
	- ﻿﻿Some antidepressants increase NA levels
	- ﻿﻿Serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs)
	- ﻿﻿Desipramine (TCA)
	- ﻿﻿Monoamine Oxidase inhibitors (MAOi)
- Locus cerulus
	- Posterior pons near 4th ventricle
	- Main source of NA in brain
	- Extensive projections that activate under stress
	- Activated in opiate withdrawal

Dopamine
- Synthesised in ventral tegmentum and substantia nigra
- Increased levels in schizophrenia
- Decreased levels in Parkinson's 
- Decreased levels in depression

GABA
- Inhibitory
- Synthesised in the nucleus accumbens (subcortex)
	- Important for pleasure reward
	- NA activated in drug addication and fear
- Decreased levels in anxiety
- Etomidate, propofol, benzos, barbit, activate GABA receptors
- GABA binds to receptor and allows Cl- into cell, hyperpolarising the neurone
- Synthesised via glutamate decarboxylase in neurones
- Broken down by GABA transaminase
- Both enzymes need B6 cofactor
- GABAa GABAb in brain
- GABAc in retina
- Benzos act on GABAa
- Alcohol, zolpidem (ambien) and barbituates also GABAa

Serotonin
- Raphe nucleus (pons)
- Decreased levels in anxiety 
- Decreased levels in depression
	- Many raise serotonin
	- SSRIs
	- SNRIs
	- MAOi - block the breakdown
- Serotonin syndrome
	- SSRIs, MAOi, SNRIs, TCAs
	- Mental state changes - anxiety, delirium, restlessness and disorientation
	- Autonomic hyperactivity - diaphoresis, tachycardia, hyperthermia
	- Neuromuscular - tremor, clonus, hyperreflexia, bilateral Babinski
	- Distinguish from neuroleptic malignant with lead pipe and high CK which you don't see in serotonin syndrome, you'll see clonus instead 
	- Treatment cyproheptadine

Acetylcholine
- Basal nucleus of Meynert (subcortex)
- Increased levels in REM sleep
- Decreased levels in Alzheimer's
- Decreased in Huntington's

Glutamate
- ﻿﻿Major excitatory neurotransmitter
- ﻿﻿N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) receptor is target
- ﻿﻿Huntington's: neuronal death from glutamate toxicity
	- ﻿﻿Glutamate binds NMDA receptor
	- ﻿﻿Excessive influx calcium
	- Cell death
- PCP - antagonist to NMDA, numbs people out. But at high dose hallucinations, agression, ataxia, nystagmus, hypertension, tachycardia
