# Peptic Ulcer Disease

Solitary mucosal ulcer involving proximal duodenum (90%) or distal stomach (10%)

Duodenal ulcer is almost always due to H pylori(>95%); rarely, may be due to ZE syndrome
- ZE syndrome - Gastrinoma leading to massive amounts of acid which can cause peptic ulcer disease

Presents with epigastric pain that improves with meals. Duodenum prepares for receiving the acid bolus from the stomach so makes neutralising and protective mechanisms, which relieves the pain. 

Diagnostic endoscopic biopsy shows ulcer with hypertrophy of Brunner glands. Brunner glands make mucus (with the aim of protecting against the excess acid)

May rupture leading to bleeding from the gastroduodenal artery or [[Acute Pancreatitis]] (both are a complication of posterior ulcer). 

-------

Gastric ulcer is usually due to H pylori (75%); other causes include NSAIDs and bile reflux.

Presents with epigastric pain that worsens with meals    
Ulcer is usually located on the lesser curvature of the antrum.
Rupture carries risk of bleeding from left gastric artery.
Differential diagnosis of ulcers includes carcinoma.

Duodenal ulcers are almost never malignant (duodenal carcinoma is extremely rare).

Gastric ulcers can be caused by gastric carcinoma (intestinal subtype).

Benign peptic ulcers are usually small(< 3 cm), sharply demarcated ("punched-out"), and surrounded by radiating folds of mucosa (Fig. 10.13A).
