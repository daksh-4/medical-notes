# Oesophageal Carcinoma

Subclassified into adenocarcinoma (due to Barrett's) or squamous cell carcinoma

Adenocarcinoma
- Malignant proliferation of glands
- Most common type of oesophageal carcinoma in the West 
- Arises from preexisting Barrett oesophagus usually involves the lower one third of the oesophagus

Squamous cell carcinoma
- Malignant proliferation of squamous cells
- Most common in the poor countries
- Usually arises in the upper and middle third of the oesophagus
- Risk factors
	- Irritation of the squamous mucosa
	- Alcohol and tobacco
	- Very hot tea (China)
	- [[Achalasia]] - rotting food irritates the mucosa
	- [[Oesophageal Web]](Plummer Vinson syndrome) - protrusion of the mucosa
	- Oesophageal injury (lye ingestion)
- Presents late 
	- Progressive dysphagia, weight loss, pain and haematemesis
	- Squamous cell carcinoma may additionally present with hoarse voice (recurrent laryngeal nerve) and cough 

Lymph node spread
- Upper third - cervical nodes
- Middle third - mediastinal or tracheobronchial nodes
- Lower third - celiac and gastric nodes
