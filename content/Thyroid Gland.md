# Thyroid Gland

BEST INITIAL TEST IN ANY THYROID CONDITION IS TSH 

Superior thyroid artery - 1st branch of the external carotid artery
Inferior thyroid - Thyrocervical trunk (off the subclavian)

Begins embryologically as epithelial cells in the floor of the pharynx

![[Pasted image 20250329111813.png|500]]

As tissue from the pharynx descends into the neck it initially maintains connection to the tongue
- Thyroglossal duct which later disappears in development

Two remnants of the duct in child/adult
- Foramen cecum in tongue
- Pyramidal lobe of thyroid 

Thyroglossal duct cyst
- Persistent remnant of the thyroglossla duct
- Midline neck mass - usually painless
- Usually discovered in childhood
- Moves up with swallowing or tongue protrusion 
- May contain thyroid cells

Ectopic thyroid
- Functioning thyroid tissue outside of the gland
- Most common location is the base of the tongue
- Presents as a mass in the tongue
	- Commonly detected during increased demand for hormones
	- Puberty and pregnancy
- May be the only functioning thyroid tissue 
	- May under produce thyroid hormone leading to hypothyroidism
	- Increased TSH increases the growth of the ectopic tissue

Thyroglobulin
- Large protein
- Produced by thyroid follicular cells
- Contains numerous tyrosine molecules
- Which it converts into T3s and T4s

Iodide in the diet is:
- Taken up by follicular cells
- Oxidised to I2 (undergoes "oxidation")
- Added to organic/carbon structures ("organification")

Thyroid peroxidase
- Catalyses 
	- Oxidation of iodide
	- Organification of iodine into MIT/DIT
	- Coupling of MIT/DIT into T3/T4
- TPO antibodies common in autoimmune thyroid disease

- ﻿﻿T4 is major hormone produced by thyroid gland
	- ﻿﻿>90% of thyroid hormone produced is T4
- ﻿﻿T3 more potent hormone
- ﻿﻿T4 is a "prohormone" for T3
- ﻿﻿5' deiodinase converts T4 → T3
- ﻿﻿Most conversion occurs in peripheral tissues

Wolff-Chaikoff effect
- Excessive iodide inhibits organification 
- Less synthesis of MIT and DIT
- Amiodarone contains iodine
	- Can cause hypothyroidism via excess iodine
	- Mimics T4 and competes with T4 for 5 deiodinase
		- This results in a drop in T3 levels leading to increased TSH a little bit when they start amiodarone but eventually levels out

Radioactive iodine
- I 131 emits B decay radiation
- Competes with elemental iodine for uptake and will concentrate in thyroid gland
- Small dose used for imaging I 123
- Large dose can be used to destroy thyroid tissue 
- Used as therapy for hyperthyroidism

TBG
- Thyroxine binding globulin
- Most plasma thyroid hormone is T4
- Thyroid hormones are poorly soluble in water
- Most T4 is bound to TBG
	- Some bound to transthyretin and albumin
	- TBG present in small amount but has high affinity
	- TBG produced in the liver
- Less TBG means less available T4/T3 to tissues

- Oestrogen can raise TBG levels - pregnancy and oral contraceptive pill
	- Modifies TBG molecules
	- Slowing clearance from plasma
	- Will raise total T4 levels

Rise in TBG
More bound T4
Less free T4
Sensed by pituitary
More TSH 
More T4 
Increased free T4 back to normal
TSH falls again

So increased T 4 level to compensate

- Liver failure lowers TBG levels
	- Less production of protein
	- Can lower total T4 levels

Effects of thyroid hormone
- Glucose, lipid metabolism
- Cardiac function
- Bone growth 
- CNS development

Metabolic effects
- Increase glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis
- Fat metabolism
	- ﻿﻿increased lipolysis
- ﻿﻿decreased concentrations of cholesterol, triglycerides
- ﻿﻿increased low-density lipoprotein receptors in liver (+ LDL)
- ﻿﻿increased cholesterol secretion in bile
	- ﻿﻿Hypothyroid patients: increased cholesterol
	- ﻿﻿Hyperthyroid patients: hyperglycemia
- increased basal metabolic rate
	- ﻿﻿Basal rate of energy use per time
	- ﻿﻿Amount of energy burned if you slept all day
- ﻿﻿increased Na/K ATPase pumps
	- ﻿﻿More pumps = more ATP consumed
	- ﻿﻿increased oxygen demand to replenish ATP
	- ﻿﻿increased respiratory rate
	- ﻿﻿increased body temperature
- ﻿﻿Hyperthyroid patients: weight loss

Cardiac effects
- Increased cardiac output, heart rate, stroke volume and contractility
- Due to increased B1 receptors in the heart

CNS and Bone effects

- ﻿﻿TH required for normal bone growth/CNS maturation
- ﻿﻿Childhood hypothyroidism cretinism
	- ﻿﻿Stunted growth
	- ﻿﻿Intellectual disability

Causes
- ﻿﻿Iodine deficiency - malnutrition 
- ﻿﻿Thyroid dysgenesis
- ﻿﻿Inborn errors of hormone synthesis (dyshormonogenesis)
- ﻿﻿TPO most common

Most common treatable cause of intellectual disability

Most babies appear normal
- Maternal T3/T4 crosses placenta
- Newborn screening programs measure T4 or TSH from heel-stick blood specimens

Cretinism
- Intellectual disability
- Coarse facial features
- Short stature
- Umbilical hernia
- Enlarged tongue

Thyroid hormone regulation
- TSH (thyrotropin) released by anterior pituitary
- ﻿﻿Binds to receptors on follicular cells
- ﻿﻿Activates cAMP/PKA 2nd messenger system
- ﻿﻿increases T3/T4 release
	- ﻿﻿increased rate of proteolysis of thyroglobulin
	- ﻿﻿Leads to rapid release of more T3/4
	- ﻿﻿Also stimulates thyroid cell growth, TG synthesis

Pregnancy
- ﻿﻿Multiple effects on thyroid hormone production
	- ﻿﻿Rise in total plasma T4/T3 levels
	- ﻿﻿Rise in TBG levels (estrogen)
- ﻿﻿hCG stimulates thyroid (same alpha unit as TSH)
- ﻿﻿Raises free T4 → lower TSH

Calcitonin

Lowers serum calcium

- ﻿﻿Inhibits renal reabsorption of calcium, phosphorus
- ﻿﻿Increased calcium in urine

- ﻿﻿Probably minor role in calcium handling in humans
- ﻿﻿Used as pharmacologic therapy for hypercalcaemia
