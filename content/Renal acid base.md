[[Nephrology]]

Every day fixed acid is generated in the form of sulfuric acid, phosphoric acid, and organic acids. The lungs can only remove CO2 so the kidney must handle a net acid load of 50-100 mmol/day. To stay in balance it adds new bicarbonate to the plasma. 

Firstly it needs to reabsorb all the filter by carbon and reclaim almost all of it. 

Then it needs to excrete the daily acid load but the urine cannot contain free hydrogen ions in large amounts so it gets trapped in ammonia and phosphate. 

Every hydrogen ion excreted as ammonia or titratable acid generates a new bicarbonate ion, which is returned to the blood. 

Segment by segment

In the proximal tubule there is bulk bicarbonate reabsorption. This does not result in net acid excretion. Mainly H+ is secreted into the lumen, which gets combined with bicarbonate into carbon dioxide and water. Carbon dioxide diffuses into the cell and carbon dioxide regenerates H+ and bicarbonate and the bicarbonate ion goes to the blood. H+ is recycled and not excreted. This process reabsorbs 80-90% of all filtered bicarbonate but results in no change in the net acid balance. 

In the thick ascending limb and the distal convoluted tubule these are mainly support roles with some bicarb reabsorption, no major acid excretion, but they set up the medullary gradient through the countercurrent exchange, which indirectly supports ammonium handling later. 

The collecting duct is the main acid-base control centre with two key cell types:

1. The alpha-intercalated cells, which perform net acid excretion. Hydrogen ATPase secretes H+ into the lumen.
2. There are H+ buffers such as ammonia and ammonium ions and phosphate ions.

The result is H+ leaves the body permanently and new bicarb is added to the blood. They also contain beta-integrated cells which do base secretions so this is the opposite direction: they secrete bicarb into urine and reclaim H+ which are used in alkalosis. 

The key concept is about titratable acid and ammonium. This refers to the phosphate system so H+ + HPO42- becomes H2PO4-. This is limited by filtered phosphate and therefore is a finite resource. 

Ammonium is the dominant adaptive system. In the proximal two-thirds glutamine is converted to NH4+ and a new bicarbonate ion. In the collecting duct NH3 ammonia diffuses into the lumen, binds to H+ and traps it as NH4+ in the urine. This NH4+ cannot diffuse easily back so it traps the acid.
