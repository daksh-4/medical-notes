# Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis

Degenerative disorder of upper and lower motor neurons of the corticospinal tract (Table 17.1). No sensory symptoms!!!!

Anterior motor horn degeneration leads to lower motor neurone signs- flaccid paralysis with muscle atrophy, fasciculations, weakness with decreased muscle tone, impaired reflexes, and negative Babinski sign.

![[Pasted image 20241009135451.png]]

Lateral corticospinal tract degeneration leads to upper motor neurone signs- spastic paralysis with hyperreflexia, increased muscle tone, and positive Babinski sign.

Atrophy and weakness of hands is an early sign.  
- Lack of sensory impairment distinguishes ALS from [[Syringomyelia]].

Most cases are sporadic, arising in middle age adults.  
- Zinc-copper superoxide dismutase mutation (SOD1) is present in some familial cases; leads to free radical injury in neurones

Death - aspiration pneumonia

Riluzole for treatment - decreases glutamate release from neurones
