# AML

Neoplastic accumulation of myeloblasts. Average age 50-60 years

This can be detected in two ways
- Using special stains to look for myeloperoxidase
- Looking for crystal aggregates of MPO seen as Auer rods
- MPO is an oxidative thing involved in killing pathogens 

Subclassification
- Cytogenetic abnormalities
	- Acute promyelocytic leukemia characterised by t(15;17)
		- RAR (retinoic acid receptor) receptor disrupted; promyelocytes accumulate
		- Promyelocytes contain numerous Auer rods; ==risk for DIC== because abnormal promyelocytic cells express pro-coagulants
		- Tretament - ATRA (all trans retinoic acid) causes blasts to mature and become neutrophils
- Lineage of myeloblasts
	- Acute monocytic leukaemia 
		- Proliferation of monoblasts; lack MPO
		- Blasts characteristically infiltrate the gums causing gingival hyperplasia
	- Acute megakaryoblastic leukaemia
		- Proliferation of megakaryoblasts; lack MPO
		- Association with [[Down's Syndrome]] before the age of 5
- Surface markers

AML may also arise from pre-existing dysplasia due to prior exposure to alkylating agents or radiotherapy. 

The develop myelodysplastic syndrome 
- Cytopenias with hypercellular bone marrow (lots of cells being made but not being released)
- Abnormal maturation with increased blasts (<20%)
- Most patients die from infection of bleeding
- May progress to acute leukaemia when the blasts goes over 20%

High risk for infections or bleeding
