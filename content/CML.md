# CML

Predominantly granulocyte proliferation but all cells of the myeloid lineage are +. 

Basophils are characteristically increased. 

![[Pasted image 20240912113550.png]]

Driven by t(9:22) resulting in a BCR-ABL fusion with increased tyrosine kinase activity

First line treatment is imatinib, which blocks tyrosine kinase activity. 

There are three phases to CML: chronic phase, accelerated phase and transformation phase

Splenomegaly is common and an enlarging spleen suggests that it is in the accelerated phase. 
Transformation to acute leukaemia usually follows after - either [[AML]] (2/3) or ALL (1/3)

Transformation
- The mutation that causes CML is in a stem cell either in the haematopoietic stem cell or the myeloblast resulting in overproduction. 
- The mutation is actually in the HSC. 
- This means that this can transform to start over producing myeloblasts or lymphoblasts. 

CML must be distinguished from leukemoid reaction (infection causing neutrophilia)
- CML granulocytes are LAP (leucocyte alkylin phosphatase) negative
	- LAP is needed for the inflammatory response 
- CML is associated with increased basophils
	- In infection you would not get increased basophils
- CML granulocytes exhibit t(9:22)
