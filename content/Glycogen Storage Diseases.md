A useful way to make glycogen storage diseases (GSDs) “stick” is to stop treating them as a list and instead map each one onto **where glycogen sits in physiology** and **what happens when flux through that pathway is blocked**. The symptoms then fall out of first principles.

---

## **1) Start from normal physiology**

Glycogen has two core roles:

- **Liver glycogen** → maintains blood glucose during fasting
    
- **Muscle glycogen** → provides rapid ATP during exertion

So immediately you get two axes of disease:

- **Hepatic GSDs** → problems with _fasting glucose homeostasis_
    
- **Myopathic GSDs** → problems with _exercise metabolism_

Everything else is a consequence of where glucose gets “stuck”.

---

## **2) Hepatic GSDs → think “fasting failure + metabolic rerouting”**

If the liver cannot release free glucose, you get:

### **Core mechanism**

Blocked glycogenolysis/gluconeogenesis → ↓ blood glucose → compensatory hormonal response (↑ glucagon, ↑ adrenaline)

### **Symptoms arise from three linked processes:**

#### **(1) Hypoglycaemia**

- Fasting intolerance
    
- Irritability, seizures (in severe cases)

#### **(2) Glycogen accumulation**

- Hepatomegaly

#### **(3) Metabolic shunting (“overflow pathways”)**

If glucose-6-phosphate (G6P) can’t be exported, it gets diverted:

- → **Glycolysis** → ↑ pyruvate → ↑ lactate → lactic acidosis
    
- → **Pentose phosphate pathway** → ↑ purines → ↑ uric acid (gout)
    
- → **Lipogenesis** → ↑ triglycerides

This gives a _signature cluster_.

---

### **Example:** 

### **Von Gierke disease**

Block: glucose-6-phosphatase

Think: “everything backs up at G6P”

So you get:

- Severe **fasting hypoglycaemia**
    
- **Hepatomegaly**
    
- **Lactic acidosis** (G6P → glycolysis)
    
- **Hyperuricaemia** (PPP flux)
    
- **Hyperlipidaemia** (lipogenesis)

A nice way to encode it:

> “If glucose can’t leave the liver, it spills into _lactate, lipids, and urate_”

---

### **Example:** 

### **Cori disease**

Block: debranching enzyme

Key difference:

- Glycogen breakdown is **impaired but not abolished**

So:

- Milder hypoglycaemia
    
- Hepatomegaly
    
- **No severe lactic acidosis** (because gluconeogenesis still works)

---

## **3) Myopathic GSDs → think “ATP failure during demand”**

Muscle glycogen is not for blood glucose—it’s for **local ATP production**.

So defects here do **not cause hypoglycaemia**, but instead:

### **Core mechanism**

Inability to generate ATP from glycogen during exertion

### **Symptoms:**

#### **(1) Exercise intolerance**

- Early fatigue
    
- Muscle cramps

#### **(2) Energy crisis → muscle breakdown**

- Rhabdomyolysis
    
- Myoglobinuria (dark urine)

#### **(3) No lactate rise (key discriminator)**

- If glycolysis is blocked → no pyruvate → no lactate

---

### **Example:** 

### **McArdle disease**

Block: muscle glycogen phosphorylase

Think: “muscle can’t access its glycogen”

So:

- Exercise intolerance
    
- Muscle cramps
    
- Myoglobinuria
    
- **Flat lactate curve on exercise testing**

Plus the classic:

- **Second wind phenomenon** → symptoms improve as muscle switches to blood glucose and fatty acids

---

## **4) Lysosomal storage → think “structural accumulation”**

### **Example:** 

### **Pompe disease**

Block: lysosomal acid α-glucosidase

This is different:

- Not a cytosolic energy problem
    
- It’s a **lysosomal accumulation problem**

So symptoms reflect **cellular dysfunction from buildup**, especially in muscle:

- Cardiomegaly (infants)
    
- Muscle weakness
    
- Respiratory failure

Think:

> “Glycogen trapped in lysosomes → organ dysfunction, not metabolic crisis”

---

## **5) A compact framework to recall everything**

Instead of memorising each disease, run this mental algorithm:

### **Step 1: Where is the enzyme expressed?**

- Liver → hypoglycaemia
    
- Muscle → exercise intolerance
    
- Lysosome → structural disease

### **Step 2: What happens to glucose flux?**

- Block before glucose release → hypoglycaemia + shunting
    
- Block in muscle glycolysis → no ATP → cramps, no lactate

### **Step 3: Where does the excess substrate go?**

- G6P → lactate, lipids, urate
    
- Glycogen → organ enlargement

---

## **6) High-yield contrasts (exam-ready)**

- **Hypoglycaemia + lactic acidosis** → think liver, early block (e.g. Von Gierke)
    
- **Hypoglycaemia without severe lactate** → partial breakdown (Cori)
    
- **Exercise intolerance + no lactate rise** → muscle glycolysis block (McArdle)
    
- **Cardiomyopathy + muscle weakness in infant** → lysosomal (Pompe)

---

## **7) The deeper unifying idea**

All GSD symptoms can be understood as a mismatch between:

\text{energy demand} - \text{accessible glucose supply}

- Liver diseases → **system-level energy failure (blood glucose)**
    
- Muscle diseases → **local energy failure (ATP)**
    
- Lysosomal disease → **structural toxicity from storage**

Once you anchor each disease to **where energy is needed and where it’s blocked**, the symptom patterns become predictable rather than memorised.
