# Heart Failure

![[Pasted image 20241126163728.png]]

Left ventricular end diastolic pressure is ALWAYS HIGH in left heart failure - closes the mitral valve early. This increases the left atrial pressure which increases pulmonary capillary pressure, resulting in dyspnoea and pulmonary oedema. 

TPR is also always high

- ﻿﻿up pulmonary capillary pressure → up PA pressure
- ﻿﻿up PA pressure → up RV pressure
- ﻿﻿up RV pressure →  up RA pressure
	- ﻿﻿Right atrial pressure = central venous pressure
	- ﻿﻿High pressure in venous system
	- ﻿﻿increased jugular venous pressure (neck veins)
	- ﻿﻿Capillary leak → pitting edema

Systolic HF - less blood pumped
- most common cause is myocardial infarction
- myocytes replaced by scar tissue

Also can be caused by non ischaemic cardiomyopathy
- ﻿﻿May follow upper respiratory infection
- ﻿﻿Many associated viruses 
	- Coxsackie
	- ﻿﻿Influenza, adenovirus, others
- ﻿﻿Virus enters myocytes
- ﻿﻿Causes myocarditis → cardiomyopathy
- ﻿﻿Myocarditis phase may go undiagnosed
- No specific therapy for virus

- Peri partum cardiomyopathy - avoid future pregnancies

- Chemotherapy - treatment with anthracyclines
	- Doxorubicin and daunorubicin

- Mutations
	- Sarcomere protein mutations (alpha and beta myosin and troponin)

- Tachycardia mediated
	- Constant rapid heart rate for weeks/months
	- Leads to depression of LV systolic function
	- Reversible with a slower heart rate

- Takotsubo 
	- Stress induced after severe emotional distress
	- Markedly reduced LVEF
	- Looks like anterior MI 
	- Usually recover in 4-6 weeks

- Alcohol

Diastolic HF - stiff ventricle leading to raised LVEDP

High output heart failure
- Severe anaemia
- Thyroid disease
- Thiamine deficiency 
- AV fistulas 
- Decreased LV filling time but there is a HIGH CARDIAC OUTPUT
- Same heart failure symptoms

Most common cause R heart failure Left heart failure
Occasionally right heart failure occurs in isolation
- ﻿﻿Normal left atrial pressure
- ﻿﻿High pulmonary artery, right ventricular, right atrial pressure
- ﻿﻿Usually secondary to a lung process
- ﻿﻿Pulmonary hypertension
- ﻿﻿COPD
- ﻿﻿This is often called "cor pulmonale"

Low flow signs/symptoms ("forward failure") 
- ﻿﻿Loss of appetite
- ﻿﻿Weight loss (cachexia)
- ﻿﻿Confusion
- ﻿﻿Cool extremities
- ﻿﻿"Narrow pulse pressure"
Seen only with very low cardiac output (systolic HF)
 Not seen in diastolic heart failure

ANP (Atrial natriuretic peptide)
- ﻿﻿Atrial stretch (pressure/volume) → ANP release
- ﻿﻿Vasodilator (LTPR)
- ﻿﻿Constricts renal efferents/ dilates afferents
- ﻿﻿up diuresis
- ﻿﻿Opposite effects of RAAS system but effect is not enough 
- ANP released by atrial myocytes

﻿﻿BNP (brain natriuretic peptide): Ventricles
- ﻿﻿Both rise with volume/pressure overload
- ﻿﻿Both counter effects of RAAS system
- ﻿﻿BNP sometimes used for diagnosis in dyspnea
- ﻿﻿High levels suggest heart failure
- ﻿﻿Low levels suggest other causes of dyspnea

Dilated Cardiomyopathy

- ﻿﻿Systolic heart failure with LV cavity dilation
- ﻿﻿"Eccentric" hypertrophy
	- ﻿﻿Volume overload (chronic retention of fluid in cavity)
	- ﻿﻿Longer myocytes
	- ﻿﻿Sarcomeres added in series

Concentric Hypertrophy

- ﻿﻿Pressure overload
- ﻿﻿Chronic high pressure in ventricle: HTN, Aortic stenosis
- ﻿﻿Decreased compliance (stiff ventricle)
- ﻿﻿Often seen in diastolic heart failure
