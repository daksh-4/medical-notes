Adrenergic Drugs
- ﻿﻿Amplify sympathetic system
- ﻿﻿Sympathomimetic drugs
- ﻿﻿Direct: NE receptor agonists
- ﻿﻿Indirect: Block NE reuptake

Block sympathetic system
- ﻿﻿Adrenergic antagonists/blockers
- ﻿﻿Alpha blockers
- ﻿﻿Beta **blockers**

Adrenergic Activation
- ﻿﻿a1: Vasoconstriction can lead to reflex bradycardia
	- Diastolic BP rises 
- ﻿﻿a2: Vasodilation decrease noradrenaline release
	- Blunting of overall sympathetic system leads to drop in HR and mean BP

- ﻿﻿B1: up Heart Rate/Contractility
	- Increased HR and contractility, increasing cardiac output
	- This increases systolic mainly, diastolic would also go up a bit but this is an indirect effect
- ﻿﻿B2: Vasodilation - reflex tachycardia
	- Diastolic blood pressure falls

Dopamine
- Does not cross BBB
- Peripheral effects are highly dependent on dose
- Low dose - dopamine agonist
	- Vasodilation in the kidneys
- Medium - beta 1 agonist
- High dose - alpha agnoist
	- Vasoconstriction

Epinephrine
- ﻿﻿Also dose dependent effects
- ﻿﻿Low dose: beta-1 and beta-2 agonist
	- ﻿﻿Increased heart rate/contractility
	- ﻿﻿Vasodilation
- High dose: alpha agonist
	- Vasoconstriction

Cocaine

- ﻿﻿Enhances monoamine neurotransmitter activity
	- ﻿﻿Dopamine, Norepinephrine, Serotonin
- ﻿﻿Blockade of presynaptic reuptake pumps
- ﻿﻿Generalized sympathetic activation
- ﻿﻿Also blocks Na channels in nerves (local anesthetic) - this is a separate effect
- Intoxication
	- Massive alpha and beta stimulation
	- Hypertension
	- Tachycardia
	- Chest pain - coronary spasm and increased O2 demands
- Treatment - benzo
	- Avoid beta blockers
	- B2 activation is blunting the alpha activation so if you stop this, you get unopposed alpha stimulation leading to severe hypertension

### Alpha Blockers

Non selective - both alpha 1 and alpha 2

Phenoxybenzamine (irreversible)
- ﻿﻿Used in pheochromocytoma
- So many catecholamines that a reversible one wouldn't cut it 

Phentolamine (reversible)
- ﻿﻿Used to reverse "cheese effect"
- ﻿﻿MAOi drugs block breakdown [[Neurotransmitters]] (depression)
- ﻿﻿Also block breakdown tyramine
- ﻿﻿Eat cheese (tyramine)→ dangerous HTN

Side Effects: hypotension, reflex tachycardia both common 

Selective alpha 1 only

Prazosin, terazosin, doxazosin, tamsulosin - used for hypertension and urinary retention due to BPH

Selective alpha 2 only

Mirtazapine
- Affects serotonin and noradrenaline levels in the CNS

### Sympathomimetic Drugs

Dobutamine
- ﻿﻿Mostly B1
- ﻿﻿up cardiac output
- ﻿﻿up heart rate

MAP pressure usually falls 
- ﻿﻿down TPR (some B2 activation)
- ﻿﻿Limited a1 effects
- ﻿﻿up cardiac output

- ﻿﻿Myocyte effect > SA node
- ﻿﻿More inotropy than chronotropy

Dopamine/Epinephrine
- ﻿﻿B1ß2a1
- ﻿﻿Effects vary with dose
- ﻿﻿up cardiac output → up SBP
- up heart rate
- up DBP (a1 - dose dependent)
- up MAP

Noradrenaline
- ﻿﻿a1ß1
- ﻿﻿a1 >> B1
- ﻿﻿Major effect: Increased TPR
	- Increased DBP and MAP

- ﻿﻿Heart rate effects variable
	- ﻿﻿Some up HR from B1
	- ﻿﻿Some down HR from reflex bradycardia
- ﻿﻿Can see no change in heart rate
- ﻿﻿Cardiac output usually goes up from B1
	- Rise in SBP

Isoproterenol

- ﻿﻿B1ß2
- ﻿﻿up HR/CO from B1

- ﻿﻿Mean blood pressure will fall
	- ﻿﻿Lower diastolic pressure (B2) which tanks
	- ﻿﻿Reflex tachycardia
- ﻿﻿Systolic may rise a bit but not as much as diastolic falls (B1)
	- ﻿﻿Pulse pressure may significantly increase

Phenylephrine

- ﻿﻿a1a2
- ﻿﻿Vasoconstrictor (ITPR)
- ﻿﻿up DBP and MAP
- ﻿﻿Reflex bradycardia
- ﻿﻿More afterload → less CO
