# Chronic Heart Failure

Can be categorised into diastolic and systolic heart failure

Lots of therapies for systolic
- Drugs
- Defibrillators
- Bi ventricular pacemakers

No direct therapies for diastolic
- Treat hypertension, diabetes, A fib
- Monitor for symptoms and diurese

Systolic heart failure
- Over activation of RAAS and B1 stimulation via sympathetic nervous system

ACE i
![[Pasted image 20241126114101.png|500]]
High levels of bradykinin can lead to cough and angiodema

Beta blockers
- Now used in chronic systolic heart failure but still not acute heart failure because it can worsen cardiac output and symptoms
- Bisoprolol and metoprolol are both B1, carvediliol B1,B2,a1

Aldosterone antagonists
- Stops resorption of sodium in the kidneys
- Spironolactone has a similar structure to testosterone
	- Blocks testosterone effects
	- Gynecomastia in men
	- Derived from progesterone
		- In women it can activate progesterone receptors leading to amenorrhea

Neprilysin inhibitors 

Sacubitril

- ﻿﻿Neprilysin: Degrades atrial/brain natriuretic peptide  

Inhibition → raised ANP/BNP allowing them to do their job
- ﻿﻿Antagonists to RAAS system
- ﻿﻿Vasodilatation
- ﻿﻿Natriuresis (sodium excretion)
- ﻿﻿Diuresis (water excretion)
- ﻿﻿Reduced sympathetic tone
- Used in combination with valsartan

Angiodema
- Neprilysina also degrades bradykinin 
- Don't give with ACE i

Ivabradine
- Selective sinus node inhibitor
- Slows heart rate without decreasing contractility benefits heart failure patients
- Inhibits the funny current 
- Used in patients on max dose beta blockers with an increased heart rate 
- Limited evidence of decreasing mortality though

Implantable Cardiac Defibrillator

Patients with heart failure have risk of V tach (which can lead to V fib and death)

Biventricular pacemaker (aka cardiac resynchronisation therapy)
