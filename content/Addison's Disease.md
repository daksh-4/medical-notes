# Addison's Disease

Lack of adrenal hormones
    
Acute insufficiency may arise with Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome.
- Characterized by hemorrhagic necrosis of the adrenal glands (Fig. 15.14), classically due to DIC in young children with N meningitidis infection (meningococcaemia).
- Look for bacterial meningitis with acute onset of shock
        
Lack of cortisol exacerbates hypotension, often leading to death.
        
Chronic insufficiency (Addison disease) is due to progressive destruction of the adrenal glands.
    
Common causes include autoimmune destruction (most common cause in the West), TB (most common cause in the developing world), Fungal, CMV and ==metastatic carcinoma (e.g., arising from lung).==

Autoimmune destruction
- Antibodies to 21-hydoxylase commonly seen 
- Loss of cortex but sparing of the medulla

In secondary causes aldosterone would be normal.
- Glucocorticoid therapy
	- Chronic suppression of ACTH release
	- Leads to adrenal atrophy and when you suddenly stop then there is hypoadrenalism

Diagnostic tests
- 8AM serum cortisol 
- Serum ACTH to distinguish between primary and secondary causes
- Synacthen test
	- If cortisol doesn't rise - primary
	- If it does - secondary 

Clinical features  
- hypotension (postural hypotension)
- weakness
- vomiting and diarrhoea.

Some features only seen in primary cause due to low aldosterone and high ACTH
- Potassium retention 
- H+ retention -> acidosis
- Sodium loss in urine leading to hypovolaemia 
- hyperpigmentation (increased ACTH by-products stimulate melanocytic production of pigment) 

Adrenal crisis
- Acute adrenal insufficiency 
- Abrupt loss of cortisol and aldosterone
- Main manifestation is shock
- Hypoglycaemia 
- Nausea, vomiting, fatigue and confusion
- Often when acute raised adrenal requirements are not met
	- Infection, surgery, trauma
