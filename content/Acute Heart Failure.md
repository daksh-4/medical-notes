# Acute Heart Failure

Left ventricular end diastolic pressure is ALWAYS HIGH in left heart failure - closes the mitral valve early. This increases the left atrial pressure which increases pulmonary capillary pressure, resulting in dyspnoea and pulmonary oedema. 

TPR is also always high

- ﻿﻿up pulmonary capillary pressure → up PA pressure
- ﻿﻿up PA pressure → up RV pressure
- ﻿﻿up RV pressure →  up RA pressure
	- ﻿﻿Right atrial pressure = central venous pressure
	- ﻿﻿High pressure in venous system
	- ﻿﻿increased jugular venous pressure (neck veins)
	- ﻿﻿Capillary leak → pitting edema

Systolic HF - less blood pumped
Diastolic HF - stiff ventricle

Most common cause R heart failure Left heart failure
Occasionally right heart failure occurs in isolation
- ﻿﻿Normal left atrial pressure
- ﻿﻿High pulmonary artery, right ventricular, right atrial pressure
- ﻿﻿Usually secondary to a lung process
- ﻿﻿Pulmonary hypertension
- ﻿﻿COPD
- ﻿﻿This is often called "cor pulmonale"

Low flow signs/symptoms ("forward failure")
- ﻿﻿Loss of appetite
- ﻿﻿Weight loss (cachexia)
- ﻿﻿Confusion
- ﻿﻿Cool extremities
- ﻿﻿"Narrow pulse pressure"
Seen only with very low cardiac output (systolic HF)
 Not seen in diastolic heart failure

ANP (Atrial natriuretic peptide)
- ﻿﻿Atrial stretch (pressure/volume) → ANP release
- ﻿﻿Vasodilator (LTPR)
- ﻿﻿Constricts renal efferents/ dilates afferents
- ﻿﻿up diuresis
- ﻿﻿Opposite effects of RAAS system but effect is not enough 
- ANP released by atrial myocytes

﻿﻿BNP (brain natriuretic peptide): Ventricles
- ﻿﻿Both rise with volume/pressure overload
- ﻿﻿Both counter effects of RAAS system
- ﻿﻿BNP sometimes used for diagnosis in dyspnea
- ﻿﻿High levels suggest heart failure
- ﻿﻿Low levels suggest other causes of dyspnea

In patients with heart failure, a transient rise in volume can put them in fluid overload because they can't deal with the increased volume

Causes
Infection/trauma/surgery
- ﻿﻿Activation of sympathetic nervous system
Ischemia (rare)
- ﻿﻿Decreased cardiac output

NSAIDS
- ﻿﻿Inhibit cyclooxygenase (COX) → decreased prostaglandins
- ﻿﻿Prostaglandins maintain renal perfusion
- ﻿﻿Result: Less renal perfusion → salt/water retention

Loop diuretics
- Imhibit NaKCl pump in the ascending loop of Henle
- IV is better than PO because the gut is also fluid overloaded and swollen so doesn't absorb well
- They are all sulphonamide drugs

- Reduce left atrial pressure - thus improving symptoms
- Left atrial pressure is a marker for preload, which reduces the cardiac output a little bit
- Fall in the cardiac output activates RAAS, increasing peripheral resistance
- Luckily most can tolerate this
- Severe end stage patients can get hypotensive

Thiazide like diuretic
- ﻿﻿Thiazide-like diuretic
- ﻿﻿Inhibits Na-Cl reabsorption distal tubule
	- When given in combo with loop diuretics, blocks all sodium reabsorption after the ascending loop which allows a lot of sodium to leave. 
- ﻿﻿Gives loop diuretics a "kick"
- ﻿﻿Vigorous diuresis
- ﻿﻿Side effects: additional fluid, K+ loss

Nitrates

- ﻿﻿Predominant mechanism is venous dilation
	- ﻿﻿Bigger veins hold more blood
	- ﻿﻿Takes blood away from left ventricle
	- ﻿﻿Lowers LVEDV (preload), LA pressure
	- ﻿﻿Less pulmonary edema → improved dyspnea
- Side effects
	- Headache due to meningeal vasodilation
	- Flushing 
	- Hypotension

Vasodilators - reduce afterload

- ACE i
- Hydralazine
- Reduce afterload increases cardiac output

Nitrates plus hydralazine

Reduce the preload with nitrate and the afterload with hydralazine

But has now been replaced by ACE i 

### Inotropes

Only for systolic heart failure where contractility is reduced

Activate B1 pathways in myocytes

Can also activate B2 pathways in smooth muscle to cause vasodilation

Milrinone
- ﻿﻿Phosphodiesterase 3 inhibitor
	- ﻿﻿PD3 breaks down cAMP in myocyctes
	- ﻿﻿Inhibition → TAMP → contraction
	- ﻿﻿Vascular smooth muscle TAMP (B2) → dilation
- ﻿﻿Increased Inotropy
- ﻿﻿Vasodilation
- ﻿﻿Hypotension

Dobutamine
- Mostly beta 1 agonist
- Weak beta 2 agonist

Dopamine 
- Low dose - dopamine agonist
	- Vasodilation in kidneys
- Medium dose - beta 1 agonist
	- Increased heart rate and contractility
- High dose - alpha agonist
	- Vasoconstriction

Epinephrine
- Low dose - beta 1 and 2 agonism
- High dose - alpha agonist
	- Vasoconstriction

ALL THESE DRUGS CAN CAUSE VENTRICULAR ARRHYTHMIAS

- Not really used anymore 
- Lots of side effects
- No mortality benefit
