# Paroxysmal Nocturnal Haemoglobinuria

Red blood cells (platelets and white blood cells) need protection against complement. They have molecules on the surface: DAF(decay accelerating factor) and MIRL (membrane inhibitor of reactive lysis). DAF accelerates the decay of C3 convertase. 

Both of these are connected to the RBC by GPI. 

In PNH, there is an ACQUIRED defect in the myeloid stem cell, altering GPI. If there is no DAF or MIRL cells are susceptible to complement destruction. 

At night, there is shallow breathing, resulting in CO2 retention, leading to acidosis and this activates complement. This results in lysis of RBCs. 

They get INTRAVASCULAR HAEMOLYSIS at night. 

RBCs, WBCs and platelets are also lysed because they have GPI. 

Haemoglobinuria, Haemoglobinaemia and Haemosiderinuria 

Sucrose or acid test activates complement: used to test for the condition. 
Nowadays use flow cytometry to detect lack of CD55 (DAF). 

Main cause of death in patients is thrombosis of hepatic portal or cerebral veins. Destroyed platelets release cytoplasmic contents causing thrombosis

Complications
- [[Iron Deficiency Anaemia]] as they pee out Hb
- [[AML]] (this is because there are multiple mutations in the myeloid lineage)
