# Cardiac Embryology

![[Pasted image 20250113133808.png]]

Right horn of the sinus venosus develops into the smooth right atrium (sinus venarum) and the left horn develops into the coronary sinus

Cardinal veins
- Form SVC and IVC 
- Superior 
	- Comes from the right common cardinal vein and the right anterior cardinal vein
- Inferior vena cava 
	- Posterior veins 

![[Pasted image 20250113134103.png]]

Cardiac looping
- Heart tube loops ar about 4 weeks gestation 
  - Establishes left right orientation in the chest
  - Requires cilia and dynein
  - Chest doesn't orient correctly in dextrocardia and is seen in Kartagener syndrome

![[Pasted image 20250113134300.png]]

Endocardial cushions
- Contribute to several cardiac structures - atrial septum, ventricular septum, AV valves, Semilunar valves
- Endocardial cushion defects
	- AV canal, AV septal, ASD, VSD, valvular malformation 
	- More common in Down Syndrome

Aorticopulmonary septum
- Formed from neural crest cells
- Migrate to truncal and bulbar ridges 
- Separates aorta and pulmonary arteries
- Fuses with the interventricular septum
- Abnormal formation leads to TGA, tetralogy of Fallot, persistent truncus arteriosus

Septum primum fuses and covers foramen primum

Eventually septum secundum grows and the septum primum acts as a valve. The hole is called the patent foramen ovale

Fetal Circulation
- High resistance to flow in the lungs
- Oxygenated blood comes from the umbilical veins (mother). About 80% saturated (30mmHg)
- Travels to the right atrium bypassing the liver via the ductus venosus 
- Bypasses lungs in the heart via the foramen ovale
- But some blood gets to the right ventricle especially from the head via the SVC. This blood also bypasses the lungs via the ductus arteriosus by connecting the left pulmonary artery to the aorta
- ![[Pasted image 20250113144404.png]]

Changes at birth 
- Pulmonary resistance falls
- More blood goes through the right ventricle through the pulmonary artery to the left atrium. 
- An increase in pressure in the left atrium compared to the right atrium leads to closure of the foramen ovale 
- Ductus arteriosus also closes due to loss of prostaglandings coming from the placental blood. 

- Placenta has a low resistance to flow
- In utero this helps to left atrial pressure low as blood is easily flowing throughout the system.
- At birth there is an increase in peripheral resistance leading to a rise in systemic blood pressure, leading to a rise in left ventricular pressure which all contributes to the increases in left atrial pressure.
