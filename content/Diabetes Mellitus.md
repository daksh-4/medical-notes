# Diabetes Mellitus

### Basic Principles

1. Composed of clusters of cells termed islets of Langerhans (Fig. 15.11)
    
2. A single islet consists of multiple cell types, each producing one type of hormone.
    
3. Insulin is secreted by beta cells, which lie in the center of the islets.
    
    1. Major anabolic hormone; upregulates insulin-dependent glucose transporter protein (GLUT4) on skeletal muscle and adipose tissue (glucose uptake by GLUT4 decreases serum glucose)
        
    2. Increased glucose uptake by tissues leads to increased glycogen synthesis, protein synthesis, and lipogenesis.
        
4. Glucagon is secreted by alpha cells; it opposes insulin in order to increase blood glucose levels (e.g., in states of fasting) via glycogenolysis and lipolysis.

### Link to nephrotic syndrome

[[Nephrotic Syndrome]]

A. High serum glucose leads to nonenzymatic glycosylation of the vascular basement membrane resulting in hyaline arteriolosclerosis [[Arteriosclerosis]].

Glomerular efferent arteriole is more affected than the afferent arteriole, leading to high glomerular filtration pressure.  
- Hyperfiltration injury leads to microalbuminuria.

Eventually progresses to nephrotic syndrome  
- Characterized by sclerosis of the mesangium with formation ofKimmelstiel- Wilson nodules (Fig. 12.11)  

ACE inhibitors slow progression of hyperfiltration-induced damage. Angiotensin II squeezes down efferent artery too so blocking it decreases the hyperfiltration injury

### Long Term Complications 

A. Nonenzymatic glycosylation (NEG) of the vascular basement membrane

l. NEG of large- and medium-sized vessels leads to atherosclerosis and its resultant complications.

1. Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death among diabetics.
    
2. Peripheral vascular disease in diabetics is the leading cause of nontraumatic amputations.
    
1. NEG of small vessels (arterioles) leads to hyaline arteriolosclerosis (Fig. 15.13A).
    
    1. Involvement of renal arterioles leads to glomerulosclerosis, resulting in small, scarred kidneys with a granular surface (Fig. 15.13B).
        
    2. Preferential involvement of efferent arterioles leads to glomerular hyperfiltration injury (due to increased pressure in the glomerulus) with microalbuminuria that eventually progresses to nephrotic syndrome; characterized by Kimmelstiel-Wilson nodules in glomeruli. This eventually results in nephrotic syndrome. 

3. NEG of haemoglobin produces glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), a marker of glycemic control.

B. Osmotic damage

1. Glucose freely enters into [[Schwann Cells]] (which myelinate peripheral nerves), pericytes of retinal blood vessels and the lens.
    
2. Aldose reductase converts glucose to sorbitol, resulting in osmotic damage. 
    
3. Leads to peripheral neuropathy, impotence, blindness, and cataracts; diabetes is the leading cause of blindness in the developed world.
