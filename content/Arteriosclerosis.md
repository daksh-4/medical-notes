# Arteriosclerosis

Atherosclerosis - thickening of the intima
Arteriolosclerosis - thickening of the wall by a deposition of protein or hyperplasia of the smooth muscle
Monckeberg medial sclerosis - calcification of the media of the blood vessel

Atherosclerosis
- Most common arteries involved are abdominal aorta, coronary, popliteal, internal carotid
- Stenosis of medium sized vessels leads to peripheral vascular disease, angina, ischaemic bowel disease. Needs >70% stenosis for symptoms
- Plaque rupture with thrombosis leads to MI or [[Stroke]]. 
- Plaque rupture with embolisation results in atherosclerotic emboli. Characterised by cholesterol clefts in the emboli. 
- Weakening of the vessel wall results in aneurysm

Arteriolosclerosis
- Narrowing of small arterioles 
- Divided into hyaline and hyperplastic types

Hyaline
- Proteins leaking into the vessel wall. 
- Produces vascular thickening
- Seen as pink hyaline on microscopy
![[Pasted image 20240913165832.png]]
- Caused by benign hypertension and diabetics (non enzymatic glycosylation of the membrane)
- Results in reduced vessel caliber with end organ ischaemia
	- Classically occurs in the kidney causing glomerular scarring. 

Hyperplastic 
- Hyperplasia of smooth muscle 
- Leads to onion skin appearance 
- ![[Pasted image 20240913170221.png]]
- Caused by malignant hypertension
- May lead to fibrinoid necrosis of the vessel wall and reduced vessel caliber
- Classically causes acute renal failure with 'flea bitten appearance' due to pin point haemorrhages 

Monckeberg Medial Calcific Sclerosis
- Calcification of the media that is non obstructive
- Not clinically significant
- Seen as an incidental finding
- ![[Pasted image 20240913170611.png]]
- Can show up on mammography as calcifications
