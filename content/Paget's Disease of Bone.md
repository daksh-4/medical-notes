# Paget's Disease of Bone

Imbalance between osteoclast and osteoblast function

Usually seen in late adulthood (average age> 60 years)

Aetiology is unknown; possibly viral

Localized process involving one or more bones; does not involve the entire skeleton

Osteoclasts start resorbing bone, eventually the osteoblast needs to lay down bone to prevent this. The osteoclast burns out and only the osteoblast is working and laying down bone. This results in thick bone but the lamellar lines have not been sealed. 

Three distinct stages are (l) osteoclastic, (2) mixed osteoblastic-osteoclastic, and (3) osteoblastic.
- End result is thick, sclerotic bone that fractures easily.
- Biopsy reveals a mosaic pattern oflamellar bone (Fig. 18.2).

Clinical features
- Bone pain - due to microfractures
- Increasing hat size - Skull is commonly affected.
- Hearing loss - impingement on cranial nerve
- Lion-like facies - involvement of craniofacial bones
- Isolated elevated alkaline phosphatase-most common cause of isolated elevated alkaline phosphatase in patients> 40 years old

Treatment includes
- Bisphosphonates - induces apoptosis of osteoclasts
- Indications include bone pain, skull or long bone deformity, fracture or periarticular Paget's

Complications include
- High-output cardiac failure-due to formation of AV shunts in bone  
- Osteosarcoma
- Deafness due to cranial nerve entrapment 
- Fractures
