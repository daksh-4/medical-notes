# Transitional Cell Carcinoma of the Bladdeer

A. Malignant tumor arising from the urothelial lining of the renal pelvis, ureter, bladder, or urethra

1. Most common type of lower urinary tract cancer; usually arises in the bladder

2. Major risk factor is cigarette smoke; additional risk factors are naphthylamine, azo dyes, and long-term cyclophosphamide or phenacetin use.
    
3. Generally seen in older adults; classically presents with painless hematuria
    
4. Arises via two distinct pathways (Fig. 12.21)
    
    1. Flat-develops as a high-grade flat tumor and then invades; associated with early p53 mutations
        
    2. Papillary-develops as a low-grade papillary tumor that progresses to a high- grade papillary tumor and then invades; not associated with early p53 mutations. A papillary growth is a fibrovascular growth with a blood vessel inside and epithelium on the top. 
        
5. Tumors are often multifocal and recur ("field defect"). The whole urothelium is mutated so there is often reoccurence
