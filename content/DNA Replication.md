# DNA Replication

## Initiation

Replication starts at origins of replication, where initiator proteins recognize the origin sequence and recruit helicase. Eukaryotic chromosomes have many origins (allowing the whole chromosome to replicate in a manageable time); bacterial chromosomes typically have one.

## Unwinding the helix

Helicase separates the two DNA strands at the origin, creating one or two replication forks that move outward. As helicase unwinds the double helix, it generates torsional strain (positive supercoiling) in the DNA ahead of the fork. Topoisomerase works ahead of the fork, making transient breaks in the backbone and rejoining it to relieve this strain — without it, the DNA ahead of the fork would over-wind and the fork would stall.

Single-strand binding proteins coat the separated single strands to keep them from reannealing or forming secondary structure before they're copied.

## Leading vs lagging strand synthesis

DNA polymerase can only synthesize 5'→3', and needs a primer (a free 3'-OH) to start — see [[DNA Polymerases]]. Because the two template strands are antiparallel, this creates an asymmetry at each fork:

- **Leading strand**: the template strand happens to run 3'→5' in the same direction the fork is opening, so the new strand is synthesized continuously, 5'→3', in the same direction as fork movement. Only one primer is needed for the entire strand.
- **Lagging strand**: the template strand runs the opposite way, so continuous synthesis toward the fork is impossible. Instead, primase repeatedly lays short RNA primers as the fork opens, and DNA polymerase extends each one 5'→3' *away* from the fork until it reaches the previously made fragment. This produces a series of short, discontinuous **Okazaki fragments**.

## Maturation of the lagging strand

Each Okazaki fragment starts with an RNA primer. DNA Pol I (prokaryotes) / Pol δ removes these primers and fills the gaps with DNA, and DNA ligase seals the remaining nicks in the sugar-phosphate backbone to join the fragments into one continuous strand.
