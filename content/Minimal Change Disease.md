# Minimal Change Disease

Most common cause of nephrotic syndrome in children

Usually idiopathic; may be associated with [[Hodgkin's Lymphoma]]

Normal glomeruli on H&E stain (Fig. 12.7A); lipid may be seen in proximal tubule cells.
- Glomerular filtration barrier contains three layers endothelial cells, basement membrane and foot processes of the podocytes (epithelial cells)
- Flattening is caused by cytokines
	- In Hodgkin syndrome, there is massive production of cytokines which is why they have B symptoms

Effacement of foot processes on electron microscopy (EM, Fig. 12.7B)

No immune complex deposits; negative immunofluorescence (IF)

F. Selective proteinuria (loss of albumin, but not immunoglobulin)  

G. Excellent response to steroids (damage is mediated by cytokines from T cells). The only nephrotic syndrome which has a massive response to steroids.
