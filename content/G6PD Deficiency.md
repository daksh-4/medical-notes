# G6PD Deficiency

X linked recessive disorder resulting in reduced half life of G6PD. Renders cells susceptible to oxidative stress. 

Aftrican variant has a mildly reduced half life and the Mediterranean variant has a markedly reduced half life of G6PD. The hihg gene frequency is likely due to a protective role against Falciparum malaria. 

A reduced half life means younger RBCs die early.  

Red blood cells protect against oxidative stress by glutathione. Once oxidised, glutathione needs to be reduced by NADPH to convert it back to glutathione. 
NADPH is made by G6PD. So if deficient it can't protect the RBCs.

Oxidative stressors
- Causes Hb to precipitate as Heinz bodies
- Include infections, drugs (primaquine, sulfa drugs and dapsone) 
- Fava beans 
Heinz bodies are removed by splenic macrophages resulting in bite cells
This leads to predominantly intravascular haemolysis.

Presents
- Haemoglobinuria 
- Back pain because the haemoglobin is nephrotoxic
- Hours after exposure to oxidative stress

Heinz preparation highlights precipitated Hb. Enzymatic studies confirm deficiency (but can only be done after the acute haemolytic attack is over)
