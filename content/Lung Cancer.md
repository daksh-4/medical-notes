# Lung Cancer

Risk factor, cigarette smoke (polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons and arsenic for squamous cell carcinoma are specifically mutagenic), radon and asbestos (more likely to develop lung cancer than mesothelioma)

Have to compare imaging with past imaging to see if the lesions have been present for a long time and unchanged. 

Benign lesions can present similarly and much more common in young patients
- Granuloma (histoplasma)
- Bronchial hamartoma(benign mass of tissue that normally belongs in that region so contains lung tissue and cartilage); often calcified on imaging

Classic division of lung carcinoma
- Small cell carcinoma 15% - not amenable to surgical resection normally chemo
- Non-small cell carcinoma 85% - does not respond well to chemo
	- Adenocarcinoma (40%) - glands or mucus production
	- Squamous cell (30%) - keratin pearls or intercellular bridges
		- ALSO ASSOCIATED WITH HYPERTROPHIC PULMONARY OSTEOARTHROPATHY
	- Large cell carcinoma (10%) - none of the above
	- Carcinoid tumour (5%)

Local involvements
 - Pleural involvement (most likely adenocarcinoma and other peripheral tumours)
 - SVC obstruction
 - Involvement of the recurrent laryngeal or phrenic nerve
 - Compression of the sympathetic chain - ptosis, pinpoint pupil ([[Miosis]]) and anhidrosis
