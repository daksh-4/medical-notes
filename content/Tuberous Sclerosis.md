# Tuberous Sclerosis

Hamartomas
- Benign malformations of cell tissue
- Resembles tissue of origin but is disorganised

Main clinical features - seizures due to CNS hamartomas

Autosomal dominant with variable expressivity

De novo mutations: 80% cases (no family history)

Mutation in TSC1 or TSC2 gene
- ﻿﻿TSC1: Hamartin
- ﻿﻿TSC2: Tuberin

Proteins inhibit mTOR
- ﻿﻿Mechanistic target of rapamycin
- ﻿﻿Kinase

Mutation → mTOR overactivity → cell growth
• Especially cell size

- ﻿﻿Widespread tumor formation
- ﻿﻿Involves MULTIPLE organ systems
- ﻿﻿Numerous hamartomas and other neoplasms

Classic features
- ﻿﻿Seizures - most common presenting feature
- ﻿﻿"Ash leaf spots": Pale, hypopigmented skin lesions
- ﻿﻿Facial skin spots (angiofibromas)
- Shagreen patches - orange peel on lower back (connective tissue hamartoma)
- Ungal fibroma
- ﻿﻿Intellectual impairment

Cortical tubers
- Distorted cotex
- Seizures
Subependymal nodules
- Ependyma - lining of ventricles

Subependymal giant cell astrocytoma
- Low grade astrocytoma 
- Usually occur at the interventricular foramen 
- May obstruct ventricles -> hydrocephalus

Rhabdomyomas [[Rhabdomyoma]]
- Tumours of muscle cells
- Clasic cardiac features
- Sometimes detected prenatal 
- Rare symptoms from obstruction and arrhythmia

Renal Angiomyolipomas
- Proliferation of epitheloid cells around vessels 
- Growth and haemorrhage -> pain
- May cause renin-dependent hypertension
- Risk of CKD 
	- Compression of normal renal tissue
