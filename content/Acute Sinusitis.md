# Acute Sinusitis

Most common infectious agents
- Strep pneumoniae
- H flu
- Rhinoviruses

Predisposing factors
- Nasal obstruction - septal deviation or polyps
- Recent local infection e.g. rhinitis or dental extraction
- swimming/diving
- smoking

Features
- Facial pain 
	- Frontal pressure pain worse on bending forwards
- Nasal discharge - thick and purulent
- Nasal obstruction

Management 
- analgesia 
- intranasal decongestants or nasal line but these aren't evidence based
- Intranasal corticosteroids if symptoms present for more than 10 days
- Phenoxymethylpenicillin for very unwell patients
	- You might see double sickening where an initial viral sinusitis worsens due to secondary bacterial infection
