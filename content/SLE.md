# SLE

Chronic systemic disorder

### Epidemiology
More common in African Americans and Hispanics. Female Bias less dramatic in children and older adults. Possibly due to oestrogen reducing the apoptosis of self reactive B cells. 

### Pathology

- Antibodies are often directed against host nuclear material. 
- This results in activation of complement which damages tissue - decreased levels of CH50, C3 and C4. 

- When cells are broken down for example by UV light, apoptosis occurs and the apoptotic material is digested by macrophages. 
	-  If there is poor clearance of the debris, a self reactive B lymphocyte may bind to it and produce antibodies against nuclear antigens. 
	- Therefore the next time a cell undergoes apoptosis antigen-antibody complexes will form and get taken up by dendritic cells and activate Toll like receptors, further activating B cells. 
	- Additional antigen antibody complexes are formed which can go into the blood, accumulate and activate complement. 

- Patients with early complement deficiency (C1q, C4, C2) are more likely to have SLE. This is because these complement molecules will activate the system, leading to the production of C3b which is an opsonin and will destroy the antibody antigen complex. 
- Erythrocytes also have a receptor called CR1 which binds C3b and takes antigen antibody complexes to the spleen for them to get destroyed. 

### Clinical Features

- Fever, weight loss, fatigue, lymphadeopathy, Raynaud's phenomenon (arterial vasospasm white to blue to red)
- Malar butterfly or discoid rash particularly on exposure to sunlight. 
- Oral or nasopharyngeal ulcers
- Arthritis
- Serositis (e.g. [[Pericarditis]])
- CNS problems
	- Psychosis or seizures
- Renal damage - common cause of death in patients with lupus
	- nephritic syndrome - diffuse proliferative glomerulonephritis
	- nephrotic syndrome - membranous glomerulonephritis
	- combination of both 
	- no damage to the kidney
- Anaemia, thrombocytopenia or leukopenia
	- Because they form antibodies against cells in the blood. This is a type 2 hypersensitivity reaction although lupus is generally a Type 3 hypersensitivity reaction. 
- Libman-Sacks [[Endocarditis]] 
	- Small vegetations on both sides of the mitral valve. Not really seen anymore because of treatment effectiveness. 

### Investigations
- ANA antibody - sensitive (tells you there might be something going on but doesn't tell you if there's lupus or not it is a good rule out)
- Anti-dsDNA and anti-Sm are specific for lupus (if present then it tells you that it could be lupus)
	- AntidsDNA is associated with disease activity and renal involvement. 
- Antiphospholipid antibody
	- Autoantibody against proteins bound to phospholipids 
		- Anticardiolipin (VDRL and RPR look for syphilis so lupus causes false positive)
		- Lupus anticoagulant (falsely elevated partial thromboplastin time test) - Antiphospholipid antibody syndrome - APA plus hypercoagulable state requiring lifelong anticoagulation
		- Anti ß2 glycoprotein 1 
- Antihistone antibody
	- Drug induced SLE - hydralazine, procainamide, isoniazid. Have a positive ANA that is specific to antihistone antibodies. 
	- CNS and renal involvement are rare and removal of the drug results in remission. 

### Treatment
- Avoid sunlight
- Glucocorticoids for flares
- Other immunosuppressive agents
