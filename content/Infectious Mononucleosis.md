# Infectious Mononucleosis

EBV infection that results in a lymphocytic leukocytosis comprised of reactive CD8+ T cells

CMV is a less common cause

EBV primarily infects the oropharynx, liver (EBV hepatitis) and B cells. 

CD8+ T cell response to detect viral proteins on MHC class 1. 

This results in:
- Generalised lymphadenopathy 
	- Lymph nodes have three regions - cortex (B cells), paracortex (T cells) and medulla
	- There is hyperplasia of the paracortex
- Splenomegaly (periarterial lymphatic sheath is expanded)
	- Spleen has red pulp and white pulp (containing the lymphocytes). The white pulp surrounds the blood vessels and this area expands in mono
- High white count with atypical lymphocytes (CD8+ T cells)
	- Large nucleus and abundant blue cytoplasm. It almost looks like a monocyte but is actually a reactive T cell. 

Screening
- Monospot test detects IgM heterophile antibodies
- Usually turns positive within 1 week after infection
- Negative test suggests CMV as the cause 
- Definitive diagnosis is made by testing for EBV viral capsid antigen. 

Complications
- Increased risk for splenic rupture avoid contact sports for up to one year
- Rash if exposed to penicillin
- Dormancy of virus in the B cells
