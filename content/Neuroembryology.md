### Neural Tube Defects

Neuropores fail to fuse in 4th week
- ﻿﻿Neuropore = opening of neural tube
- ﻿﻿Rostral neuropore at head, Caudal at tail

﻿﻿Spina Bifida
- ﻿﻿Caudal neuropore fails to close posteriorly
- ﻿﻿Bones do not close around spinal cord/meninges
- ![[Pasted image 20250116152701.png]]
- Occulta - meninges and spinal cord are ok but bones don't form so all you see is the tuft of hair
- Meningocele - meninges bulge out but the spinal cord stays in
- Myelomeningocele - associated with Chiari Malformation and the spinal cord + meninges come out
- Can be detected in utero and surgery can repair the defect
- Permanent neuro deficits often result
	- Leg weakness or paralysis
	- Bowel/bladder problems

Anencephaly ("without head")
- ﻿﻿Rostral neuropore fails to close anteriorly
- ﻿﻿Absence of major portions brain/skull
- Not compatible with life
- Ultrasound
	- Open calvaria
	- Frog-like appearance of fetus
- Mother will have polyhydramnios because the baby can't swallow amniotic fluid normally 

Encephalocele
- Brain or meninges herniate through a skull defect
- Most common site is the occipital bone
- Least common neural tube defect

Risks
- Decreased folic acid intake
- Type 1 diabetes
- Obesity
- Valproic acid and carbamazepine

Alpha Fetal Protein

- ﻿﻿Fetal specific globulin
- ﻿﻿Made by fetal yolk sac, fetal organs  
    Function unknown
- ﻿﻿Excreted by fetal kidneys
- ﻿﻿16 to 18 weeks → measure maternal serum level
- ﻿﻿If high, MAY indicated NTD
	- ﻿﻿Interpretation complex

Follow-up tests

- ﻿﻿Amniotic fluid AFP (requires amniocentesis)
- ﻿﻿Amniotic fluid acetylcholinesterase (AChE)
- ﻿﻿If both elevated, strongly suggests NTD

### Holoprosencephaly (cephalic malformation)

- ﻿﻿Cephalic malformation
- ﻿﻿Failure of cleavage of prosencephalon
- ﻿﻿Left/right hemispheres fail to separate
- ﻿﻿Usually happens during weeks 5-6
- ﻿﻿Failure of signalling molecules
	- ﻿﻿Sonic hedgehog implicated
- ﻿﻿Key findings are facial abnormalities:
	- ﻿﻿Cleft lip/palate
	- ﻿﻿Cyclopia (one eye)

Associations: trisomy 13 (Patau syndrome), trisomy 18 (Edward's syndrome), Fetal alcohol syndrome

### Chiari Malformation

Anatomic anomalies of cereballum

Group of congenital disorders - 1 through 4

Downward displacement of the cerebellum. Can result in hydrocephaly and brain stem compression

Chiari I malformation
- Abnormal shape of cerebellar tonsils
	- Small rounded structure at the bottom of the cerebellum
- Tonsils displaced below foramen magnum
- Associated with Syringomyelia
- Usually no symptoms until adolescence/adulthood
- Headache
	- Due to meningeal irritation
	- Cough headache
- Cerebellar symptoms
- Cranial nerve dysfunction

Chiari II malformation (Arnold Chiari)
- Downward displacement of cerebellar vermis and tonsils
- Brainstem malformation becuase there is much more brain
- Spinal myelomeningocele - usually seen prenatally
- Blockage of aqueduct leading to hydrocephalus
- Myelomeningocele -> paralysis below the defect
- Hydrocephalus in infants
	- Large head circumference
	- Anterior fontanelle distended
	- Sutures widely split
	- Abnormal percussion - cracked pot sound or Macewen's sign

### Dandy Walker Malformation

- ﻿﻿Developmental anomaly of the fourth ventricle
- ﻿﻿Often detected by ultrasound in utero
- ﻿﻿Hypoplasia or agenesis of cerebellar vermis
- ﻿﻿Cerebellar hemispheres often flattened
	- ﻿﻿Separated by "Dandy-Walker cyst"
- ﻿﻿Cysts of 4th ventricle → hydrocephalus
- ﻿﻿Many, many associated symptoms/conditions
- ﻿﻿Affected children
	- ﻿﻿Hydrocephalus
	- ﻿﻿Delayed development
	- ﻿﻿Motor dysfunction (crawling, walking)
