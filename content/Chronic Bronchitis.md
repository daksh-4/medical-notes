# Chronic Bronchitis

Chronic productive cough (cups of mucus) lasting at least 3 months

Associated with smoking

- Respiratory epithelium is pseudostratified - tall ciliated and sit on a basement membrane.
- Underneath you have the lamina propria which contains the blood vessels
- Underneath that you have the submucosa which has serous glands which makes a wet fluid that moistens the lungs and there are mucinary glands which trap particles. The mucinous glands take up around 40% of the wall. 
- Underneath that you have cartilage. 

If someone smokes, the lungs increased the amount of mucus produced to deal with the amount of particles. Hypertrophy of bronchial mucinous glands

Ried index - thickness of the mucinous glands as compared to the wall of the lungs

Some of the excess mucus is coughed up. Some goes back and plugs the airways.

Clinical features
- Productive cough
- Cyanosis increased PaCO2 and decreased PaO2
- Increased risk of infection due to obstruction and cor pulmonale
	- Blood vessels of the lung recognise where there is low oxygen in the alveoli and clamp down vessels supplying that area. The right heart has to pump harder if the vessels clamp down. This leads to right ventricular hypertrophy and eventually right sided heart failure which leads to cor pulmonale
