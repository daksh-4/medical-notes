# Inflammatory Bowel Disease

Chronic, relapsing inflammation of bowel

Possibly due to abnormal immune response to enteric flora

Classically presents in young women (teens to 30s) as recurrent bouts of bloody diarrhea and abdominal pain

More prevalent in the West, particularly in Caucasians and Eastern EuropeanJews

Diagnosis of exclusion; symptoms mimic other causes of bowel inflammation (e.g., infection).

Subclassified as ulcerative colitis or Crohn disease (Table 10.1)

You get granulomas in Crohn's. Granulation tissue have myofibroblasts which contract and can narrow the lumen, causing a stricture. They also pull the fat up with it causing the fat to come closer to the serosa (creeping fat). There is normally oxalate in the bowel but not normally absorbed but if there is inflammation it is absorbed and binds with calcium to cause calcium oxalate kidney stones. 

Fistulas are caused by full thickness inflammation.
