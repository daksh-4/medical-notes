# Restrictive Cardiomyopathy

Decreased compliance of ventricular endomyocardium
Restricts filling during diastole - SEVERE diastolic dysfunction

Causes - something infiltrating the myocardium
- [[Amyloidosis]]
- [[Sarcoidosis]] 
- [[Haemochromatosis]] - commonly causes dilated cardiomyopathy
- Post radiation - chronic inflammation leading to fibroblast involvement
	- Can also develop restrictive [[Pericarditis]], coronary artery disease, valvular disease and conduction abnormalities
- Endocardial fibroelastosis (happens to children)
	- Endocardial thickening (innermost myocardium)
	- ﻿﻿Infants in first year of life
	- ﻿﻿Thick myocardium
	- ﻿﻿Proliferation of fibrous (collagen) and elastic fibers
- Loeffler syndrome - eosinophilic infiltrate in the wall of the heart leading to fibrosis also get eczema and lung fibrosis
	- Primary due to neoplasm
	- Secondary due to overproduction as a result of cytokines - parasitic infections (ascaris lumbricoides). Tumours and lymphomas another secondary cause
	- Eosinophils deposit in the endomyocardium, which can result in thrombus formation

Presents as congestive heart failure

ECG findings
- Myocardial infiltration may disrupt electrical activity 
- Classic finding is low voltage ECG - seen in amyloidosis and Fabry disease
- Diminished QRS amplitudes
- Can also get V tach and AV block 

Have a normal left ventricular ejection fraction

Left ventricular volume is also normal 

Restricted filling leads to increased atrial pressure
- Dilated left and right atria 

Clinical Features
- Dyspnoea
- Prominent right heart failure (right ventricle is more affected by restrictive cardiomyopathy)
	- Elevated JVP
	- Lower extremity oedema 
	- Liver congestion
	- May lead to cirrhosis 
- Kussmual's sign - inspiration causes a rise in the JVP
