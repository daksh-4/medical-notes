# Cervical Spondylosis

Clinical features  

- Neck pain: The most common symptom is chronic neck pain, which may be associated with stiffness and limited range of motion. Pain can be localised or radiate to the shoulders and upper back.
- Radiculopathy: Compression of cervical nerve roots can lead to radicular pain, paraesthesia, and weakness in the upper limbs. The distribution of symptoms typically follows a dermatomal pattern corresponding to the affected nerve root(s).
- Myelopathy: Cervical spondylotic myelopathy is a more severe manifestation, resulting from spinal cord compression. Symptoms include clumsiness in the hands, gait disturbance, limb weakness, and, in advanced cases, bowel or bladder dysfunction.
- Headaches: Occipital headaches can occur due to irritation of the cervical nerve roots or muscles in the upper cervical region.
- Examination findings: Key findings may include reduced range of motion in the neck, tenderness over the cervical spine, and signs of radiculopathy or myelopathy (e.g., positive Spurling's test, hyperreflexia, or gait instability).

Imaging:  

- X-rays: Plain radiographs of the cervical spine can reveal disc space narrowing, osteophytes, and facet joint arthropathy.
- MRI: Magnetic resonance imaging is the gold standard for evaluating cervical spondylosis, providing detailed images of soft tissue structures, including the spinal cord, nerve roots, and intervertebral discs.
- Electrophysiological studies: Nerve conduction studies and electromyography can help differentiate cervical radiculopathy from peripheral neuropathies or other conditions.

Management  

- Conservative
    - Analgesia: Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are the first-line treatment for pain management.
    - Physiotherapy: including exercises to improve neck strength and flexibility, is often beneficial. Postural training and ergonomic adjustments can also help alleviate symptoms.
    - Cervical collar: A soft cervical collar may be used temporarily for symptomatic relief in acute flare-ups, although long-term use is discouraged due to the risk of muscle weakness.
- Interventional procedures
    - Cervical epidural steroid injections: In patients with radicular symptoms, epidural steroid injections can provide temporary relief by reducing inflammation around the affected nerve root.
    - Facet joint injections or radiofrequency ablation: These procedures may be considered for patients with chronic facet-mediated pain.
