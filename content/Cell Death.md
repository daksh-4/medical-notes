# Cell Death

- Hallmark of cell death is the loss of nucleus via three steps
    
    - Pyknosis (nucleus shrinks down)
    - Karyorrhexis (nucleus breaks down into pieces)
    - Karyolysis (the pieces are broken down into enzymes into individual subunits)

- Necrosis is defined by the death of a lot of cells followed by acute inflammation. It is always pathologic
    - Coagulative necrosis when tissue remains firm and cells are present but there is no nucleus. Occurs in ischaemic infarction but not in the brain
    - Red infarction is when the blood reenters and if tissue is loosley organised allowing the blood to reeenter
    - Liquefactive necrosis - occurs in brain infarction because microglial cells destroy everything (lipid-rich brain tissue with little structural stroma). Seen in abscesses (neutrophil-derived enzymes) and pancreatitis. ENZYME MEDIATED
    - Gangrenous necrosis - tissue holds its structure but becomes mummified. Characteristic of ISCHAEMIA OF THE LOWER LIMB. If superimposed with an infection, wet gangrene occurs
    - Caseous necrosis - tissue becomes soft and friable like cottage cheese. Liquefactive and coagulative combination. The thickness is attributed to granulomatous inflammation. Mycolic acid in the mycobacterial cell wall resists macrophage digestion, while central hypoxia and TNF-alpha-mediated macrophage death in the granuloma add to the debris
    - Fat necrosis - lipase releases free fatty acids from triglycerides, which then react with calcium to form calcium soaps (saponification), consuming serum calcium (hypocalcaemia is a poor prognostic marker in pancreatitis). Seen in trauma to the fat and pancreatitis mediated damage of peripancreatic fact. Presents as a mass due to inflammatory response (biopsy shows giant cells, fat and calcification). Presence of calcification on mammography can be a sign of ductal carcinoma in situ but can also be benign due to trauma. Side note - Psammoma bodies seen in cancer are a result of saponification where fatty acids released by trauma of lipase join with calcium leading to dystrophic calcification.
    - Fibrinoid necrosis - necrotic damage to the blood vessel wall. Leaking of proteins into the wall results in bright pink staining which is characteristic of malignant hypertension (very bad leads to papilloedema) or [[Vasculitis]]. Pre-eclampsia is a cause of fibrinoid necrosis in the placental blood vessels.

- Apoptosis - energy dependent, genetically programmed cell death
    - Examples - endometrial shedding during menstrual cycle, removal of cells during embryogenesis, CD8+ T cell-mediated killing of virally infected cells
    - Morphology - dying cell shrinks, nucleus condenses and fragments, apoptotic bodies fall from cell and are removed by macrophages and not followed by inflammation.
    - Mediators - CASPASES. They activate proteases to break down the cytoskeleton allowing it to shrink. Activate endonucleases to break down DNA.
        - Intrinsic mitochondrial pathway - cellular injury, DNA damage or decreased hormonal stimulation inactivates Bcl2 (stabilises the mitochondrial membrane preventing the leakage of cytochrome C) or cytochrome C leaking from inner mitochondrial matrix into the cytoplasm
        - Extrinsic receptor ligand pathway - FAS ligand binds FAS death receptor (CD95) on target cell. TNF binds TNF receptor on target cell
        - Cytotoxic CD8+ T cell pathway - perforins create pores in the membrane of the target cell and the granzyme enters pores and activates caspases.
