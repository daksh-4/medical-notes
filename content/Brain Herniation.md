# Brain Herniation

Displacement of brain tissue due to mass effect or increased intracranial pressure

Tonsillar herniation involves displacement of the cerebellar tonsils into the foramen magnum. 6 on the figure
- Most commonly caused by a posterior fossa mass lesion
- Compression of the brain stem leads to cardiopulmonary arrest.  

C. Subfalcine herniation involves displacement of the cingulate gyrus under the falx cerebri. 3 on the figure
Compression of the anterior cerebral artery leads to infarction.  

D. Uncal herniation involves displacement of the temporal lobe uncus under the tentorium cerebelli. 1 on the figure
- Compression of ipsilateral cranial nerve III (oculomotor) leads to the eye moving "down and out" and a dilated pupil.
- Compression of posterior cerebral artery leads to infarction of occipital lobe (contralateral homonymous hemianopsia).
- Cerebral peduncle compression which can be on the side of the lesion (contralateral paresis) or on the other side (ipsilateral paresis) leading to paresis
- Rupture of the paramedian artery leads to Duret ([[Brainstem]]) hemorrhage.

Transtentorial Herniation number 2 on the diagram
- Brain tissue crosses the lobes to the [[Cerebellum]]
- THalamus, hypothalamus and medial parts of both temporal lobes forced through tenotium cerebelli
- Somnolence, small reactive pupils that become non reactive and is usually fatal

| Type of herniation                | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Subfalcine                        | Displacement of the cingulate gyrus under the falx cerebri                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Central                           | Downwards displacement of the brain                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Transtentorial / uncal herniation | Displacement of the uncus of the temporal lobe under the tentorium cerebelli. Clinical consequences include an ipsilateral fixed, dilated pupil (due to parasympathetic compression of the third cranial nerve) and contralateral paralysis (due to compression of the cerebral peduncle) |
| Tonsillar                         | Displacement of the cerebellar tonsils through the foramen magnum. This is called 'coning'. In raised ICP this causes compression of the cardiorespiratory centre. In Chiari 1 malformation, tonsillar herniation is seen without raised ICP                                              |
| Transcalvarial                    | Occurs when brain is displaced through a defect in the skull (e.g. a fracture or craniotomy site)                                                                                                                                                                                         |
