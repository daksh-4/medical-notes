# Emphysema

Loss of the elastic recoil of alveoli. This means they can't empty very well. 
Balloons vs shopping bags. 

As you breathe out you shoot the air out of the alveoli. The speed of this air drags on the wall of the tube which tries to collapse the tube. 
Normally you have cartilage in the bronchus wall to keep the wall open. In the bronchioles, there are air sacs with elastic recoil that pull open the walls. 

If there is no more elastic recoil, the walls drag in and close which results in obstruction.

This occurs due to an imbalance of proteases and antiproteases
- Every time you breathe in, it is filtered by the lung and mucus. There are no defense mechanisms at the bottom of the lung. 
- The only defense is alveolar macrophages. 
	- When the macrophages eat particles that get through they cause a little bit of inflammation and make proteases which are damaging to the lung. As a result, the body makes antiproteases to stop this (e.g. alpha-1 antitrypsin). There is normally a balance between the two. 
- In emphysema there is either an overproduction of proteases or reduction in anti-proteases
- Most commonly, there is a lot of inflammation in the distal lungs caused by inflammation (due to the particulates in smoke) which leads to increased proteases
	- Results in centriacinar emphysema that is more severe in the upper lobe
	- Functional unit of the lung is the acinous which has a bronchiole, alveolus and blood vessel. The central part is more exposed to cigarette smoke 
	- Damages upper lobe more because the smoke rises
- Second group have alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency (rare).
	- Results in panacinar emphysema
	- Liver cirrhosis may also be present
		- This is because the liver is making a wonky alpha-1 antitrypsin and accumulates in the endoplasmic reticulum of the liver cells. 
		- ![[Pasted image 20240925142553.png]]Pink PAS positive globules
	- PiM is the normal allele
	- PiZ is most commonly clinically relevant mutation. 
	- Heterozygous are usually asymptomatic with decreased circulating levels of A1AT but if they smoke there is a very high risk of emphysema
	- Homozygous has a significant risk for panacinar emphysema and cirrhosis

Clinical features
- Dyspnoea and cough with minimal sputum
- Prolonged expiration with pursed lips - Pursed lips creates back pressure to hold the airway open 
- Weight loss
- Increased AP diameter of the chest ('barrel chest') 
	- Chest wall expands more because of loss of elastic recoil. This increases the FRC (functional residual capacity)
	- Fibrosis does the opposite because there is increased elastic recoil
- Late complications
	- Hypoxaemia 
		- Eventually the capillaries get destroyed too 
	- Cor pulmonale
		- Blood vessels constrict, leading to right ventricular failure
