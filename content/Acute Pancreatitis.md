# Acute Pancreatitis

Inflammation and hemorrhage of the pancreas

Due to autodigestion of pancreatic parenchyma by pancreatic enzymes

Premature activation of trypsin leads to activation of other pancreatic enzymes such as phospholipase, chymotrypsin and elastase. 
- Trypsin is secreted as an inactive trypsinogen
- Normally activated at brush border by enterokinase which then activates all other protein enzymes. 

Results in liquefactive hemorrhagic necrosis of the pancreas and fat necrosis of the peripancreatic fat (Fig. 11.1)

Most commonly due to alcohol and [[Gallstones]]; other causes include trauma, hypercalcemia, hyperlipidemia, drugs, scorpion stings, mumps, and rupture of a posterior duodenal ulcer.
- Alcohol causes contraction of the sphnicter of Oddi which decreases the drainage of the pancreas
- Gall stones block the ampulla 
- Trauma - automobile accidents in children due to the seatbelt compressing the abdominal structures
- Calcium is an activator of enzymes and can activate the pancreatic enzymes 
- Mumps can infect the pancreas
- Hypertriglyceridaemia - chylomicrons in the plasma can obstruct capillaries leading to ischaemia and vessel damage can expose triglycerides to pancreatic lipase which leads to production of free fatty acids.
- Rupture of a posterior duodenal ulcer can cause it because the head of the pancreas sits behind this region
- Drugs - GLP-1 agonists, sulfa drugs, 6 mercaptopurine
- Toxins - venom of some arachnids and reptiles - brown recluse spider, some scorpions and gila monster lizard

Clinical features
- Epigastric abdominal pain that radiates to the back
- Nausea and vomiting
- Periumbilical and flank hemorrhage (necrosis spreads into the periumbilical soft tissue and retroperitoneum)
- Elevated serum lipase and amylase; lipase is more specific for pancreatic damage. Because amylase could also be raised if there is damage to the [[Salivary Glands]]
- Hypocalcemia (calcium is consumed during saponification in fat necrosis)
- Rare features associated with pancreatitis include:  
	- ischaemic (Purtscher) retinopathy - may cause temporary or permanent blindness

Complications
- Shock-due to peripancreatic haemorrhage and fluid sequestration
-  Pancreatic pseudocyst-formed by fibrous tissue surrounding liquefactive necrosis and pancreatic enzymes
	- Presents as an abdominal mass with persistently elevated serum amylase
	- Rupture is associated with release of enzymes into the abdominal cavity and haemorrhage.
- Pancreatic abscess-often due to E coli; presents with abdominal pain, high fever and persistently elevated amylase
- Pancreatic necrosis 
- DIC
- ARDS
	- ![[Pasted image 20241105094316.png|500]] fluid leaking is due to a leaky vascular endothelium

Investigations:  

- serum amylase 
    - raised in 75% of patients - typically > 3 times the upper limit of normal
    - levels do not correlate with disease severity
    - specificity for pancreatitis is around 90%. Other causes of raised amylase include: pancreatic pseudocyst, mesenteric infarct, perforated viscus, acute cholecystitis, diabetic ketoacidosis
- serum lipase
    - more sensitive and specific than serum amylase
    - it also has a longer half-life than amylase and may be useful for late presentations > 24 hours
- imaging
    - a _diagnosis_ of acute pancreatits can be made without imaging if characteristic pain + amylase/lipase > 3 times normal level
    - however, early ultrasound imaging is important to assess the _aetiology_ as this may affect management - e.g. patients with [[Gallstones]]/biliary obstruction
    - other options include contrast-enhanced CT

### Scoring systems

The specifics of each scoring system will not be repeated here. However, some common factors indicating severe pancreatitis include:  

- age > 55 years
- hypocalcaemia
- hyperglycaemia
- hypoxia
- neutrophilia
- elevated LDH and AST

Popular mnemonic is **GET SMASHED**  

- **G**allstones
- **E**thanol
- **T**rauma
- **S**teroids
- **M**umps (other viruses include Coxsackie B)
- **A**utoimmune (e.g. polyarteritis nodosa), **A**scaris infection
- **S**corpion venom
- **H**ypertriglyceridaemia, **H**yperchylomicronaemia, **H**ypercalcaemia, **H**ypothermia
- **E**RCP
- **D**rugs (azathioprine, mesalazine*, didanosine, bendroflumethiazide, furosemide, pentamidine, steroids, sodium valproate)
