[[Endocrinology]]

Dietary fat contains triglycerides, cholesterol, and fat-soluble vitamins in the intestines. Bile salts emulsify fat. Pancreatic lipase breaks triglycerides into 3 fatty acids and 2 monoglycerides, which are absorbed in the enterocyte. Within the enterocyte, they're reassembled into triglycerides and packaged with cholesterol into chylomicrons. The major structural protein here is APOB48. Therefore, chylomicrons represent the dietary triglyceride transport. 

Chylomicrons enter the lymphatics and then the bloodstream. They pick up apo C2 and apo E from HDL. Apo C2 activates LPL, which hydrolyzes triglycerides into glycerol and free fatty acids. The free fatty acids enter tissues, and the leftover chylomicron remnant, which is now rich in cholesterol, returns to the liver by an apo E-mediated uptake. 

In the fed state, the liver converts excess glucose into fatty acids. These fatty acids are esterified into triglycerides and packed into VLDL with ApoB100. The VLDL is liver-made triglyceride, opposed to the chylomicrons, which are dietary triglycerides. VLDL also encounters LPL, and triglycerides get removed. The VLDL gradually loses triglyceride, turning into IDL and then LDL, which is very cholesterol rich. 

The LDL main job is to deliver cholesterol to cells that express LDL receptors, binding to ApoB100. LDL releases cholesterol into the cells, which is then used for cell membranes, steroid hormones, vitamin D, and bile acid production in the liver. Too much LDL promotes atherosclerosis. 

The HDL's main job is to reverse cholesterol transport, collecting excess cholesterol from peripheral tissues and macrophages and returning it to the liver. The key proteins are Apo A1 and LCAT, which is activated by Apo A1, which esterifies free cholesterol so it can be carried safely within HDL. 

In the fasting state, insulin falls, glucagon and adrenaline rise, which activate hormone-sensitive lipase in adipocytes. The stored triglycerides are broken down into glycerol and free fatty acids. Free fatty acids travel through the blood bound to albumin and are delivered to tissues that need energy.
