# HIV Genome and Gene Products

Three main genes, all translated as polyprotein precursors that get cleaved into functional pieces:

gag ("group-specific antigen") — structural proteins forming the physical architecture of the virion. Cleaved by protease into p17 (matrix protein, lines the inner surface of the viral envelope) and p24 (capsid protein, forms the conical shell encasing the RNA genome). p24 antigen is measured directly in early combination HIV tests, since it appears in blood before antibodies do.

pol — encodes three enzymes: reverse transcriptase (converts the RNA genome to DNA before integration), integrase (splices that DNA into the host genome), and protease (cleaves the gag and gag-pol polyproteins into their functional pieces, including p17/p24).

env — encodes the envelope glycoprotein precursor gp160, cleaved into gp120 (surface glycoprotein; binds CD4 as the primary receptor, plus a co-receptor — CCR5 for R5-tropic virus, dominant in early/chronic infection and required for macrophage/microglia infection, or CXCR4 for X4-tropic virus, which tends to emerge later and associates with faster CD4 decline) and gp41 (transmembrane protein; drives fusion of viral and host membranes once gp120 has engaged its receptors).

CD4 is expressed on CD4+ T-helper cells (the main site of depletion driving AIDS), monocytes/macrophages, dendritic cells, and microglia (resident CNS macrophages — the reservoir underlying HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder).

## Regulatory and accessory genes

- tat — boosts transcription of the integrated provirus by host RNA Pol II; binds the TAR RNA element on nascent transcripts and recruits the host elongation factor P-TEFb, preventing premature stalling.
- rev — exports unspliced/partially spliced viral mRNA (needed to encode gag, gag-pol, and env, or to serve as genomic RNA) out of the nucleus before host splicing surveillance retains or fully splices it, which would otherwise remove essential coding sequence.
- nef — downregulates CD4 and MHC class I on the infected cell surface, hiding it from cytotoxic T cells and preventing superinfection; enhances viral infectivity.
- vif — counteracts APOBEC3G, a host restriction factor that would otherwise hypermutate the viral genome during reverse transcription.
- vpr — helps import the pre-integration complex into the nucleus; arrests the host cell in G2 phase, favouring viral gene expression.
- vpu — degrades CD4 in the ER; antagonises tetherin, a host protein that would otherwise physically tether new virions to the infected cell surface.
