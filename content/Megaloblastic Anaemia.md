# Megaloblastic Anaemia

Folate comes into the body as THF and is quickly methylated. 
In order for THF to be involved in the synthesis of DNA precursors, it needs to lost the methyl group. It does this by giving it to vitamin B12. 
Vitamin B12 passes on the methyl group to homocysteine to make methionine. 
Methionine is used to transfer methyl groups to other molecules that need it. 

If there is a folate or vitamin B 12 deficiency, THF will not be participating in synthesis of DNA precursors. 

Fewer DNA precursors means one less division occurs in the erythrocytes, resulting in bigger red blood cells. This is normally due to an upset in the amount of DNA precursors available. 

Changes seen
- Megaloblastic anaemia
- Hypersegmented neutrophils (more than 5 lobes in the nucleus)
- Megaloblastic change in rapidly-dividing epithelial cells (e.g. in the intestine)

### Folate

Obtained from green vegetables and some fruits
Absorbed in the jejunum

Deficiency usually develops within months because there isn't a large storage of folatew

Causes 
- Poor diet (alcoholics and the elderly)
- Increased demand (pregnancy, cancer, haemolytic anaemia)
- Folate antagonists (methotrexate)

Clinical and lab findings
- Raised MCV >100 and hypersegmented neutrophils
- Glossitis (because cells of the tongue aren't turning over as regularly)
- Decreased serum folate
- Increased serum homocysteine. If there is no passing of a methyl group to Vitamin B12, there is no transfer of methyl group to homocysteine to convert it into methionine. 
- Normal methylmalonic acid (vitamin B 12 is required to convert this into succinyl coA. Normal because it is not a vitamin B12 deficiency). This is how you distinguish between folate and vitamin B12 deficiency. 

### Vitamin B12

Dietary vitamin B12 is complexed to animal-derived proteins. Once in the body it is cleaved from the proteins and bound to R binder (which is produced by the salivary gland). 

This complex travels to the small bowel, where it is cleaved from R binder by proteases made by the pancreas. 
V B12 now binds with intrinsic factor which is produced by the parietal cells in the stomach. 
This complex is then absorbed in the ileum (terminal). 

It takes years to form a deficiency of vitamin B12 because there is large hepatic stores of vitamin B12. 

Most common cause is pernicious anaemia 
- Autoimmune destruction of the parietal cells in the body of the stomach leading to intrinsic factor deficiency. 

3Ps rule - Parietal cells of the stomach have proton pump (they make the acid), pink (as opposed to blue chief cells), associated with pernicious anaemia 

Other causes of vitamin B12 deficiency
- Pancreatic insufficiency (pancreas makes the enzymes responsible for cleavage from R binder) 
- Damage to the terminal ileum due to Crohn disease or Diphyllobothrium latum (fish tapeworm)
- Dietary deficiency is rare, except in vegans. Vegan diets also tend to run high in folate but low in vitamin D and calcium alongside B12.

Clincal and lab findings are similar to folate deficiency
- [[Macrocytic Anaemia]] with hypersegmented neutrophils
- Glossitis
- [[Subacute Combined Degeneration]] OF SPINAL CORD
	- Vitamin B12 is also involved in the conversion of methylmalonic acid to succinyl coA. A build up of methylmalonic acid in the myelin of the spinal cord will cause degeneration of specific regions in the spinal cord. 
- Decreased serum B12
- Increased serum homocysteine. This is because no homocysteine --> methionine reaction 
- increased methylmalonic acid.
