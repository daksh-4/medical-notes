# Cell Injury
- Most common cause of Budd-Chiari Syndrome is Polycythaemia Vera

- IV methylene blue is the treatment for methaemoglobinaemia because Fe3+ is reduced back to Fe2+. This conditions is seen with oxidant stress for example due to sulfa and nitrate drugs or newborns because they don’t have the enzymes that convert Fe3+ to Fe2+

- Low ATP in cells also causes Cell Injury
    - Na+/K+ pump stops leading to a build up of sodium in the cell
    - Calcium pump stops and intracellular Ca2+ triggers enzymes
    - Aerobic glycolysis switches to anaerobic

- The main sign of reversible injury is cellular swelling however if this progresses to membrane damage then irreversible injury has taken place. Damaging of the mitochondrial membrane releases cytochrome C triggering apoptosis. Lysosome also releases enzymes
