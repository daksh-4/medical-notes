# Diabetic Ketoacidosis

Characterized by excessive serum ketones
- Insulin must be low
- Often arises with stress (e.g., infection); epinephrine stimulates glucagon and adrenaline secretion increasing lipolysis (along with gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis).
- Increased lipolysis leads to increased free fatty acids (FFAs).
- Liver converts FFAs to ketone bodies acid and acetoacetic acid). Low insulin leads to hyperglycaemia
- Results in hyperglycemia(> 300 mg/dL), anion gap metabolic acidosis, and hyperkalemia. Insulin normally drives potassium into cells and H+ moves into cells and bring K+ out to deal with the acidosis. Overall the body is low in potassium due to renal excretion
	- When you administer insulin you normally need to add potassium to the fluids to prevent hypokalaemia 
- Develop low phosphate due to polyuria
	- Muscle weakness leading to respiratory failure
	- Heart failure due to not enough ATP
- Presents with Kussmaul respirations (deep laboured breathing to breath off carbon dioxide, dehydration, nausea, vomiting, mental status changes, and fruity smelling breath (due to acetone)
- Treatment is fluids (corrects dehydration from polyuria), insulin, and replacement of electrolytes (e.g., potassium).
	- CONTINUE INSULIN UNTIL THE ACIDOSIS GOES AWAY 
	- Even if the glucose is low, just add dextrose
- Cerebral oedema can occur
- Mucormycosis
	- Fungal infection starts in sinuses and spread
	- Thrive in high glucose conditions
	- Fever, headache and eye pain
