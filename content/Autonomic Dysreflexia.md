# Autonomic Dysreflexia

1. Injury to spinal cord at C6 level (in this example)  
2. Stimulus (sometimes noxious - i.e. full bladder, distended bowel) BELOW T6 level sends stimulus up spinal cord, trying to get to the brain, to send parasympathetic signals down to release noxious stimulus (i.e. urination, defecation)  
3. Signal cannot get past the spinal cord lesion (C6), therefore you have uncontrolled sympathetic action below injury, causing raised BP, raised HR (initially), etc.  
4. Baroreceptors detect this raised BP and therefore try and lower it by slowing the HR down (reflex bradycardia).  
5. However, parasympathetic signals still cannot travel down spinal cord to go BELOW lesion, and sympathetic signals still cannot travel up spinal cord to go ABOVE lesion  
6. This results in sympathetic action below lesion (inc. vasoconstriction (=paleness), and parasympathetic action above lesion  
7. To try and counteract the rising BP, CNVII dilates blood vessels in face (=flushing). The combination of dilated vessels + severe hypertension = headache.  
  
Overall, you have FIGHT OR FLIGHT response BELOW lesion (due to initial noxious stimulus), and REST AND DIGEST response ABOVE lesion (as it attempts to counteract sympathetic fight or flight response)
