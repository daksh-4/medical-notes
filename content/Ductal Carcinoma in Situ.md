# Ductal Carcinoma in Situ

Histology - pleomorphic cells with prominent central necrosis that do not penetrate the basement membrane. It is the precursor to invasive ductal carcinoma 

Malignant proliferation of cells in ducts with no invasion of the basement membrane. You get dystrophic calcification around the dead cells in the centre.

Often detected as calcification on mammography; DCIS does not usually produce a mass.

Mammographic calcifications can also be associated with benign conditions such as fibrocystic changes (especially sclerosing adenosis) and fat necrosis.

Biopsy of calcifications is often necessary to distinguish between benign and malignant conditions.

C. Histologic subtypes are based on architecture; comedo type is characterized by high-grade cells with necrosis and dystrophic calcification in the center of ducts (Fig. 16.5).

Don't normally have any symptoms
