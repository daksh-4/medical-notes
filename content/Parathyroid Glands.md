# Parathyroid Glands

A. Chief cells regulate serum free (ionized) calcium via parathyroid hormone (PTH) secretion, which  increases bone osteoclast activity, releasing calcium and phosphate

 Increases small bowel absorption of calcium and phosphate (indirectly by activating vitamin D)
    
Increases renal calcium reabsorption (distal tubule) and decreases phosphate reabsorption (proximal tubule). In the proximal tubule 1 alpha hydroxylase converts vitamin D to active form

Vitamin D3 is first hydroxylated in the liver to 25-hydroxyvitamin D (calcidiol) — the storage/measured form — before the kidney's 1α-hydroxylase converts it to the active 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol).
