# Acute Gastritis

Two types
- Acidic damage to the stomach mucosa
- Due to imbalance between mucosal defenses and acidic environment

Defenses include mucin layer produced by foveolar cells (aka epithelial cells), bicarbonate secretion by surface epithelium, and normal blood supply (provides nutrients and picks up leaked acid).

Risk factors  
- Severe burn (Curling ulcer) - Hypovolemia leads to decreased blood supply.
- NSAIDs (decreased PGE2) - decrease blood flow to the mucosal barrier
- Heavy alcohol consumption - damage to the mucosa
- Chemotherapy - knocking out the cells in the stomach that are turning over in the mucosa. If you can't generate new cells then the risk is higher
 - Increased intracranial pressure (Cushing ulcer) - Increased stimulation of vagus nerve (more acetylcholine which binds to the parietal cell) leads to increased acid production.
 - Shock - Multiple (stress) ulcers may be seen in ICU patients.

 Acid damage results in superficial inflammation, erosion (loss of superficial epithelium), or ulcer (loss of mucosal layer).
