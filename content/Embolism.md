# Embolism

Intravascular mass that travels and occludes downstream vessels.
Most common type of embolus is a thromboembolus - thrombus that dislodges

Fat embolus
- Associated with bone fractures and soft tissue trauma
- Develops while fracture is still present 
- Presents with dyspnoea and petechiae on the skin overlying the blood

Gas embolus
- Seen in decompression sickness - nitrogen bubbles out due to rapid ascension.
- Presents with joint and muscle pain and resp symptoms
- Chronic form (Caisson disease) characterised by multifocal ischaemic necrosis of the bone. 
- Also could arise during laparoscopic surgery due to gas pumping

Amniotic fluid embolus
- Enters maternal circulation during labour or delivery 
- Presents with shortness of breath, neurologic symptoms and DIC (amniotic fluid loaded with tissue thromboplastin)
- Squamous cells and keratin debris from fetal skin in the embolus. 

Pulmonary embolus
- Usually due to thromboembolus
- Most common source is DVT of the lower extremity
- Normally clinically silent 
	- Lung has a dual blood supply
	- Embolus is usually small and self-resolving
- Complication - pulmonary infarction
	- Big embolus blocking large or medium sizes artery with pre-existing cardiopulmonary compromise
	- Only 10% of PEs cause infarct
	- SOB, haemoptysis, pleuritic chest pain and pleural effusion
	- Lower extremity doppler 
	- Elevated D Dimer
	- Haemorrhagic wedge infarct. 
	- Sudden death due to large saddle embolus or significant occlusion of a large artery
		- Death is due to electromechanical dissociation as the heart pumps against the embolus. 
- Pulmonary hypertension
	- Due to chronic emboli causing reorganisation

Systemic embolus 
- Usually due to thromboembolus 
- Most commonly arise in the left heart 
- Travel down the systemic circulation to occlude flow to organs most commonly the lower extremities
