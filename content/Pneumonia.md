# Pneumonia

Infection of the lung parenchyma 

Occurs when normal defenses are impaired

Damage to the mucociliary escalator can increase risk of pneumonia
- Viral pneumonia damaging the epithelial cells leading to superimposed bacterial pneumonia
- Mucus plugging

Clinical features
- Fever and chills 
- Cough with yellow green or rusty sputum
- Tachypnoea with pleuritic chest pain (sensitised by bradykinin and prostaglandin E2)
- Decreased breath sounds with dullness to percussion 
- Elevated WBC count

Diagnosis
- Chest x ray 
- Sputum gram stain and culture
- Blood cultures

Lobar pneumonia - consolidation of a whole lobe 
- Usually bacterial
- Most common causes are Strep pneumoniae and Klebsiella pneumoniae (affects malnourished patients who aspirate as Kleb is an entericl flora that is aspirated. Thick mucoid capsule results in currant jelly sputum and often complicated by abscess)
- ![[Pasted image 20240924164111.png|500]]
- 4 classic phases 
	- Congestion - increased blood flow and oedema
	- Red hepatisation - neutrophilic and bloody exudate resulting in solid appearance
	- Grey hepatisation - red blood cells broken down
	- Resolution - exudate resolved and healing begins. Type II pneumocytes start to regenerate the lining of the lung. 

Bronchopneumonia - patchy consolidation of a lobe
- Consolidation centred around bronchioles often multifocal and bilateral
- Caused by a variety of bacteria
- ![[Pasted image 20240924164623.png|500]]
- Causes 
	- staph aureus (most common cause of secondary pneumonia when you have viral first often complicated by abscess or empyema) 
	- H influensa (pneumonia superimposed on COPD)
	- Pseudomonas (cystic fibrosis), Moraxella catarrhalis (superimposed on COPD)
	- Legionella (immunocompromised, arises from water source, intracellular organism visualised by silver stain)

Interstitial pneumonia - no consolidation but inflammation within the outside of the airway. Increased lung markings. Usually non bacterial and probably viral
- Atypical pneumonia
- Diffuse interstitial infiltrates
- Presents with relatively mild upper respiratory symptoms, minimal sputum, cough and low fever
- ![[Pasted image 20240924165015.png|500]]
- Empty air sacks but lots of inflammatory cells in the walls of the interstitium
- Atypical bugs
	- Mycoplasma pneumonia most common cause, usually affects young aduls, Complications include autoimmune haemolytic anaemia IgM and erythema nodosum/multiforme
	- Chlamydia pneumoniae - second most common cause of atypical pneumonia in young adults
	- RSV most common cause in infants
	- CMV - posttransplant immunosuppresion
	- Influenza - elderly, immunocompromised and those with preexisting lung disease. Also increases risk for superimposed Staph aureus or H influenzae
	- Coxiella burnetti - high fever (Q fever), seen in farmers and vets because Coxiella spores are deposited on cattle by ticks or are present in cattle placentas. It is a rickettsial organimsm but distinct because it causes pneumonia and does not require arthropod vector for transmission (survives as highly heat-resistant endospores) and does not produce a skin rash

Aspiration pneumonia
- Alcoholics and comatose patients
- Anaerobic bacteria in the oropharynx - Bacteroides, Fusobacterium and Peptococcus
- Classically results in right lower lobe abscess
