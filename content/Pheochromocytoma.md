# Pheochromocytoma

Tumor of chromaffin cells (Fig. 15.15)

Clinical features are due to increased serum catecholamines. Can secrete adrenaline, norad or dopamine

It releases catecholamines in spurts so symptoms are episodic
- Episodic hypertension
- headache
- palpitations
- tachycardia
- sweating

PHEo - Palpitations, headache, episodic sweating

Diagnosed by increased serum metanephrines and increased 24-hour urine metanephrines and vanillylmandelic acid

Monoamine oxidase converts metanephrins to vanillylmandelic acid.

 MIBG - Metaiodobenzylguanidine

- ﻿﻿Chemical analog of norepinephrine
- ﻿﻿Diagnosis of pheochromocytoma & [[Neuroblastoma]]
- ﻿﻿Concentrated in sympathetic tissues
- ﻿﻿Labeled with radioactive iodine ([131)
- ﻿﻿Will concentrate in tumors → emit radiation
- ﻿﻿Special note: thyroid gland must be protected
	- ﻿﻿Simultaneous administration of potassium iodide
	- ﻿﻿Non-radioactive iodine
	- ﻿﻿Will be taken up by thyroid instead

Treatment is surgical excision.
Catecholamines may leak into the bloodstream upon manipulation of the tumour.
Phenoxybenzamine (irreversible a-blocker) is administered perioperatively to prevent a hypertensive crisis.

Often follows the 'rule of lOs:' 10% bilateral, 10% familial, 10% malignant, and 10% located outside of the adrenal medulla (e.g., bladder wall or organ of Zuckerkandl at the inferior mesenteric artery root)

Associated with MEN 2A and 2B, von Hippel-Lindau disease, and neurofibromatosis type 1
