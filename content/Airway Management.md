# Airway Management

|Device|Notes|
|---|---|
|**Oropharyngeal airway**|- Easy to insert and use<br>- No paralysis required<br>- Ideal for very short procedures<br>- Most often used as a bridge to more definitive airway|
|**Laryngeal mask**|- Widely used<br>- Very easy to insert<br>- Device sits in pharynx and aligns to cover the airway<br>- Poor control against reflux of gastric contents<br>- Paralysis is not usually required<br>- Commonly used for wide range of anaesthetic uses, especially in day surgery<br>- Not suitable for high-pressure ventilation (small amount of PEEP often possible)|
|**Tracheostomy**|- Reduces the work of breathing (and dead space)<br>- May be useful in slow weaning<br>- Percutaneous tracheostomy widely used in ITU<br>- Dries secretions, humidified air usually required|
|**Endotracheal tube**|- Provides optimal control of the airway once the cuff inflated<br>- May be used for long or short-term ventilation<br>- Errors in insertion may result in oesophageal intubation<br>    - accounts for around 5% of major airway complications<br>    - therefore monitor end-tidal CO2 (capnography)<br>- Paralysis often required<br>- Higher ventilation pressures can be used|
