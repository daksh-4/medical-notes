# Chronic Inflammation

- T cells made in the bone marrow as progenitor T cells and T cell receptor undergoes rearrangement in the thymus. Progenitor cells become CD4+ T cells or CD8+ T cells
    
- CD4+ T cells recognise MHC class II and CD8+ T cells recognise MHC class 1
    
- CD4+ T cells require 2 signals for activation
    
    - MHC class II binds to the T cell receptor
    - B7 on the antigen presenting cell binds CD28 on CD4+ T cells providing a 2nd activation signal.

- Once active CD4+ T cells secrete cytokines which can help B cells or CD8+ T cells
    - Th1 - IL2 (T-cell growth factor and CD8+ T cell activator) and IFN gamma (macrophage activator)
    - Th2 - Helps the B cells by producing IL4 (class switching to IgG and IgE) and IL5 (eosinophil chemotaxis and activation, maturation of B cells to plasma cells and class switching to IgA). Also IL10 which inhibits the Th1 phenotype (macrophages produce IL10 in acute inflammation to shut down the inflammatory response)

- CD8+ T cell activation requires MHC class 1 binding, IL2 from Th1 cell providing the 2nd signal.
    - Secretion of performs and granzyme induce apoptosis of the target cell
    - Expression of FasL, binds Fas on the target cell activating apoptosis

- B cell activation happens via antigen binding by surface IgM or IgD
    - Can also happen by binding to antigen presentation on CD4+ helper T cells via MHC 2
    - CD40 receptor on B cell binds the CD40L on helper T cells providing the 2nd activation signal. This also activates the type 2 helper T cell, secreting IL4 and IL5.

- Granulomatous inflammation is a subtype of chronic inflammation
    
    - The key cell that characterises a granuloma is an epithelia histiocyte (macrophages with abundant pink cytoplasm). They are surrounded by giant cells and a rim of lymphocytes. Granulomas can be divided into caseating and non-caseating subtypes.
        
        - Non caseating granulomas lack central necrosis - reaction to foreign material, sarcoidosis, beryllium exposure, Crohn disease, cat scratch disease
	        ![[Pasted image 20240826220052.png]]

        - Caseating granuloma - key differentials are TB and fungal infections. AFB stain tests for TB and GMS stain for fungal infections.
	        ![[Pasted image 20240826220106.png]]

    - Steps involved in granuloma formation
        
        - Macrophages present antigen via MHC 2 to CD4+ helper T cells
        - Macrophages secrete IL-12 inducing CD4+ helper cells to differentiate into the Th1 subtype
        - Th1 cells secrete IFN gamma which converts macrophages to epithelia histiocytes and giant cells.
