# Hernias

In the femoral canal, vein is most midline, then artery and nerve most lateral. 

Femoral triangle - superior inguinal ligament, medial adductor longus, lateral sartorius

Nerve is not contained within the femoral sheath

### Spermatic Cord

Ductus deferens, arteries, veins, nerves

Three fascial layers
- External spermatic rascia
- Cremasteric fascia 
- Internal spermatic fascia

### Inguinal Canal

Carries spermatic cord in males and round ligament in females

### Inguinal Hernias

#### Indirect Inguinal Hernia

- ﻿﻿"Indirectly" through abdominal wall
	- ﻿﻿Travel through inguinal canal
	- ﻿﻿Not "directly" through a hole
- ﻿﻿Origin lateral to epigastric vessels
- ﻿﻿Follows path of descent of testes
	- ﻿﻿Covered by all layers of spermatic fascia
	- ﻿﻿Contrast with direct hernias (outer layer only)
- ﻿﻿Congenital defect
	- ﻿﻿Bowel protrudes through patent processus vaginalis
	- ﻿﻿Should close after descent of testes
- Most common type of inguinal hernia, typically occurring on the right side

Herniates through the internal and external inguinal rings and is covered by all three layers of the spermatic fascia

#### Direct Inguinal Hernia

- Bowel bulges directly through Hesselbach's triangle in the abdominal wall. 
- Origin is medial to the epigastric vessels 
- Travels through the external ring and is covered by external spermatic fascia only
- Should never bulge into the scrotum 

Borders of Hesselbach's triangle - inguinal ligament, inferior epigastric, rectus abdominis, floor is the transversalis fascia

![[Pasted image 20250627220157.png]]

Caused by transversalis fascia breakdown resulting in weakness in the floor of the inguinal canal

### Processus Vaginalis

Testes descend behind processus vaginalis which is an outpouching of the peritoneum. Remains open in the newborn period and should close in infancy and get replaced by fibrous tissue

Part remains as tunica vaginalis testis which is a serous covering of the testes. 

### Femoral Hernias

- ﻿﻿Hernia through femoral ring
	- ﻿﻿Medial to femoral vessels
- ﻿﻿Bowel protrudes below inguinal ligament
	- ﻿﻿Differentiates from both types of inguinal hernias
- ﻿﻿More common in women than men
	- ﻿﻿But indirect inguinal most common type for both genders
- ﻿﻿High risk of incarceration
	- ﻿﻿Femoral ring is small opening

### Hiatal Hernias

Type I: Sliding hiatal hernia (95%)

- ﻿﻿Displacement of GE junction above diaphragm
- ﻿﻿Stomach in usual alignment
- ﻿﻿Fundus remains below GE junction
- ﻿﻿"Hourglass" appearance
- ﻿﻿Herniation through hiatus

﻿﻿Types II, I, IV: Paraesophageal
﻿﻿
- ﻿﻿GE junction in normal location
- ﻿﻿Protrusion of stomach fundus
- ﻿﻿Defect in the "phrenoesophageal membrane"
- ﻿﻿Bowel sounds in lung fields is classic finding

### Congenital Diaphragmatic Hernia

- Developmental defect of diaphragm
	- ﻿﻿Defective formation pleuroperitoneal membrane
	- ﻿﻿Hole in diaphragm
- ﻿﻿Abdominal organs herniate into chest
- ﻿﻿In utero herniation -> pulmonary hypoplasia
- Often fatal
