# Iron Deficiency Anaemia

Most common type of anaemia. Approximately one third of the world is deficiency is iron

Consumed in haeme (meat derived) and non-haeme (vegetable). Meat form is more readily absorbed. The body can't get rid of iron (except a little bit in skin sloughing off or through the menstrual cycle). 

Iron has the ability to form free radicals so it is always bound to proteins. 

- Enterocytes transport iron in to the blood via ferroportin
- Transferrin transports iron and delivers it to liver and bone marrow storage 
- Transferrin transports iron and delivers it to liver and bone marrow macrophages for storage.
- Stored intracellular iron is bound to ferritin. 

TIBC - total iron binding capacity tells you how many transferrin molecules are present in the blood. 

Deficiency can by caused by dietary lack or blood loss
- Infants - breast feeding
- Children - poor diet
- Adults - [[Peptic Ulcer Disease]] (males), menorrhagia or pregnancy (females)
- Elderly - colon polyps/carcinoma (Western world), hookworm - necator and ancylostoma (developing world)

Other causes - malnutrition, malabsorption, gastrectomy (Fe2+ is more easily absorbed by the body and acid maintains the Fe2+ state so gastrectomy reduces the bioavailability)

Stages of iron deficiency
1. Storage iron is depleted
2. Serum iron is depleted
3. Normocytic anaemia 
4. Microcytic, hypochromic anaemia

Serum ferritin is the first to decrease and TIBC increases. 
Serum iron will go down and saturation will go down next. 

THE FIRST STAGE OF IRON DEFICIENCY ANAEMIA IS A NORMOCYTIC ANAEMIA. 

Clincal features
- Anaemia
- Koilonychia
- Pica (chew on dirt and ice)

Lab findings
- Microcytic, hypochromic anaemia with increased red blood cell distribution width (what is the spread of red blood cells)
- Decreased ferritin and increased TIBC
- Decreased serum iron and decreased saturation
- Increased free erythrocyte protoporphyrin (if there isn't enough iron to bind to protoporphyrin to make haeme then it is free in the blood)

Red blood cells should be the size of the nucleus of a lymphocyte

- Ferrous sulphate
