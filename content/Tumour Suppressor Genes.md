# Tumour Suppressor Genes

Decrease the risk of tumour formation, p53 and Rb (retinoblastoma) are common examples

## p53
- p53 regulates the G1 to S phase. 
	- If p53 looks at the DNA and sees that the DNA is wrong, it will call DNA repair enzymes. 
	- If the DNA is too mutated, it calls in BAX, which destroys Bcl2. 
		- Bcl2 is the molecule that stabilises the mitochondrial membrane so cytochrome c doesn't leak out. 
			- Cytochrome c activates caspases and causes apoptosis
- Both copies of p53 must be knocked out for tumour formation. Loss is seen in >50% of cancers. This is known as Knudson's two hit hypothesis (both alleles need to be inactivated).
- Germline mutation results in Li-Fraumeni syndrome; increased risk for multiple types of carcinomas and sarcomas. \

## Rb
- Also regulates progression from G1 to S phase. 
	- One of the molecules needed to go from G1 to S phase is E2F which is bound to Rb. 
		- When Rb gets phosphorylated, it releases E2F, allowing it to move from G to S phase
		- Rb gets phosphorylated by cyclinD/CDK4 complex. 
- Rb mutation results in consitutively free E2F, allowing free movement through the cell cycle
- Both copies of Rb must be knocked out for tumour formatiom. 
	- Sporadic mutation - unilateral retinoblastoma 
	- Germline mutation - bilateral retinoblastoma, osteosarcoma

Retinoblastoma
- It is a tumour that forms in the back of the eye. 

## Regulators of Apoptosis
- Prevent apoptosis in normal cells 
- Promote apoptosis in mutated cells whose DNA cannot be repaired

## Bcl2
- Maintains mitochondrial membrane, preventing the release of cytochrome c
- Bcl2 is overexpressed in follicular lymphoma
	- t(14;18) moves Bcl2 (on chromosome 18) to Ig heavy chain locus (chromosome 14); resulting in increased Bcl2
	- In the follicle, B cells are normally undergoing somatic hypermutation to increase the diversity of the antibodies being produced by the B cell. But if the mutations fail, you want the cells to die via apoptosis. 
	- If the B cells don't die, they pile up and form a follicular lymphoma. 

## Telomerase
Necessary enzyme for cell immortality. 
- Normally telomeres shorten with serial cell division resulting in senescence (process of deterioration with age)
- Cancers have upregulated telomerase which preserves telomeres. 

## Angiogenesis
Fibroblast growth factor and vascular endothelial growth factor are commonly produced by cancer cells. 

Tumour cells can evade immune surveillance by downregulating expression of MHC 1. 
Immunodeficiency (primary and secondary) increases the risk for cancer.
