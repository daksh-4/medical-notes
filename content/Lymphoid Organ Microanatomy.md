# Lymphoid Organ Microanatomy

Thymus:
- Cortex — dense, immature T cells undergoing positive selection
- Medulla — mature T cells, negative selection, Hassall's corpuscles

Lymph node:
- Follicles (cortex) — B cells; germinal centers form on activation
- Paracortex — T cells; this is the zone that's underdeveloped in DiGeorge syndrome
- Medulla — plasma cells (medullary cords) and macrophages (medullary sinuses)

Spleen:
- White pulp — PALS (periarteriolar lymphatic sheath) around the central artery (T cells) plus follicles (B cells)
- Marginal zone — macrophages/APCs filtering blood-borne antigens; first line against encapsulated organisms
- Red pulp — macrophages culling old/damaged RBCs and encapsulated bacteria; this is why splenectomy raises the risk of overwhelming infection with encapsulated organisms

See [[Lymphadenopathy]], [[Primary Immunodeficiencies]], [[Hairy Cell Leukaemia]] (red pulp involvement).
