# Autonomic Nervous System

Acetylcholine
- Binds to "cholinergic" receptors
- Two types: nicotinic and muscarinic
- Many subtypes: M1 to M5

Norepinephrine (noradrenaline)
- ﻿﻿Binds to "adrenergic" receptors
- ﻿﻿Two types: alpha and beta
- ﻿﻿Many subtypes: alpha 1, alpha 2, beta 1, beta 2, beta 3

![[Pasted image 20241105120539.png|500]]
Parasympathetic nerves come from the medulla and the sacrum

Sympathetic ganglia are paravertebral T1-L3

Parasympathetic ganglia are near target organs

All gangilionic synapses use acetylcholine and all ganglionic receptors are nicotinic. 

Major consequence of nicotine modulation is fasciculations or paralysis. 

Parasympathetic nervous system
- ﻿﻿Major effector neurotransmitter: acetylcholine
- ﻿﻿Receptors: Muscarinic
- ﻿﻿Muscarinic subtypes: M1, M2, M3, M4, M5 
- M2: Heart
- ﻿﻿M3: Most other locations
	- ﻿﻿Blood vessels
	- ﻿﻿Lungs
	- ﻿﻿Salivary glands

Sympathetic nervous system
- ﻿﻿Major effector neurotransmitter: norepinephrine, but not all the time. 
- ﻿﻿Binds adrenergic receptors
- ﻿﻿Alpha receptors: a1, a2
- ﻿﻿Beta receptors: B1, B2, B3
- ﻿﻿Exceptions: adrenal glands and sweat glands
	- Adrenal gland uses acetylcholine and receptor is nicotinic (neuronal subtype). Adrenal medulla releases 80% adrenaline, 20% noradrenaline. Amplifies sympathetic response. 
	- Sweat glands use acetylcholine and muscarinic M3 receptors

Dopamine
- ﻿﻿Minor SNS neurotransmitter
- ﻿﻿Released onto renal vasculature
- ﻿﻿Vasodilates blood vessels
- ﻿﻿Only at low dosages
- ﻿﻿High dosages activate alpha/beta receptors
	- ﻿﻿Used to treat shock

Smooth Muscle

- ﻿﻿Major target of autonomic nervous system
	- ﻿﻿Arterioles, bronchioles, GI tract
Sympathetic often contracts and relaxes
- ﻿﻿Depends on tissue beds
- ﻿﻿Different receptors in different tissue beds
- ﻿﻿Direct effect of norepinephrine on smooth muscle

Alpha 1 receptors lead to contraction of smooth muscle
- Found in vascular smooth muscle
- Vasoconstriction to raise bp
- Skin 
- GI tract

Beta 2 lead to smooth muscle relaxation
- Blood vessels of muscle and liver tissue because you want more blood flow to these regions during fight or flight
- Lungs - bronchodilation

Parasympathetic causes smooth muscle contraction and relaxation depending on the region
- Lungs - bronchoconstriction
- Bladder - detrusor

Relaxation
- GI peristalsis
- Penile erection
- Indirect effect of acetylcholine - triggers the release of NO
	- NO also stimulated by bradykinin, serotonin, shear forces
	- Acetylcholine can't cause relaxation without the presence of an intact endothelium to make the NO. 

Skin

- ﻿﻿Largest organ in the body
- ﻿﻿Under sympathetic control
- ﻿﻿No major parasympathetic effects
SNS activation → vasoconstriction
- ﻿﻿a1 receptors
- ﻿﻿Saves blood for vital organs (brain, heart)
- ﻿﻿Skin becomes cool/ cold to touch
Other SNS effects
- ﻿﻿Sweat ("cold sweat")
- ﻿﻿Arrector pili muscles ("goosebumps")

Lungs

Sympathetic
- Bronchodilation via beta 2 receptors 
- Respond much more to adrenaline than noradrenaline

Heart 

Sympathetic - B1
Parasympathetic M2 via vagus nerve

Kidney 
Sympathetic activation
- B1 receptors - renin release
- Constricts afferent/efferent arterioles via alpha receptors
- Decreases GFR to limit sodium/water excretion
Net result is increased sodium and water retention

Gastrointestinal Tract

Sympathetic nervous system (a1))
- ﻿﻿Decreases GI blood flow (vasoconstriction)
- ﻿﻿Slows transit/motility (constriction of sphincters)
Parasympathetic system → increases motility  
- Relaxation of sphincters
Anticholinergic drugs: constipation
Cholinergic excess: diarrhoea (organophosphates)

Salivary Glands

Saliva production increased by SNS and PNS
- ﻿﻿SNS: thick saliva
- ﻿﻿PNS: watery saliva

Parasympathetic control dominates
- ﻿﻿Activated by food smell, sight, etc.
- ﻿﻿Muscarinic receptors (M3)
- ﻿﻿Anticholinergic drugs: dry mouth

Blood Vessels

Arterioles
- ﻿﻿Vessels of skin, GI tract, kidneys
- ﻿﻿Determine overall resistance of vascular system
- ﻿﻿Control systemic vascular resistance (SVR)
- ﻿﻿Contract via a1 stimulation

Veins
- ﻿﻿Constriction sends blood to heart
- ﻿﻿Increases preload
- ﻿﻿Mediated via a1 stimulation

Eye

Pupil diameter
- ﻿﻿SNS (a1) → increases (dilates; mydriasis)
- ﻿﻿PNS (M3) → decreases (constricts; [[Miosis]])

Accommodation (altered lens shape)
- ﻿﻿Ciliary muscle contraction
- ﻿﻿Mostly under parasympathetic control
- ﻿﻿PNS (M3) → constriction for near vision
﻿﻿Overall SNS effect: more light, far vision

Aqueous Humour Production
- Fluid allows eye to focus during fight/flight
- B2 -> produces fluid 
- parasympathetic -> constricts ciliary muscle to drain fluid 

Uterus 

Has smooth muscle so is under innervation 
B2 receptors
- Smooth muscle relaxation
- Terbutaline is a beta 2 agonist used in preterm labour
Alpha receptors
- Uterine contraction at delivery

Bladder

Three muscles
- Detrusor - smooth
- Internal urethral sphincter - smooth
- External urethral sphincter - skeletal

Bladder filling
- Sympathetic control dominates
- Detrusor relaxation via B3
- Internal sphincter contraction via a1
- External sphincter contraction voluntary

Micturition
- Parasympathetic control dominates
- Detrusor contraction via M3
- Internal relaxation via M3
- External voluntary

Metabolism

Direct innvervation of adipose tissue via B1, 2, 3 

Adrenaline
- Opposes effects of insulin
- inhibits insulin release via pancreatic alpha 2
- down glycogen synthesis and up glycogen breakdown via hepatic B2
- down storage of fatty acids in adipose tissue
