# Cardiac Tamponade

- ﻿﻿Accumulation of pericardial fluid
- ﻿﻿High pericardial pressure
- ﻿﻿Filling restriction of cardiac chambers
- ﻿﻿Amount of fluid variable
	- ﻿﻿Acute accumulation (bleeding): small amount of fluid
	- ﻿﻿Chronic accumulation (cancer): large amount of fluid
	- This is because, given time the pericardium can stretch to accommodate the excess fluid

Causes
- Cancer mets 
- Uraemia 
- Pericarditis
- Trauma

Clinical features
- Distant heart sounds
- Dyspnoea 
- Elevated JVP

Beck's triad
- Distant heart sounds
- Elevated JVP 
- Hypotension
- Seen in rapidly developing traumatic effusions leading to low cardiac output from LV function impairment

Pulsus paradoxus
- Classic finding
- Systolic BP always falls slightly on inspiration but in tamponade there is an exaggerated fall greater than 10 mmHg
- In a severe fall in bp the pulse can disappear
- ![[Pasted image 20241213152548.png|500]]
- Can also be seen in asthma and COPD
	- Due to pulmonary pressure fluctuations leading to a large drop in left sided flow

Equalisation of pressures
- Occurs when the cardiac chambers cannot relax
- Pressures fall but then abruptly stop
- Also seen in tamponade and pericardial effusion
