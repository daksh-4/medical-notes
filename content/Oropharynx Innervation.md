# Oropharynx Innervation

General sensation of the oropharynx (posterior pharyngeal wall, tonsillar area, posterior third of tongue) is carried by the glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX) — this is the afferent limb of the gag reflex.

The efferent/motor limb of the gag reflex is the vagus nerve (CN X), via the pharyngeal plexus, producing bilateral pharyngeal muscle contraction.

Pharyngeal arch–cranial nerve mapping: arch 1 = trigeminal (V), arch 2 = facial (VII), arch 3 = glossopharyngeal (IX), arch 4/6 = vagus (X, superior/recurrent laryngeal branches).

Exceptions to "vagus supplies all pharyngeal muscle motor function": stylopharyngeus is glossopharyngeal (its own arch 3 muscle); tensor veli palatini is trigeminal V3 (an arch 1 muscle).

Anterior two-thirds of the tongue: lingual nerve (trigeminal V3) carries general sensation; chorda tympani (facial nerve branch, runs through the middle ear before joining the lingual nerve sheath) carries taste, plus secretomotor fibres to the submandibular/sublingual glands.

Posterior third of the tongue: glossopharyngeal (CN IX) alone carries both general sensation and taste — no split, unlike the anterior two-thirds.
