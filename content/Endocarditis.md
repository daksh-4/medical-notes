# Endocarditis

Endocardium - Innermost layer of the heart that lines the surface of the cardiac valves. 

Usually due to bacterial pathogens
- Staph aureus - high virulence organism infecting normal valves (tricuspid classically) results in large vegetations that destroy the valve (acute endocarditis)
	- Catalase positive, coagulase positive
- Strep viridans - low-virulence organism can only infect previously damaged valves. Results in small vegetations that do not destroy the valve.
	- Damaged endocardial surface expresses tissue factor and collagen underneath it, leading to the development of thrombotic vegetations (platelets and fibrin). Dextran made by the bacteria adheres to fibrin
	- Transient bacteraemia leads to trapping of bacteria in vegetations 
	- Classic predisposing condition is mitral valve prolapse
	- Catalase negative 
	- Normal mouth flora - can occur after a dental procedure
- Staph endocarditis
	- Prosthetic valves
- Strep bovis
	- Endocarditis in patients with underlying colorectal carcinoma 
	- Strongest subtype association is subtype 1 - strep gallolyticus
- Enterococcus 
	- Normal gut bacteria 
	- Subacute endocarditis course which commonly occurs in older men
	- Associated with manipulation of the GI tract 
		- Abdominal surgery
		- Urinary catheter
		- TURP for treatment of benign prostatic hyperplasia

Negative blood culture endocarditis HACEK
- Coxiella Burnetti 
	- Obligate intracellular bacteria
	- causes abortions in farm animals
	- Humans inhale aerosolised bacteria leading to Q fever which may present as pneumonia. In half of cases there are no symptoms
		- They can go on to develop chronic Q fever which commonly manifests as endocarditis
- Bartonella quintana
	- Lice transmission
	- Patients with poor hygiene
- Bartonella henselae
	- Found in cats - leads to cat scratch fever 
- Haemophilus, actinobacillus, cardiobacterium, eikenella, kingella

Clinical features
- Fever, chills, sweats
- Murmur - due to vegetations on the valve
- Janeway lesions - erythematous non tender lesions on the palms and soles
- Osler nodes - painful on fingers and toes
- Roth spots - red lesion with a pale centre 
- Splinter haemorrhages
- Petechiae - small spots
- [[Anaemia of Chronic Disease]] - chronic inflammation 

Embolic symptoms
- Stroke
- Paralysis
- Blindness 
- Limb ischaemia
- Splenic or renal infarction
- Pulmonary embolism if vegetations are on tricuspid 
- Acute myocardial infarction if coronary artery get clotted

Lab findings
- Positive blood cultures
- [[Anaemia of Chronic Disease]] - hepcidin release by the liver ferritin high, TIBC low, serum iron decreases 
- TEE is useful for detecting lesions on valves 

Nonbacterial thrombotic endocarditis
- Sterile vegetations that arise with hypercoagulable state or underlying adenocarcinoma
- Vegetations arise on the mitral valve along lines of closure and result in regurgitation

Libman-Sacks endocarditis
- Sterile vegetations associated with lupus. Vegetations are on both sides of the valve. Most commonly affecting the mitral valve
- Mitral regurgitation
- Also seen in other hypercoagulable states, specifically cancer
- Often asymptomatic and rarely causes murmurs but embolises very easily 

Complications
Abscess beneath valve annulus
- Persistent fever, bacteraemia often indicates abscess
- Aortic valve abscess can lead to heart block due to the close proximity to the AV node
