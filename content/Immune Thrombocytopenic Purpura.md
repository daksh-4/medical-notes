# Immune Thrombocytopenic Purpura

Clinical features
- brusing
- petechial or purpuric rash
- bleeding is less common and typically presents as epistaxis or gingival bleeding

ITP
- Autoimmune production of IgG against platelet antigens (GPIIb/IIIa)
- Most common cause of thrombocytopenia in children and adults. 
- Autoantibodies are produced by plasma cells in the spleen
	- Antibody-bound platelets are consumed by splenic macrophages, resulting in thrombocytopenia. 
- Acute form arises in children - weeks after viral infection or immunisation
	- Self-limited usually resolving within weeks of presentation. 
- Chronic form - usually seen in women of child bearing age
	- May be primary or secondary (associated with [[SLE]]). Patients with lupus normally have antibodies against something in the blood. 
	- May cause short-lived thrombocytopenia in offspring; anti-platelet IgG can cross the placenta. 

Lab finding
- Decreased platelet count often below 50 
- Normal PT/PTT 
- increased megakaryocytes on the bone marrow biopsy (because bone marrow is responding to try to make more platelets)
- ==bone marrow examinations is only required if there are atypical features ==e.g.
    - lymph node enlargement/splenomegaly, high/low white cells
    - failure to resolve/respond to treatment

Management
- Usually no treatment is required
- Initial treatment is corticosteroids
	- Works well for kids, not so much for adults they can relapse
- IVIG can raise platelet count in symptomatic bleeding. 
	- Splenic macrophages start eating this immunoglobulin instead of the platelet bound immunoglobulin. 
- Last stage treatment is splenectomy
	- This eliminates the primary source of antibody and the site of destruction, only performed in refractory cases.
