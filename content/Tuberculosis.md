# Tuberculosis

![[Pasted image 20260908121115.png|600]]

Inhalation of aerosolised Mycobacterium tuberculosis

Primary TB arises with initial exposure
- results in focal caseating necrosis in the lower lobe of the lung and hilar lymph nodes
- Foci undergo fibrosis and calcification forming Ghon complex - subpleural nodule
- Primary TB is generally asymptomatic but leads to a positive PPD

Secondary TB arises with reactivation of TB
- Reactivation of Ghon complex
- Commonly due to AIDS may also be seen with aging
- Occurs at the apex of the lung because the oxygen is highest there
- Forms cavitary foci of caseous necrosis may also lead to miliary pulmonary TB or tuberculous bronchopneumonia

Clinical features
- Fevers and night sweats
- Cough with haemoptysis
- Weight loss
- Biopsy reveals caseating granulomas; AFB stain reveals red acid-fast bacilli
- ![[Pasted image 20240924165912.png|600]] 
- Caseating necrosis with epitheloid histiocyts

Systemic spread often occurs
- Can involve any tissue
- Meningitis with granulomas at the base of the brain 
- Cervical lymph nodes
- Kidney (most common) sterile pyuria
- Lumbar vertebrae (Pott disease)

Treatment
- Rifampicin acts by blocking DNA-dependent RNA polymerase
