# Haemostasis

Platelet plug, plug stabilisation (mediated by the coagulation cascade)

### Primary Haemostasis 

1. Transient vasoconstriction of the damaged vessels. "Knee jerk reaction". 
	- This is mediated by neural reflexes and endothelin released by endothelial cells
2. vWf will come in and line the surface at the level of the sub endothelial collagen
3. vWf acts as a linker molecule with GP1b to bind vWf resulting in platelet adhesion. 
	- Comes from the Weibel Palade bodies in the endothelial cells and alpha granules of platelets. WP bodies are viewable by microscopy and contains a few things important for endothelial function - P selectin (selectin speed bumps in inflammation) and vWf
4. Platelets get activated via adhesion, undergo shape change allowing dumping of mediators (degranulation)
	- ADP and thromboxane A2 (derivative of platelet cyclooxygenase). ADP induces the platelets to express GP2b/3a receptors (for platelet aggregation). TXA2 is the signal for further platelet aggregation. 
5. Platelet aggregation at site of injury via GpIIb/IIIa using fibrinogen as a linking molecule. This results in the formation of platelet plug. 

### Disorders of primary haemostasis

Can be quantitative or qualitative (not enough platelets vs not good enough platelets)

Clinical features of mucosal and skin bleeding
- Epistaxis, haemoptysis, GI bleeding, haematuria and menorrhagia. 
- Intracranial bleeding occurs with severe thrombocytopenia. 
- Petechiae (pinpoint bleeding), purpura (3mm), ecchymoses (1cm)
- Easy bruising
- Petechiae are a sign of thrombocytopenia and are not usually seen with qualitative disorders. 

Useful lab studies
- Platelet count
- Bleeding time
- Blood smear
- Bone marrow biopsy (to check the megakaryocytes that make platelets)

### Secondary Haemostasis

Stabilises the weak platelet plug using the coagulation cascade. The end product of the coagulation cascade is thrombin which converts fibrinogen to fibrin, which can be cross linked which cross links to strengthen the clot. 

1. Factors of the coagulation cascade are produced in the liver in an inactive state
	- Activation of factors requires exposure to an activating substance, phospholipid surface and calcium. 
2. Sub endothelial collagen activates factor 12 
3. Tissue thromboplastin activates factor 7.
4. Factor 10, 5, phospholipid and calcium converts prothrombin to thrombin

### Clinical Features of Disorders related to Secondary Haemostasis

Deep tissue bleeding into muscles and joints
Rebleeding after surgical procedures. 

### Lab studies

PT - measures extrinsic and common pathways
PTT - measures intrinsic and common pathways. Used for assessing heparin activity
