# Gastrointestinal Anatomy

Intraperitoneal
- Covered by mesentery

Retroperitoneal
- Not completely wrapped in peritoneum
- SAD PUCKER
- Suprarenal glands
- Aorta and IVC - if rupture, blood will collect in the retroperitoneal space
- Duodenum
- Pancreas - head and body
- Ureters
- Colon (Ascending and descending)
- Kidneys
- Esophagus
- Rectum

### Greater and Lesser Sac

Peritoneal cavity divided into greater/lesser sac

Greater sac 
- entire width of abdomen
- diaphragm to pelvic floor

Lesser sac
- closed space behind the liver, stomach and lesser omentum

Epiploic foramen is the opening between the greater and lesser sacs

Pectinate line
- above is columnar epithelium
- below is stratified squamous epithelium

### Blood Supply

Celiac trunk - left gastric, common hepatic, splenic are main branches

Gastric ulcer - left gastric artery from the lesser curvature

Posterior duodenal ulcers - gastroduodenal artery  

Short gastric arteries - branches of the splenic artery which supply the fundus and upper cardiac portions, vulnerable to ischeamia because no anastomoses. 

Hepatoduodenal ligament
- Contains proper hepatic artery, portal vein and common bile duct
- Pringle's maneuver
	- Clamping to control liver bleeding 

Superior mesenteric artery - main branches ileocolic, right colic, middle colic, ileal and jejunal arteries

SMA syndrome
- SMA courses over the distal 1/3 of the duodenum
- Mesenteric fat keeps the SMA away from the duodenum 
- If pressed downwards -> obstruction
- Classic patient - recent weight loss 
	- Fat pad shrinks

Inferior Mesenteric Artery - main branches left colic and sigmoid arteries

Collateral blood supplies

Celiac trunk and SMA - superior and inferior pancreaticoduodenal arteries

SMA and IMA - marginal artery of drummond with branches from the middle and left colic arteries

IMA and Iliac - superior rectal merges with middle rectal 

### Digestive Tract Layers

Mucosa, submucosa, muscular layer, serosa

Submucosa and muscular layer contain nerve plexuses

Mucosa - 3 layers
- Epithelium - absorption 
- Lamina propria - support and gastric glands in the stomach
- Muscularis mucosa - motility

Submucosa
- Connective tissue
- Meissner's plexus
- Hirschprung's Disease

Muscular layer
- Inner circular and outer longitudinal layers of smooth muscle
- Auerbach's plexus
	- Between smooth muscle layers
	- Abnormal in achalasia 
	- Also absent in Hirschprung's disease

Serosa
- Surrounds GI tract
- Layers of surface epithelial cells called mesothelium which secretes a lubricating fluid
- Retroperitoneal structures don't have mesothelium, they have adventitia
	- Adventitia is loose connective tissue that is not lubricated

Plicae Circularis
- Folds are called Valves of Kerckring or Valvulae conniventes
- Folds of mucosa and submucosa
- Most abundant in the jejunum

Goblet cells
- Found in the small and large intestine
- Produce mucinogen which is converted to mucus
- Increase in number from duodenum to the ileum
- Not normally found in the stomach 
	- Occur in setting of chronic inflammation due to gastritis 
	- Called intestinal metaplasia

Brunner's Gland
- Only found in the duodenum
- Found in the submucosa
- Produces alkaline fluid to protect 
- Increased thickness in peptic ulcer disease

Peyer's Patches
- Lymph cells are found in the lamina proprium 
- In ileum, cells aggregate to form Peyer's patches
	- Found in the muscularis mucosa/submucosa

Lacteal
- Lymphatic channels within villi
- Important for absorption of fats

### Colon

Produces a lot of mucus, absorbs fluid and electrolytes. Hallmark features are crypts without villi and lots of goblet cells. 

Haustra 
- Pouches of the colon 

Erosions - mucosa only

Ulcers - submucosa and muscularis mucosa

### Enteric Nervous System

Submucosal plexus (Meissner's)
- Controls secretion and blood flow

Myenteric (Auerbach's)
- Controls GI motility

Slow waves
- Oscillating membrane potential of GI smooth muscle
- Originate in intersitial cells of Cajal (pacemaker cells of the intestines)
- Membrane potential slowly rises near threshold
- When near threshold, action potentials may occur
- Stomach 3/min, Duodenum 12/min, Ileum 8/min
