# Stroke

Regional ischaemia to the brain that results in focal neurologic deficits lasting > 24 hours  

If symptoms last < 24 hours, the event is termed a transient ischemic attack (TIA).  

Subtypes include thrombotic, embolic, and lacunar strokes.

Thrombotic stroke is due to rupture of an atherosclerotic plaque.
- Atherosclerosis usually develops at branch points (e.g., bifurcation of internal carotid and middle cerebral artery in the circle ofWillis).
- Results in a pale infarct at the periphery of the cortex (Fig. 17.4)

Embolic stroke is due to thromboemboli.
- Most common source of emboli is the left side of the heart (e.g., atrial fibrillation).
- Usually involves the middle cerebral artery
- Results in a hemorrhagic infarct at the periphery of the cortex because the thrombus gets lysed and blood goes through again.

Lacunar stroke occurs secondary to hyaline arteriolosclerosis, a complication of hypertension.
- Most commonly involves lenticulostriate vessels, resulting in small cystic areas of infarction (Fig. 17.5)
- Involvement of the internal capsule leads to a pure motor stroke.
- Involvement of the thalamus leads to a pure sensory stroke.

Ischaemic stroke results in liquefactive necrosis.
- Eosinophilic change in the cytoplasm of neurons (red neurons, Fig. 17.6A) is an early microscopic finding (12 hours after infarction).
- Coagulative necrosis (24 hours), infiltration by neutrophils (days 1-3) and microglial cells (days 4-7), and granulation like tissue (weeks 2-3) then ensue.
- Results in formation of a fluid-filled cystic space surrounded by gliosis (Fig. 17.6B) reactive astrocytes that line the cystic space. 
- This is similar to the sequence in MI. 

Haemorrhagic stroke
- Often small arteries
- Hypertension, anticoagulation, CNS malignancy 
- Can also be caused by an ischaemic stroke that is now reperfused
- Occur most commonly in the putamen (also subcortex, [[Cerebellum]], thalamus )
	- Putamen stroke symptoms - contralateral hemiparesis due to the internal capsule next to it
	- Hemisensory loss due to loss of blood flow to the thalamus
	- Gaze deviation toward side of bleed (due to frontal eye field damage)
	- Left paralysis, sensory loss but eyes deviated to the right
