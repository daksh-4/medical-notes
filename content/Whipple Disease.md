# Whipple Disease

Systemic tissue damage characterized by macrophages loaded with Tropheryma whippelii organisms; partially destroyed organisms are present in macrophage lysosomes (positive for PAS). This is because they can only partially kill them so it stays chilling in the macrophage. 

Classic site of involvement is the small bowel lamina propria (Fig. 10.19). Lamina propria is where the enterocytes sit on. 

Micelles are absorbed by the enterocytes, converted to chylomicrons and go into the lacteals, which drains through lymphatics into the blood. 

Macrophages compress lacteals.
Chylomicrons cannot be transferred from enterocytes to lymphatics.
Results in fat malabsorption and steatorrhea
Other common sites of involvement include synovium of joints (arthritis), cardiac valves, lymph nodes, and CNS.
