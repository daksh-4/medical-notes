# Growth Adaptations

- Permanent tissues can undergo hypertrophy only for example cardiac + skeletal muscle and nerves

- Pathological hyperplasia leads to dysplasia which leads to cancer with the exception of benign prostatic hyperplasia

- Decrease in cell size is mediated by ubiquitin-protesosome degradation of the cytoskeleton + autophagy of the cellular components

- There are three main groups of epithelium cells - squamous (keratinised and non-keratinised) which is found in the oesophagus, columnar which is found in the stomach and transitional which is found in the urogenital tract

- Dysplasia and metaplasia are reversible

- Barrett’s is a risk factor for adenocarcinoma

- Vitamin A is needed for maintenance of specialised epithelium such as conjunctiva

- Mesenchymal tissues can undergo metaplasia for example myositis ossificans trauma and inflammation causes muscle to turn into bone.
