# Small Bowel Infarction or Mesenteric Ischaemia

Small bowel is particularly susceptible to ischaemic injury. Needs a lot of ATP for digestion. 

Transmural infarction occurs with thrombosis/embolism (from AF) of the superior mesenteric artery or thrombosis of the mesenteric vein. Could also be from [[Vasculitis]] increasing of thrombosis, e.g. [[Polyarteritis Nodosa]]. Thrombosis of the mesenteric vein can be caused by [[Polycythaemia Vera]] or [[SLE]] from lupus anticoagulant.

Mucosal infarction occurs with marked hypotension. If you decrease the blood flow slightly, then the mucosa will be the first to go. Happens in hypotension because not enough blood flowing. 

Clinical features include abdominal pain, bloody diarrhea, and decreased bowel sounds.
