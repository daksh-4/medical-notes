# Hyperaldosteronism

Excess aldosterone
    
Presents as hypertension with hypernatremia, hypokalemia, and metabolic alkalosis in a young age. Hypokalaemia is an unreliable finding

In the late distal tubule of the collecting duct
- Principal cell will grab sodium and dump potassium 
- alpha intercalated cell will dump hydrogen ions
in response to aldosterone (hypernatraemia, hypokalaemia and metabolic alkalosis)

Aldosterone increases sodium absorption and secretion of potassium and hydrogen ions (distal tubules and collecting duct).
- Increased absorption of sodium expands plasma volume leading to hypertension. 

Primary hyperaldosteronism is most commonly due to an adrenal adenoma; Sporadic adrenal hyperplasia and adrenal carcinoma are less common causes. 
- Plasma aldosterone concentration measurement (but aldosterone can be elevated in a lot of things such as hypertension)
- Plasma renin activity
- Renin/aldosterone ratio
- if aldosterone high and renin low then primary 
	- High blood pressure downregulates renin via negative feedback
- If aldosterone high and renin high then secondary
	- E.g. renal artery stenosis, CHF, low volume
- Abdominal imaging
- Adrenal vein sampling

D. Secondary hyperaldosteronism is seen with activation of the renin-angiotensin system (e.g., renovascular hypertension, CHF, low volume).  Fibromuscular dysplasia decreases blood flow to the kidney in young females. 

I. Characterised by high aldosterone and high renin

Excess aldosterone does lead to salt retention but does not cause fluid overload because the body diureses the extra water. However, the body is not able to lower the blood pressure.
