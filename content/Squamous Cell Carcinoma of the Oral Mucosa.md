# Squamous Cell Carcinoma of the Oral Mucosa

Tobacco and alcohol

Floor of the mouth is the most common location 

Leukoplakia and erythroplakia are precursor lesions (squamous dysplasia)
- Often biopsied to rule out carcinoma 
- You can't scrape these off
- Erythroplakia suggests that there is angiogenesis which is more indicative of dysplasia

Hairy leukoplakia is normally on the side of the tongue (EBV caused) associated with an immunocompromised state. Only represents hyperplasia no dysplasia so not a precursor to squamous cell carcinoma of the oral mucosa
