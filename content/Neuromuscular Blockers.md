Suxamethonium
- Depolarising neuromuscular blocker
- Two ACh molecules joined togehter
- Very strong nicotine receptor agonist
- Sustained depolarisation of the muscle eventually leads to paralysis
- Phase 1
	- Depolarising phase
	- Fasciculations
	- Na channels open and close - becoming inactivated
	- Normally ACh is hydrolysed rapidly by AChE but this doesn't happen to suxamethonium
	- Prolonged activation of ACh receptors
- Phase 2
	- Desensitising 
	- Depolarisation has occured 
	- No more contraction
- No reversal via ACh inhibitor but it has a rapid washout 
- Main side effect is hyperkalaemia
	- Caution in burn patients and dialysis patients or other hyperk
- Malignant hyperthermia [[Malignant Hyperthermia]]

Other non depolarising NM blockers
- Competitive antagonists
- Compete with ACh for nicotinic receptors
- ﻿﻿Produce paralysis
- ﻿﻿Many cause marked histamine release
	- ﻿﻿Hypotension → compensatory tachycardia
- ﻿﻿Can be reversed by flooding synapse with ACh
- ﻿﻿This is done by inhibiting AChE - physostigmine, neostigmine, pyridostigmine, edrophonium

Sometimes given to patients on ventilators
- This is associated with ICU weakness after prolonged treatment

Assessing neuromuscular blockade
- Peripheral nerve stimulation using 4 stimulations to nerve
- Goal is to see them contract at least 1 or 2 times for every 4 electricla stimulation
