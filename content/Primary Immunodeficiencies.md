# Primary Immunodeficiencies

- DiGeorge Syndrome - development failure of the 3rd and 4th pharyngeal pouches. Due to 22q11 microdeletion.
    - This results in T cell deficiency due to lack of thymus, hypocalcemia due to lack of parathyroids and abnormalities of the heart, great vessels and face
- SCID - defective cell-mediated (T cell) and humeral (B cell) immunity
    - Cytokine receptor defects
    - Adenosine deaminase deficiency - build up of adenosine and deoxydenosine in lymphocytes are toxic
    - MHC class 2 deficiency
    - Susceptible to fungal, viral (because of lack of T cells) and bacterial, protozoal (because of lack of B cells). Including opportunistic infections and live vaccines.
- X linked agammagloblinaemia
    - Lack of immunoglobulin due to disordered B cell maturation
    - This results in naiveB cells not being able to mature into plasma cells.
    - Due to Bruton tyrosine kinase mutation (X linked). Signalling molecule that is necessary for B cell to become a plasma cell. Bacterial (because you can’t opsonise with IgG), enterovirus (virus that effects the mucosal surface of the GI tract and IgA protects against mucosal infection) and Giardia infections (same as enterovirus) and live vaccines must be avoided
    - Usually after 6 months of life once mum’s antibodies have run out
- Common variable immunodeficiency CVID
    - Low immunoglobulin due to B cell or helper T cell defects
    - Increased risk for bacterial, enterovirus and Giardia infections usually late in childhood
    - Increased risk for autoimmune disease and lymphoma
- IgA deficiency - low serum and mucosal IgA - most common Ig deficiency
    - Increased risk for mucosal infections especially viral
    - Coeliac patients associated IgA deficiency
- Hyper-IgM syndrome - characterised by elevated IgM
    - Due to mutated CD40 ligands or CD40 receptor. Second single cannot be delivered to helper T cells during B cell activation. The cytokines necessary for Ig class switching not produced. Therefore, low IgA, IgG and IgE result in recurrent pyogenic infections, especially at mucosal sites.
- Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome
    - Thrombocytopenia, eczema, recurrent infections due to defective humeral and cellular immunity.
    - Due to mutation in the Wiskott Aldrich syndrome protein X linked
- Complement deficiency
    - C5-C9 deficiency increased risk of Neisseria infection
    - C1 inhibitor deficiency - hereditary angioedema characterised by oedema of the skin especially periorbital and mucosal surfaces. C1 is an early activator of the complement system. C1 inhibitor controls excessive activation of C1, if it is deficiency, there is over activation of the complement system resulting in vasodilation and the formation of oedema.
