# Autoimmune Haemolytic Anaemia

Antibody mediated IgG or IgM destruction of RBCs

### IgG

Usually involves extravascular haemolysis
IgG binds RBCs in the relatively warm central body
Membrane of antibody coated RBC is consumed by splenic macrophages. Membrane slowly gets less and less.
Results in spherocytes

Associated with SLE, CLL and certain drugs (penicillin)
Drug can induce production of autoantibodies (like methyldopa) or antibodies bind to the drug which binds to RBCs. 

Treatment
- Cessation of the drug
- Steroids
- IVIG (splenic macrophages eat the IVIG instead of the RBCs)
- Splenectomy (removes the source of antibody and destruction)

### IgM

Usually intravascular
Binds RBCs and fixes complement in the relatively cold temperature of the extremities
Associated with Mycoplasma pneumoniae and [[Infectious Mononucleosis]]

Direct Coombs test confirms the presence of antibody-coated RBCs
- Anti-IgG is added to the patient RBCs
- Agglutination occurs if RBCs are already coated with IgG antibody
- Most important test for AIHA

Indirect Coombs test confirms the presence of antibodies in the patient serum
- Anti IgG and test RBCs are mixed with the patient serum 
- Agglutination occurs if serum antibodies are present
