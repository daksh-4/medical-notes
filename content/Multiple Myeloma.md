# Multiple Myeloma

Monoclonal gammopathy of unknown significance (MGUS) is the origin. [[Monoclonal Gammopathy of Undertermined Significance]]

Malignant proliferation of plasma cells in the bone marrow
Most common primary malignancy of the bone (most common secondary is malignant carcinoma)
High serum IL-6 is sometimes present

Bone pain with hypercalcaemia 
- Neoplastic plasma cells produce osteoclast activating factor which activate RANK
- Lytic punched out lesions seen on x-ray especially in the vertebrae and skull
- Increased risk of fracture

Elevated serum protein
- Neoplastic plasma cells produce immunoglobulin
- M spike (stands for monoclonal) is present on serum protein electrophoresis, most commonly due to monoclonal IgG or IgA
- This spike is present in the gamma region

Increased risk for infection
- Monoclonal antibody lacks antigenic diversity
- Infection is the most common cause of death

Rouleaux formation on blood smear
- Increased serum protein decreases charge between RBCs. 
- This causes RBCs to clump up

Primary AL [[Amyloidosis]]
- Free light chain circulates in the serum and deposits in tissues. 

Proteinuria
- Free light chain is excreted in urine as Bence-Jones proteins
- Deposition in kidney tubules leads to risk for renal failure (myeloma kidney)

Spinal cord compression - 80% of patients have bone lesions in the spine
- Lose 10-15 cm of height due to wedge fractures
- Fracture or tumour can cause spinal cord compression
- Bed-rest and back brance to maintain spinal alignment
- High-dose steroids
- Urgent MRI 
- Neurosurgical/clinical oncology (for radiotherapy) referral

Hyperviscosity syndrome - seen more in IgM 
- Reduces delivery of oxygen and cellular nutrients to tissues - end organ dysfunction
- Incidence of 2-6% in myeloma
- Can also occur in acute leukaemia, Waldenstrom's
- Plasmapharesis to treat
