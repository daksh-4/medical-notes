# Gastrooesophageal Reflux Disease

Lower sphincter is not as tight as it should be, which allows for acid and gastric contents to shoot up into the oesophagus, which changes the stress on the cells of the lower oesophagus

They undergo a metaplasia to better handle this stress. They are normally non-keratinised squamous epithelium and become non ciliated columnar cells with goblet cells. 

Risk factors
- Alcohol
- Tobacco
- Obesity
- Fat rich diet
- Caffeine 
- Hiatal hernia
	- Sliding is more common - the stomach herniates upwards through the diaphragm into the oesophagus allowing for reflux 
	- There is a little bit of tone in the lower sphincter which leads to an hourglass appearance due to compression of the stomach
	- You could develop a hernia where the stomach herniates next to the oesophagus (called a para oesophageal hernia) much less common and you would hear bowel sounds in the lower lung fields. If this is congenital then there is less space for the lung to develop and would result in lung hypoplasia

Clinical features
- Heartburn
- Asthma and cough (adult onset)
- Damage to enamel of teeth 
- Ulceration with stricture and Barrett oesophagus are late complications
