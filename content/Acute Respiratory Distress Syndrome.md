# Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome

Damage to the alveolar capillary interface, leading to leaking of protein rich fluid into the airsac. This is reorganised into a hyaline membrane. 

Thickening of the diffusion barrier leads to hypoxaemia and cyanosis

Hyaline membranes are sticky and increase the surface tension of the air sac, leading to collapse of the alveoli. 

![[Pasted image 20240926213709.png|500]]

Clinical features
- Hypoxaemia and cyanosis 
- Respiratory distress\
- ![[Pasted image 20240926213828.png|500]]

Aetiology
- Sepsis, shock, trauma, aspiration, pancreatitis, DIC, hypersensitivity reactions and drugs
- Activation of neutrophils induces protease mediated and free radical damage of type 1 and type 2 pneumocytes (this is common to all causes)

Treatment
- Address the underlying cause
- Ventilation with positive end expiratory pressure (PEEP) to reopen the collapsed alveoli
- Recovery may be complicated by interstitial fibrosis
	- Destruction of type 2 pneumocytes (which are stem cells that regenerate the lung) will lead to fibrosis.
