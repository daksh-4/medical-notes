# Wound Healing

Labile tissues continuously cycle to regenerate tissue
- Small and large bowel via stem cells in mucosal crypts
- Skin via stem cells in the basal layer
- Bone marrow via haematopoietic stem cells (CD34+)
- Lung - type 2 pneumocyte

Stable tissues are quiescent, but can reenter the cell cycle (growth)
- Regeneration of the liver by compensatory hyperplasia after partial resection
- Each hepatocyte produces additional cells and then reenters quiescence

Permanent tissues lack significant regenerative potential so (repair) replaces damaged tissue with a fibrous scar
- Myocardium
- Skeletal muscle
- Neurons
Repair also occurs when regenerative stem cells are lost or when tissue lacks regenerative capacity (like when you get a deep cut). 

Repair process
- Granulation tissue is the initial phase of repair has three components:
	- Fibroblasts (deposit type III collagen)
	- Capillaries (provide nutrients)
	- Myofibroblasts (contract wound)
- Granuloma characteristic feature epitheloid histiocyte and forms when IL12 hits CD4+ T cell converts it into Th1 which secretes interferon gamma which gives the macrophage a epitheloid histiocyte appearance. THIS IS DIFFERENT TO GRANULATION. 

- Granulation tissue eventually results in scar formation. 
	- Type III collagen is replaced with type I collagen. 
	- Collagenase removes type III collagen and requires zinc as a cofactor. 

- Mechanisms of regeneration and repair are mediated by paracrine signaling via growth factors
	- Interaction of factors with receptors results in gene expression and growth
		- TGF alpha - epithelial and fibroblast growth factor
		- TGF beta - important fibroblast growth factor and inhibits inflammation
		- PDGF (platelet derived growth factor released when platelets are activated)- endothelium, smooth muscle, fibroblast growth factor
		- FGF - angiogenesis - skeletal development
		- VEGF - angiogenesis 

- Cutaneous healing occurs via primary or secondary intention
	- Primary - wound edges brought together - minimal scar formation
	- Secondary - edges are not approximated so granulation tissue fills in the defect. Myofibroblasts bring the skin together

- Delayed wound healing
	- Most common cause is infection - continued inflammation impairs healing
	- Vitamin C deficiency - when collagen is produced, it is produced as separate alpha chains which wrap around each other and make procollagen. Procollagen is secreted and cross linked outside of the cell, one of the things that needs to happen before cross linking can occur is hydroxylation of the proline and lysine residues. 
	- Lack of copper because lysyl oxidase (the enzyme that cross linkes lysine and hydroxylysine and requires copper as a co factor)
	- Zinc deficiency for conversion from type III collagen to type I collagen. \
	- Foreign body, ischaemia, diabetes and malnutrition

Complications
- Dehiscence - rupture of wound
- Hypertrophic scar - excess production of scar tissue local to the wound - predominantly made of type I collagen 
- Keloid - excess production of scar tissue way out of proportion to the wound. Characterised by excess type III collagen 

Different types of collagen 

1 - Bone
2 - Cartilage
3 - Stretchy stuff - Blood vessels, granulation tissue, embryonic tissue

Drugs that impair wound healing
- Non steroidal anti inflammatory drugs
- Steroids
- Immunosupressive agents
- Anti neoplastic drugs
