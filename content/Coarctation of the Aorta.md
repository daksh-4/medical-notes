# Coarctation of the Aorta

Narrowing of the aorta divided into infantile and adult forms. 

Infantile form 
- Associated with a [[Patent Ductus Arteriosus]]
- Coarctation lies distal to the aortic arch but proximal to the PDA
- Presents as a lower extremity cyanosis 
- Associated with [[Turner's Syndrome]] 

Adult form
- Not associated with PDA
- Coarctation is distal to the aortic arch
- Extra blood in the aortic arch leads to hypertension in the upper extremities and hypotension in the lower extremity
- Classically discovered in adulthood
- Associated with [[Bicuspid Aortic Valve]]
- Collateral circulation develops across intercostal arteries leading to engorged arteries which present as notching of the ribs on the x ray
