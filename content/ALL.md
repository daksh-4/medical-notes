Why does Down's Syndrome increase your risk for ALL?

ALL lab values - Crowding out of the bone marrow by clonal lymphocyte progenitors. This leads to anaemia and thrombocytopenia as well as low neutrophils. But the WBC can be normal because low neutrophils are replaced by lymphocytes.

Summary - Crowding out of the bone marrow by clonal lymphocyte progenitors. This leads to anaemia and thrombocytopenia as well as low neutrophils. But the WBC can be normal because low neutrophils are replaced by lymphocytes. 

Prognosis
- Poor prognosis in boys because cancer spreading to the testicles is harder to get rid of 
- age < 2 years or > 10 years
- WBC > 20 * 109/l at diagnosis
- T or B cell surface markers
- non-Caucasian

Clinical features
- Bone pain
- Splenomegaly
- Hepatomegaly
- Fever in 50% of new cases
- Testicular swelling

Neoplastic accumulation of lymphoblasts. 

Positive nuclear staining for TdT (DNA polymerase)

TdT is absent in myeloid blasts and mature lymphocytes

Most commonly arises in children. ASSOCIATED WITH DOWN SYNDROME AFTER THE AGE OF 5. ==This is due to the t(12:21) subtype of B ALL==

Can be classified into  B-ALL or T-ALL (whether the blasts make B cells or T cells). 
- This is based on surface markers
- The most common subtype is CD10 present pre-B phenotype

B-ALL
- Most common type
- Lymphoblasts classically express CD10, CD19 and CD20. 
- Excellent response to chemotherapy but requires prophylaxis to the scrotum and CSF because blood doesn't get there. 
- Prognosis based on cytogenetic abnormalities
	- t(12;21) has a good prognosis more commonly seen in kids
	- t(9;22) has a poor prognosis more commonly seen in adults 

T-ALL
- Lymphoblasts that express markers ranging from CD2-CD8
- Blasts do not express CD10
- Presents as a ==mediastinal (thymic) mass in a teenager. ==
- Called ==lymphoblastic lymphoma (this is because the malignant cells are limited to a mass and aren't in the blood)==
