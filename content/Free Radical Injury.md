# Free Radical Injury

- Examples of pathological free radical injury - ionising radiation (OH from hitting water and causes it to split up), inflammation, metals, drugs and chemicals
    
- The hydroxyl free radical is the most damaging
    
- White blood cells kill pathogens with NADPH oxidase which converts oxygen to superoxide. This is converted into hydrogen peroxide which is converted to HOCl
    
- Haemochromatosis causes tissue damage from buildup of iron which generates free radicals. Same with Wilson’s disease.
    
- Paracetamol broken down by the liver forming free radicals, which can damage the liver in high amounts.
    
- How do free radicals damage tissues?
    
    - Peroxidation of lipids
    - Oxidation of DNA (could lead to oncogenic mutations) and proteins
- How does the body get rid of free radicals?
    - Antioxidants including Vitamin A, C, E
    - Enzymes - SOD, glutathione peroxidase, catalase
    - Metal carrier proteins for example transferrin etc. they hide away the metal so that reactions do not take place
- Oxygen —> Superoxide —> Hydrogen Peroxide —> Hydroxide free radical —> Water
    
- Superoxide handled by superoxide dismutase, hydrogen peroxide is handled by catalase, Hydroxide free radical is handled by glutathione peroxidese
    
- CCl4 forms CCl3 free radical in the blood in the p450 system in the liver, CCl4 are seen in dry cleaning. Liver damage mean fat can be repackaged and sent out, this leads to fatty changes in the liver.
    
- Reperfusion injury - oxygen and inflammatory cells return to dead tissue, which can generate free radicals which can further damage cardiac myocytes. If cardiac enzymes continue to rise after Cath lab, it is reperfusion injury
