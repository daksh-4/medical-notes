# Acute Rheumatic Fever

Systemic complication of pharyngitis due to group A beta haemolytic streptococci. 

Affects children 2-3 weeks after strep throat

Caused by molecular mimicry; bacterial M protein resembles human tissues

Diagnosis based on Jones criteria
- Evidence of prior infection (Anti Streptolysin titre or anti DNase B titer)
- Minor criteria - fever and elevated ESR
- Major criteria - all resolve with time without long term sequelae
	- Joint problems - migratory polyarthritis 
	- O (Heart problems) - pancarditis (peri, [[Endocarditis]], myo) 
		- [[Endocarditis]] (innermost) - little vegetations on the tissue attached to the valve leading to mitral valve regurg
		- Myocarditis - Aschoff bodies (underneath) chronic inflammation with giant cells and fibrinoid material. Enitchkow cells (histiocytes with slender wavy nuclei). Most common cause of death in the acute phase

			- ![[Pasted image 20240918184315.png|500]]
		- [[Pericarditis]] - friction rub
	- Nodules 
	- Erythema marginatum 
	- Sydenham's Chorea - rapid involuntary movements of the muscles

Acute attack resolves but may progress to chronic disease 
- Repeat exposure to group A beta haemolytic strep results in relapse of acute phase
- This increases the risk for chronic rheumatic valvular disease
	- This results in stenosis which almost always involves the mitral valve (aortic next which leads to fusion of the commissures that sticks the valves together leading to stenosis)
	- Complication of a damaged valve is [[Endocarditis]]

Management
- Antibiotics
- Anti inflammatories (NSAIDs)
- Treatment of heart failure
