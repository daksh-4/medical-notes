How to distinguish between osteoblastoma?
# Osteoid Osteoma

Benign tumor of osteoblasts (that produce osteoid) surrounded by a rim of reactive bone

Occurs in young adults < 25 years of age (more common in males)

Arises in cortex of long bones (e.g., femur)

Presents as bone pain that resolves with aspirin
    
Imaging reveals a bony mass(< 2 cm) with a radiolucent core (osteoid).
    
Osteoblastoma is similar to osteoid osteoma but is larger(> 2 em), arises in vertebrae, and presents as bone pain that does not respond to aspirin.

Males younger than 25 

Night time bone pain that is relieved by NSAIDs (key distinguisher between osteoblastoma this is the only distinguishing factor)

Bony mass with a radiolucent core
