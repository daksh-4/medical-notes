# Haemochromatosis

A. Excess body iron leading to deposition in tissues (hemosiderosis) and organ damage (hemochromatosis)

Tissue damage is mediated by generation of free radicals.  
B. Due to autosomal recessive defect in iron absorption (primary) or chronic transfusions (secondary)

Enterocyte in the duodenum takes up all the iron present in the gut. But it will hold onto it and will not pass it on unless there is a need to in the blood. 

Primary is due to mutations in the HFE gene most commonly C282Y (cysteine is replaced by tyrosine at amino acid 282).

Presents in late adulthood
- Classic triad is cirrhosis, secondary [[Diabetes Mellitus]], and bronze skin; other findings include cardiac arrhythmias and gonadal dysfunction (due to testicular atrophy).
- Labs show t ferriti up, TIBC down, serum iron up, and  up % saturation.
- Liver biopsy reveals accumulation of brown pigment in hepatocytes (Fig. ll.llA);
- Prussian blue stain distinguishes iron (blue) from lipofuscin (wear and tear pigment that is found naturally) (Fig. ll.llB).

i. Lipofuscin is a brown pigment that is a by-product from the turnover ('wear and tear') of peroxidized lipids; it is commonly present in hepatocytes (Fig. ll.llC).

Increased risk of hepatocellular carcinoma because iron generates free radicals which can damage DNA
   
Treatment is phlebotomy.
