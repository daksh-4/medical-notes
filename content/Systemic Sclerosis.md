# Systemic Sclerosis

- Sclerosis of the skin and visceral organs 
- Middle aged females

### Pathology

- Fibroblasts activation leads to deposition of collagen 
	- Autoimmune damage to the mesenchyme
	- Endothelial dysfunction
	- Perivascular fibrosis
	- Organ damage

- Immune reaction against mesenchymal antigen (in connective tissue in endothelial wall) this results in increased expression of adhesion molecule allowing inflammaotyr cells to come into the connective tissue. 
	- There is increased production of endothelin and reduced production of NO leading to profound vasoconstriction. 
	- Endothelial cells will begin to produce platelet derived growth factor and TGF beta leading to fibrosis around the blood vessel. 
- This leads to ischaemia and additional fibrosis which leads to end organ damage

### Clinical Features

Limited type CREST syndrome
- Calcinosis/anti Centromere antibodies
- Raynaud's phenomenon
- Esophageal dysmotility (of both solids and liquids)- due to fibrosis of the lower portion of the oesophagus. 
- Sclerodactyly 
- Telangiectasias

Diffuse type diffusely involves skin with early visceral involvement
- Vessels - Raynaud
- GI tract - stomach delayed emptying, dysphasia, GORD, malabsorption
- Lung - most common cause of death interstitial fibrosis or pulmonary hypertension
- Kidneys - scleroderma renal crisis - very high blood pressure treat with ACE inhibitor
- Heart - heart failure due to pulmonary hypertension or fibrosis of the heart itself
- Antibodies against DNA topoisomerase I
