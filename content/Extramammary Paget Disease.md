# Extramammary Paget Disease

1. Characterized by malignant epithelial cells in the epidermis of the vulva (Fig. 13.2)
    
2. Presents as erythematous, pruritic, ulcerated vulvar skin
    
3. Represents carcinoma in situ, usually with no underlying carcinoma

1. Paget disease of the nipple is also characterized by malignant epithelial cells in the epidermis of the nipple, but it is almost always associated with an underlying carcinoma. As opposed to of the vulva, there is no underlying cancer. 

D. Must be distinguished from melanoma, which rarely can occur on the vulva

1. Paget cells are PAS+, keratin+, and SlOO-. PAS is a stain for mucus so must be Paget's because epithelial cells make mucus
    
2. Melanoma is PAS-, keratin-, and SlOO+.
