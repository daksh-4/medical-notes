# Different types of RNA Polymerase

![[Pasted image 20260820111358.png|800]]

RNA polymerase I is found strictly only in the nucleolus. The nucleolus is dedicated to making ribosomal RNA.

As cells become more differentiated, their growth slows and they require fewer numbers of ribosome for protein production. In contrast, malignant cells with high metabolic activity usually have a large number of active rRNA genes and prominent nucleoli.

## RNA Polymerase I, II, and III

| Polymerase | Location | Product | Alpha-amanitin sensitivity |
|---|---|---|---|
| RNA Pol I | Nucleolus | rRNA (the major species: 28S, 18S, 5.8S) | Insensitive |
| RNA Pol II | Nucleoplasm | mRNA (pre-mRNA/hnRNA), most snRNAs | Highly sensitive (low doses inhibit it) |
| RNA Pol III | Nucleoplasm | tRNA, 5S rRNA, other small RNAs | Sensitive only at higher doses (intermediate) |

Alpha-amanitin (the *Amanita phalloides* death cap toxin) is a useful way to remember the three: Pol II is knocked out first because mRNA production is the most immediately consequential to inhibit, Pol III needs a higher dose, and Pol I isn't affected at all — which fits with rRNA/ribosome production being more of a slower, background process rather than something the cell needs to shut down instantly to die from the toxin.

Unlike DNA polymerase (see [[DNA Polymerases]]), RNA polymerase does not require a primer — it can initiate transcription de novo at a promoter sequence.
