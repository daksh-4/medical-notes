# Congestive Heart Failure

Causes of left-sided heart failure
- Ischaemia
- Hypertension - (hypertrophic cardiomyopathy means there is not enough blood supply do supply the thick muscle wall)
- Dilated cardiomyopathy
- Myocardial infarction
- [[Restrictive Cardiomyopathy]]

Pulmonary congestion
- Pulmonary oedema with dyspnoea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea, orthopnea and crackles (oodema in the interstitial fluid)
- Heart-failure cells - rupture of engorged capillaries causing blood to leak into the alveoli. The macrophages will consume it and become full of iron - haemosiderin laden macrophages
- ![[Pasted image 20240918173150.png]]

Decreased forward perfusion
- Activation of the renin-angiotensin system - resulting in increased total peripheral resistance and increased blood volume which results in a downwards spiral. 
- Mainstay of treatment is ACE inhibitor

Right-sided failure is most commonly due to left failure
- Back up of blood all the way through the pulmonary circulation to the right heart resulting in heart failure
- Other important causes include left to right shunt and chronic lung disease (cor pulmonale) - blood vessels in the lung constrict due to hypoxia and the right heart has to pump against increased pressure 

Clinical features of right side failure
- jugular vein distension
- painful hepatosplenomegaly may lead to cardiac cirrhosis
- dependent pitting oedema
