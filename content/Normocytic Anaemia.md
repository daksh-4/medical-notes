# Normocytic Anaemia

Is the anaemia due to increased peripheral destruction or underproduction?

Reticulocyte count helps distinguish between these two. 

Reticulocytes are young RBCs that are released from the bone marrow. Identified on blood smear as larger cells with bluish cytoplasm (residual RNA in the cytoplasm)

![[Pasted image 20240910150025.png]]

Normal RC is 1-2% 
RBC lifespan is 120 days 

In properly functioning bone marrow, it will respond to anaemia by increasing RC to >3%. 
However, RC is falsely elevated in anaemia because it is calculated as a percentage out of all RBCs present. 
Corrected by multiplying reticulocyte count by Haematocrit/45

Peripheral RBC destruction (hameolysis)
- Divided into extravascular and intravascular haemolysis
- Both result in anaemia with good marrow response

### Extravascular haemolysis 

In diseases with extravascular haemolysis there is also some degree of intravacular haemolysis too 

Extravascular haemolysis involves RBC destruction by the reticuloendothelial system including macrophages of the spleen, liver and lymph. 
		- Globin --> amino acids
		- Haeme --> Iron and protoporphyrin 
		- Protoporphyrin --> Unconjugated bilirubin (which is bound to serum albumin delivered to the liver for conjugation and excretion into the bile)

- Anaemia with splenomegaly (example of hypertrophy)
- Jaundice due to unconjugated bilirubin 
	- Unconjugated bilirubin will go the liver to get conjugated. But the liver can only conjugate a set amount of molecules every second. 
	- Once conjugated, there will be more bile so increased risk for bilirubin [[Gallstones]]
- Marrow hyperplasia with corrected reticulocyte count >3%

### Intravascular haemolysis 

- Destruction of the RBC within vessels when Hb is released into blood. 
- When Hb is released into the blood it immediately binds with haptoglobin and takes it to the spleen for reprocessing. 
- Haemoglobinemia 
- Haemoglobinuria
- Haemosiderinuria 
	- Hb taken up by the renal tubular cells and piles up as haemosiderin.
	- Once the renal tubular cells die and slough off, the haemosiderin will be released into the urine. 
	- Takes a bit longer to happen 
- Decreased serum haptoglobin because it is used up binding to Hb
