# Alcohol Related Liver Disease

A. Damage to hepatic parenchyma due to consumption of alcohol
 Most common cause of liver disease in the West  
 
B. Fatty liver is the accumulation of fat in hepatocytes (Fig. 11.9A).
Results in a heavy, greasy liver; resolves with abstinence (Fig. 11.9B)  

C. Alcoholic hepatitis results from chemical injury to hepatocytes; generally seen with binge drinking
- Acetaldehyde (metabolite of alcohol) mediates damage.
- Characterized by swelling of hepatocytes with formation of Mallory bodies (damaged cytokeratin filaments, Fig. 11.10), necrosis, and acute inflammation
- ![[Pasted image 20241005153025.png|500]]
- Presents with painful hepatomegaly and elevated liver enzymes (AST > ALT); may result in death

D. Cirrhosis is a complication of long-term, chronic alcohol-induced liver damage;

occurs in 10-20% of alcoholics
