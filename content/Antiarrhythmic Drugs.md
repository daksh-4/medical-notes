# Antiarrhythmic Drugs

![[Pasted image 20241102183557.png|500]]

Class 1 block sodium channels 

Class 2 are beta blockers 

Class 3 block potassium channels

Class 4 block calcium channels

![[Pasted image 20241102183916.png|500]]

Class 1 and 3 exert effects on cardiac myocytes. 2 and 4 exert effects on sinoatrial node in pacemaker cells.  

![[Pasted image 20241102184028.png|500]]

Phase 0 is the depolarisation section ie the QRS. So if you block this you can get broadening of the QRS complex. 

QT interval is the start of Phase 0 to the end of Phase 3. Drugs that block potassium lengthen the QT interval. 

![[Pasted image 20241102184217.png|500]]

### Class 1 drugs
- Block sodium channels and prolong QRS
- Some also affect potassium channels
- Can prolong action potential duration 
- Can prolong effective refractory period
- 1c are pure sodium channel blockers
	- Only affect QRS
- 1a have some potassium channel blocking too 
	- Affect QRS and QT
- 1b slightly shrink the action potential 
- Use dependent drugs - more binding in fast heart rates

Class 1a Drugs - Quinidine and procainamide
- Prolong QRS 
- Also some potassium channel blocking 
- Quinidine
	- Oral drug
	- Can decrease recurrence rate of a fib
	- Associated with increased mortality 
- Procainamide
	- IV drug 
	- Slows conduction in accessory pathways such as WPW
	- Drug induced lupus - rash, arthritis, anaemia

Class 1b Drugs - Lidocaine and Mexiletine 
- Little effect on QRS on normal HR 
- Slightly decreas Qt interval 
- Only effective at high heart rates 
- Bind Na channels in the depolarised state
	- In ischaemia more myocytes are depolarised 
	- So most effective in ischaemic arrhythmias
- Rapidly unbind when they exit depolarised state
	- So only effective in fast heart rates because there is less time for the drug to unbind before the Na channels open again
	- Ischaemic VT
- In re-entry there are multiple pathways with varied conduction abilities. This means activity in one pathway can travel to other areas to create a circuit. Lidocaine causes a bidirectional block which stops the circuit
- Lidocaine
	- Local anaesthetic
	- May cause CNS stimulation 
		- Tremor agitation 
	- Cardiovascular side effects
		- Excessive block - bradycardia, heart block and hypotension

Class 1c Drugs - Flecainide, Propafenone
- Block open Na channels 
- Very slow undbinding so markedly prolong QRS 
- Limited use due to concern of toxicity and pro-arrhythmic effects
- ONLY USE IN STRUCTURALLY NORMAL HEARTS
- Must monitor for QRS prolongation especially at high heart rates where more binding happens

### Class 3 drugs

Feared complication is Torsade de Pointes due to QT prolongation
Class 1A and III drugs prolong QT

Amiodarone
- K channel blocker 
- Lowest incidence of Torsade of all class 3s 
- ALSO HAS CLASS 1, 2 and 4 effects
	- Prolongs QRS
	- Slow heart rate, delays AV conduction
- Good for AF and V tach 
- Highly lipid soluble so accumulates with a half life of 58 days
- Very long washout 
- Biliary excretion (due to lipid solubility) so safe in renal disease
- Side effects are due to accumulation
	- Hyper and [[Hypothyroidism]]
	- Increased LFTs
	- Skin sensitivity to sun
	- Pulmonary fibrosis
		- Most common cause of death from amiodarone
		- Foamy macrophages filled with amiodarone and phospholipids
		- Honey combing
	- Blue grey discolouration most prominent on the face
	- Corneal deposits
		- Secretion by lacrimal glands 
		- Accumulation on the corneal surface (cat whiskers)

Sotalol and Dofetilide
- Can prolong QT with significant risk for Torsade
- Sotalol also has beta blocking properties 
- Can be used in patients with cardiomyopathy
- Reverse use dependence
	- Class III drugs bind best in the resting state 
	- Exert greatest effects during bradycardia when the channel is not in use

Ibutilide
- IV drug with short half life of 2 to 12 hours
- Used for chemical cardioversion
- May cause Torsade

### Class 2 drugs

Beta blockers have main effect on pacemaker cells and decrease the slope of phase 4 and prolong repolarisation (phase 3)

![[Pasted image 20241102192434.png|500]]

### Class 4 drugs

Slow heart rate and slow AV node conduction. Calcium channels are needed for phase 0 of the pacemaker cycle. 

Results in decreased slope of phase 0

Both class 3 and class 4 drugs can result in AV blocks - type 1 and type 2 mobitz 1

### Others

[[Adenosine]]
- Nucleoside base
- Used to make ATP
- Receptors in many location (purinergic receptors)
	- AV nodal tissue 
	- Vascular smooth muscle
- In AV nodal tissue
	- Activates K+ channels
	- Drives K+ out 
	- Takes longer to depolarise 
	- Also blocks Ca influx 
	- This slows conduction through the AV node
- Short half life
- Used in SVT (called super because it originates above the HIS bundle so AV nodal block is needed)
- Most common SVT is AV node recurrent tachycardia (AVNRT)
	- [[Adenosine]] slows conduction and breaks the conduction
- Effects blocked by theophylline and caffeine 
	- Block [[Adenosine]] receptors
- Side effects due to [[Adenosine]] receptors outside of the AV nodes
	- Vasodilator - skin flushing and hypotension
	- Dyspnoea and chest pain but this resolves quickly

Magnesium
- Acute management of Torsade 
- Blocks influx of Ca into cells 
- Influx leads to early after depolarisations (depolarisations that trigger during phase 3)

Atropine 
- Muscarinic receptor antagonist
- Parasympathetic block - speeds up conduction in AV node
- Used in bradycardia especially brady caused by AV block 
- Side effects due to muscarinic block 
	- Dry mouth 
	- Constipation 
	- Urinary retention 
	- Confusion 

Digoxin
- Increases contractility 
- Slows AV node conduction 
- ![[Pasted image 20241102211738.png|500]]
- More Ca leads to increased contractility
- Increases vagal tone as a separate effect
	- Can be used to decrease heart rate in rapid AF
- Renal clearance - toxic in CKD
- Hypokalaemia promotes toxicity 
	- K+ and digoxin compete for the same binding site
	- Precipitated by loop diuretics which can cause hypok
- Toxicity
	- Nausea vomiting
	- Lethargy, confusion
	- Visual changes
	- Cardiac arrhythmias
		- More sodium in cells raises the resting potential of cells
		- This means that they can spontaneously depolarise by themselves (automaticity)
		- Extra beats 
- Digibind to treat
