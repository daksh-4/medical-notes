### PR Interval 

Normal is 120-200ms so if it is longer than 1 big box then it is PR prolongation

Prolonged PR in first degree AV block 
Short PR is WPW 

### QRS Interval

Normal is <120ms so shorter than 3 small boxes. 

Prolonged - bundle branch blocks

### QT Interval

QT interval is affected by heart rate

T wave should end before you are halfway between 2 QRS complexes

Short QT - hypercalcaemia 

Prolonged QT - hypocalcaemia, drugs, long QT syndrome

Congenital long QT syndrome is due to abnormal K/Na channels. 
- Family history of sudden death due to torsades
- Young patient with recurrent "seizures" with ECG showing long QT
- Jervell and Lange-Nielsen Syndrome
	- Norway and Sweden
	- Long QT with association to deafness

Acquired Long QT
- Antiarrhythmic drugs
- Levofloxacin
- Haloperidol

### Interpreting ECGs

 Are there p waves present?
- P waves are upright in leads II, III and aVF

Is the rhythm regular or irregular?
- R-R intervals constant or not?

﻿﻿P waves present, irregular rhythm
- ﻿﻿Sinus rhythm with PACs
- ﻿﻿Multifocal atrial tachycardia
- ﻿﻿Sinus with AV block

No p waves, regular rhythm
- ﻿﻿Hidden p waves: retrograde
- ﻿﻿Supraventricular tachycardias (SVTs)
- ﻿﻿Ventricular tachycardia

Is the QRS wide or narrow?
- Narrow - HIS-Purkinje works and no bundle branch blocks are present
- Wide - bundle branch block (especially in stable patients), ventricular rhythm

Check the intervals: PR and QT?
- PR - lengthens with vagal tone drugs, AV block, shortens with sympathetic tone
- QT - prolonged by hypoca, antiarrhythmics, shortened with hyperca

ST segments?
- T wave abnormalities 
	- Inverted in ischaemia
	- Peaked in early ischaemia, hyperkalaemia
	- Flat/U waves in hypok
- ST depression
	- Subendocardial ischaemia
- ST elevation
	- Transmural ischamia
