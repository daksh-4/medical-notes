[[Nephrology]]

Tf/P = tubular fluid has the same concentration as plasma.  
  
Tf/P > 1 = tubular fluid is more concentrated than plasma.

In the glomerulus filtration simply filters the plasma and everything stays isotonic. 

In the PCT about 65% of sodium is reabsorbed and about 65% of water follows immediately. Glucose, amino acids, and bicarb are also reabsorbed and water follows the solute because this segment is highly permeable to water. Therefore you have solute going out and water going out by the same proportion so the concentration hardly changes. 

In the thin descending limb, this segment is extremely permeable to water but almost impermeable to NaCl. The surrounding medulla is becoming progressively more concentrated as part of the [[Countercurrent Exchange]]. The remaining tubular fluid becomes increasingly concentrated so Tf/P is greater than one. 

In the thin ascending limb, the opposite occurs. This limb is impermeable to water and allows NaCl to leave so water cannot follow. NaCl decreases and water stays. TF/P is decreasing in this segment. 

In the thick ascending limb, this is the classic diluting segment where the NK CC-2 transporter is pumping out NaCl but it is completely impermeable to water. So if you pump out salt but don't change water, then the tubular fluid concentration drops and TF/P is less than one. 

In the early distal convoluted tubule a similar thing happens as in the thick ascending limb. 

In the late distal tubule and collecting ducts it is all dependent on ADH. Without ADH water cannot leave, sodium continues to be reabsorbed, and tubular fluid becomes even more dilute. With ADH water leaves and is removed faster than solute.
