How does cancer invade and spread?
# Tumour Progression

Accumulation of mutations results in invasion and spread. 

There are several ways which cancer achieves this:
- Down regulation of e cadherin
	- Cadherins keep cells attached to each other. 
	- To allow tumour to spread, it needs to dissociate from its neighbours. 
	- The cell will instead attach to the laminin in the basement membrane (which is comprised of collage IV and laminin). 
	- Then tumour cells make colleganse to break the basement membrane and exit into the extracellular matrix and attach itself to fibronectin to spread locally or through blood or lymph nodes. 

- Lymphatic spread is characteristic of carcinomas with initial spread to the regional draining lymph nodes. 

- Haematogenous spread is characteristic of sarcomas (cancers of mesenchymal tissue) and some carcinomas (listed below)
	- For example renal cell carcinoma (renal vein)
	- Hepatocellular carcinoma (hepatic vein)
	- Follicular carcinoma of the thyroid
	- Choriocarcinoma - invades into the placenta

- Seeding of body cavities is characteristic of ovarian carcinoma
	- "caking of the omentum"
