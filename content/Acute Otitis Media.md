# Acute Otitis Media

Most commonly strep pneumoniae, h flue, moraxella catarrhalis 

Viral URTIs are thought to disturb the normal microbiome, allowing bacteria to infect the middle ear via the Eustachian tube

Features
- otalgia
- fever in 50% 
- hearing loss
- recent viral URTI
- ear discharge if tympanic membrane perforates

Otoscopy findings
- bulging tympanic membrane
- opacification or erythema of the tympanic membrane
- perforation with purulent otorrhea 
- decreased mobility if using a pneumatic otoscope

Usually self limiting and does not require management. Give analgesia. 

Prescribe antibiotics if 
- Symptoms lasting more than 4 days or not improving
- Systemically unwell but not requiring admission
- Immunoompromise or high risk of complications
- Younger than 2 years with bilateral otitis media 
- Perforation and/or discharge in the canal

Sequelae 
- Perforation of the tympanic membrane leading to otorhea 
	- unresolved may develop into chronic suppurative otitis media
